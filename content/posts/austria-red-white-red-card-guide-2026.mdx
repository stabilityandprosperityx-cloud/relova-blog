Austria’s **Red-White-Red Card (RWR)** is the main skilled-worker residence route for non-EU professionals. It is quieter than Germany’s Opportunity Card or the classic EU Blue Card conversation — which often means less applicant noise for the same class of roles — while Vienna continues to rank near the top of the Economist Intelligence Unit’s Global Liveability Index.

## Which RWR track matches your profile

The system is points-based and split into lanes rather than one catch-all visa.

**Very Highly Qualified Workers** need **70 of 100 points** (qualification, experience, age, language). With that score you can apply for a six-month Job Seeker visa **without** a signed offer, then convert once an Austrian employer hires you.

**Skilled Workers in Shortage Occupations** need **55 of 90 points**. There is **no labour-market test** because Austria has already flagged the occupation as scarce. In **2026** the shortage list covers **64 nationwide** and **66 regional** roles — dominated by engineering, IT, healthcare, construction, and skilled trades.

**Other Key Workers** and related employer-tied lanes still use points plus a firm job offer. The salary floor for this “other key workers” band rose on **1 January 2026** (details below). Highly paid hires can also sit in the **EU Blue Card** lane that Austria runs inside the same skilled-migration stack.

## 2026 salary floors you must put in the contract

From **1 January 2026**, minimums moved up again:

| Route / label | 2026 threshold | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Other Key Workers | **€3,465 gross/month** | Was €3,225 in 2025 (**+7.4%**) |
| EU Blue Card (via RWR system) | **€55,678 gross/year** | High-skill alternative |
| “Super Key Employee” / landing-formalities relief | **€8,316/month** | Top-pay track |

Austrian pay is usually calculated on **14 monthly payments** (extra “13th” and “14th” salaries typically paid in June and December). Write that structure into the contract. The Public Employment Service (**AMS**) and the Economic Chamber (**WKO**) will reject salary math that quietly assumes twelve equal months when the offer is really fourteen.

Separately, net **subsistence** checks still use **€1,308.39/month** for a single adult plus **€201.88** per child. Gross offer and net living minimum are different tests — pass both.

## Points, German, and how long the card lasts

German is not always mandatory on day one, but **ÖSD** or **Goethe-Zertifikat** from **B1** upward scores real points and speeds integration. Age and relevant experience fill the rest of the grid; treat the points table as a spreadsheet before you book embassy slots.

The first RWR Card is usually issued for **24 months** and is **tied to that employer**. After roughly **21 months** of qualifying work you can move to the **RWR Card Plus**, which unlocks open labour-market access. Permanent residence is typically after **five years** of lawful stay on the right sequence of titles.

## Processing, family, and a practical filing order

Most files clear in **6–12 weeks**. In 2026 more of the pipeline sits on a digital platform; clean digital packets often land closer to **~8 weeks**, while incomplete translations or salary letters that ignore the 14-pay custom still stall.

Spouses and minor children can usually join from the start. After a short transition, a spouse can work without a separate skilled-worker card of their own — confirm the exact dependent title on your approval letter.

Practical sequence that works: (1) score yourself against the right lane, (2) lock an offer whose **gross** meets the 2026 floor **and** whose payroll calendar shows 14 payments if that is how the company pays, (3) gather degree recognition / translations, (4) file, (5) plan the RWR Plus switch around month 21 so you are not stuck employer-tied longer than necessary.

Austria rewards preparation more than luck. If your occupation is on the 2026 shortage list — or your points clear 70 without an offer — the RWR Card is often a cleaner path than hunting for a Blue Card narrative that does not fit your salary band.


If you are comparing Austria with Germany, remember the branding difference: Germany’s Opportunity Card and Blue Card dominate English-language forums, while Austria’s RWR still feels niche. That niche status does not mean soft standards — AMS and WKO will still reject fuzzy salary letters — but it can mean fewer applicants chasing the same shortage-list roles in regional Länder.

Regional shortage lists matter more than people expect. A role that is not scarce nationwide may still be scarce in a specific state, which can unlock the **55/90** skilled-worker lane without a labour-market test. Ask the employer (or counsel) which list applies before you invest in translations.

For families, budget beyond the subsistence line of **€1,308.39** plus **€201.88** per child: school deposits, German courses for the spouse, and the cash float until the first Austrian payroll lands (especially if the company pays monthly with 13th/14th later). The Card Plus transition around month **21** is the moment many households renegotiate housing or switch employers — put it on the calendar when the first card is stamped.
