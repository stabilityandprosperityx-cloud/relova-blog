**Bali vs Thailand expats 2026** usually comes down to Thailand winning on price and infrastructure, and Bali winning on atmosphere — provided you can stomach how much Canggu's rents have climbed since 2022. The two destinations attract overlapping crowds of remote workers, but they've diverged noticeably on cost, connectivity, and visa clarity over the past few years, and it's worth being honest about which of those you actually prioritize before booking a one-way ticket.

## Cost of living and connectivity

Thailand remains the cheaper default for most remote workers. Chiang Mai runs a comfortable solo budget of roughly $1,000–1,500 a month, Bangkok $1,500–2,000, and both include considerably more built-out infrastructure — reliable fiber internet, an established coworking scene, extensive public transit in Bangkok — for the money. Bali, and specifically Canggu, has priced up sharply: a comfortable budget there now runs $1,400–2,200 a month, reflecting roughly 30–50% cost inflation in the tourist-nomad north since 2020–2022 as the area's popularity outpaced its housing supply. Ubud remains the exception, with budgets starting nearer $1,200 for a genuinely quieter, less inflated pace of life.

Internet reliability is the second-order decider, and it matters more than people expect until they're mid-video-call when the power flickers. Thai fiber in the cities commonly delivers 100–300 Mbps with real stability; Bali averages closer to 30–80 Mbps and is meaningfully less consistent, particularly during rainy season when outages are more common. English works reasonably well in both destinations' tourist and nomad zones, but drops off quickly outside them in ways that matter for anything beyond ordering food or booking a scooter.

## Visas: Thailand's DTV versus Bali's shifting options

Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) is the clearer product by a wide margin. It offers up to five years of validity with 180 days permitted per entry, extendable once to 360 days total, explicitly allows remote work (a genuine departure from Thailand's historically murky stance), costs around $280 (฿10,000) to apply for, and requires roughly ฿500,000 (about $15,000) sitting in a bank account for at least three months before filing. High earners can instead pursue the Thailand LTR (Long-Term Resident) visa, which targets applicants from about $80,000 a year in income and offers a preferential 17% flat tax rate — a genuinely attractive deal for anyone who qualifies.

Bali's options remain comparatively unsettled. The long-standing workaround has been the B211A social/business visa (60 days, extendable toward roughly six months), which technically doesn't authorize remote work but has been used that way for years in a legal grey zone immigration hasn't aggressively enforced. Indonesia's dedicated E33G Remote Worker visa, introduced in 2024, requires close to $60,000 a year in income, runs for one year initially, and can typically be extended once for a two-year total before you must exit and reapply — details on renewal mechanics differ across sources, so confirm directly with Indonesian immigration before relying on any secondary guide, including this one. For wealthier movers, Indonesia's Second Home visa offers 5–10 years of residency but requires roughly $130,000–133,000 parked on deposit — a wealth-gated option, not a working nomad's visa.

## Tax residency and safety

Thailand's tax system carries a territorial flavor: foreign-sourced income is only taxed if it's remitted into Thailand within the same calendar year it was earned, which gives careful planners real flexibility, taxed progressively from 0% up to 35% once it counts as Thai income. Indonesia is less forgiving — cross 183 days of physical presence and you become a tax resident on your worldwide income, taxed progressively from roughly 5% up to 35%, with no equivalent remittance-timing workaround. On safety, general indexes tend to score Thailand somewhat ahead of Indonesia (commonly cited around 7.2 versus 6.8 out of 10), though these composite scores are directional at best and shouldn't substitute for normal city-specific awareness in either country.
