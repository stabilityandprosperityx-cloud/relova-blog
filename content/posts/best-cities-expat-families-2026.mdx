**Best cities expat families 2026** rankings should weigh school quality, safety, healthcare access, and day-to-day parental sanity — not just headline tax rates. The right city for a family looks different from the right city for a solo remote worker, because school fees and family-sized housing usually dwarf every other line item in the budget.

## The premium tier: Singapore, Vienna, and Amsterdam

Singapore consistently tops safety rankings and offers some of the strongest STEM-focused international schools in the world, but that quality comes at real cost: international school tuition commonly runs $20,000–40,000 a year per child, on top of a cost of living that's among the highest in Asia. Families who choose Singapore are usually doing so for a specific employer package that covers school fees, since the sticker price is difficult to absorb on a typical single income.

Vienna takes a different approach entirely and repeatedly tops global liveability indexes for good reason: its public school system (including bilingual and international tracks) is strong enough that many expat families skip private international schools altogether, and the city's excellent public healthcare and later near-free university access make it a genuinely long-term-friendly base rather than just a comfortable posting. Amsterdam offers something similar in spirit — a public education system open to progressive, English-track options and a city built around cycling infrastructure that lets even young kids get around independently, plus a resident population drawing from 240-plus nationalities that makes "the new kid" a much smaller social hurdle than in more homogeneous cities.

## The value tier: [Lisbon](/blog/move-to-lisbon-family-guide-2026) and Kuala Lumpur

Lisbon pairs a genuinely family-friendly climate and safety profile with international school fees in the €8,000–18,000 range — meaningfully below Singapore or Dubai, while still offering an established expat family community and easy access to beaches and outdoor life. Kuala Lumpur undercuts nearly everyone on tuition, with international schools commonly running MYR 25,000–60,000 a year (roughly $5,500–13,000), making it one of the most affordable ways to access a genuinely international curriculum, though families should weigh that against a hotter climate and less mature healthcare infrastructure than Singapore's.

## Dubai and the rest of the shortlist

Dubai offers tax-free income and widely available British-curriculum schools, but school costs land in a wide AED 30,000–80,000 band depending on curriculum and reputation, and the overall cost of living (particularly housing) can offset the tax advantage quickly for a family rather than a single earner. Monthly family budgets vary enormously by city — a comfortable Singapore family budget commonly runs $8,000–12,000, while a Lisbon equivalent sits closer to €3,500–5,000 — a gap significant enough to change which country makes sense for a given income. Auckland, Prague, Porto, and Berlin round out a solid second-tier shortlist for families who want a strong balance of safety, outdoor access, and cost without the premium-city price tag; Porto in particular is drawing growing numbers of families priced out of Lisbon while still wanting Portugal's climate and safety.

## What actually matters beyond the ranking

School fees are the number everyone fixates on, but they're rarely the number that determines whether a family move works long-term. Application timing matters as much as budget — the best international schools in Singapore, Amsterdam, and Vienna often have multi-year waitlists for popular grade levels, so families relocating mid-school-year frequently end up choosing from whatever has space rather than their first choice. Healthcare access matters just as much: Vienna and Amsterdam both offer strong public systems that extend to legal residents relatively quickly, while Dubai and Singapore rely more heavily on private insurance, which needs to be arranged before you land rather than after a first pediatrician visit reveals a gap in coverage. And language immersion cuts both ways — a public-track school forces faster integration and fluency but asks more of a child in year one, while an international school offers continuity at a real financial cost. None of these trade-offs show up in a simple cost table, which is exactly why they deserve more attention than the headline tuition figure gets.
