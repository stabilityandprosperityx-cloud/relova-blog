InterNations **Expat Insider 2026** surveyed roughly **7,800–8,000** expats from **162** nationalities across **31** destinations (fieldwork **1 February–31 March 2026**). It is a satisfaction and settling survey — not a visa-difficulty index — which is exactly why it is useful as a lived-experience ranking and dangerous if you treat it as a sole relocation algorithm.

## Top 10 destinations (Expat Insider 2026)

| Rank | Country | Standout strengths | Who it often fits |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Panama** | 3rd consecutive #1; #1 Working Abroad & Personal Finance; #2 Ease of Settling In & Expat Essentials; **90%** say disposable income covers a comfortable life; **87%** satisfied abroad (vs **70%** global avg) | Remote / finance-focused movers wanting ease |
| 2 | **Mexico** | #1 Ease of Settling In; local friendliness | People prioritising social warmth and access |
| 3 | **Thailand** | Lifestyle + cost balance in the survey mix | Nomads and retirees testing Asia hubs |

| 7 | **Singapore** | Efficiency and expat infrastructure | Career movers who accept high costs |

| 10 | **Luxembourg** | Order, services, finance hub | EU professionals chasing stability |

Panama’s three-year streak is the headline: money stretch and work-abroad scores, not Instagram beaches alone.

## Where the ranking hurts: the bottom of the table

**Norway** finished **31st of 31**. Only **46%** of expats there report life satisfaction (vs **70%** globally); **72%** say making local friends is hard (vs **40%** average); just **25%** are happy with their social life (vs **54%**). **Germany** landed around **30th**, dragged especially by **Ease of Settling In**. Also in the lower band: **Turkey**, the **United Kingdom**, and **Canada** (**27th**). High public-service quality does not automatically equal easy social entry — the survey keeps proving that point.

## Quality of Life Index note

On InterNations’ separate **Quality of Life** lens, **Spain** leads. **UAE** and **Qatar** score strongly on comfort and safety; **Austria** and **Luxembourg** on order and public services. **China** sits surprisingly around **6th**, powered by transport (metro depth in Shanghai, Shenzhen, Beijing, Guangzhou and high-speed rail).

## Methodology caveat (who is missing)

Popular expat magnets such as **Georgia** do not appear when a country lacks enough survey respondents for a stable ranking — even if visa-free stays, low taxes, and moderate living costs attract remote workers in practice. Absence from the Top 31 is not a verdict; it is a sample-size limit. Use Expat Insider for soft factors (friends, finance feel, settling), then overlay hard constraints: visa eligibility, tax residence, school seats, and healthcare enrolment.

Read the ranking as a conversation starter among people already abroad — then stress-test your passport and income against the destinations that scored well for *your* priority column, not someone else’s.


How to use the ranking without cargo-culting Panama: match the **column** you care about. If disposable income comfort is your bottleneck, Panama’s Personal Finance leadership and the **90%** comfort statistic are relevant. If climate and daily lifestyle dominate, Spain’s Quality of Life lead may outweigh a lower overall rank. If you need soft landing and local friendliness first, Mexico’s Ease of Settling In #1 is the signal.

Career movers should still overlay visa sponsorship reality — survey happiness among people who already hold status says nothing about whether *your* passport can enter that status. Combine Expat Insider soft scores with hard immigration filters, then shortlist three countries and visit before you resign.


Remember the fieldwork window (**Feb–Mar 2026**) when you read year-labelled blogs. Macro shocks after spring 2026 — housing rules, tax reforms, visa fee changes — will not appear in this wave. Re-check immigration and tax pages for your shortlist the week you apply, even if the soft ranking still looks right.


Document decisions as you go: which visa category you chose and why, which temporary address you booked, which school waitlists you joined. Future-you will need that trail when a consulate, landlord, or insurer asks for a timeline. Relocation amnesia is real after the adrenaline fades — write the rationale while it is fresh, in the same folder as your scans.
