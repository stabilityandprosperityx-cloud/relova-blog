"Best island for expats" depends entirely on what you're optimizing for — EU access, time zone, income requirement, or straightforward cost — so here's how four genuinely different islands stack up against each other rather than a generic ranking.

## [Madeira](/blog/madeira-vs-azores-expat-guide-2026): EU access without applying for a separate island visa

Madeira is an autonomous region of Portugal, which means there's no distinct "Madeira visa" — you use Portugal's national routes, most commonly the D7 visa (aimed at people with passive or remote income) or Portugal's [digital nomad visa](/blog/moving-to-bali-guide-2026) for employees and freelancers meeting Portugal's income thresholds (broadly benchmarked to multiples of the Portuguese minimum wage, so the exact euro figure shifts as that wage is updated). What Madeira adds on top of mainland Portugal is a genuinely different lifestyle: a subtropical, mild year-round climate, dramatic coastal scenery, and — notably for remote workers — the Digital Nomad Village initiative in Ponta do Sol, one of the first government-backed coworking and community hubs of its kind in Europe. Cost of living sits in the moderate range for Portugal: cheaper than Lisbon, comparable to or slightly above smaller mainland cities.

## Malta: English-speaking EU membership at a price

Malta offers full EU membership with English as an official language — a rare and genuinely useful combination for anglophone remote workers who want EU access without a new-language learning curve. Its dedicated Nomad Residence Permit targets non-EU remote workers and freelancers, with income requirements commonly cited around EUR 2,700 net per month (confirm the current figure directly with Residency Malta, since thresholds are periodically revised), plus proof of accommodation and health insurance. Malta is also the most expensive of the four islands here for day-to-day living and rent, a trade-off many applicants accept in exchange for the EU passport-adjacent convenience and English-language ease.

## Barbados: the North American time zone play

Barbados's Welcome Stamp is a twelve-month remote-work visa built specifically around North American time zones and easy flight connections from the US and Canadian East Coast — a deliberate positioning choice distinct from the EU-facing programs above. It commonly requires proof of income around USD 50,000 annually (roughly USD 4,000+ monthly, though some sources cite lower thresholds closer to USD 2,000 monthly for a base qualifying income — check the current official figure on the Barbados Welcome Stamp government portal before applying, since cited numbers vary), and the application fee itself has historically run in the low thousands of dollars for an individual, more for a family. Barbados is not cheap by Caribbean standards, but it offers reliable infrastructure and a genuinely different appeal: same-day overlap with US business hours.

## Bali: the long-stay outlier with real legal complexity

Bali (part of Indonesia) is the odd one out on this list because Indonesia's residency options for long-term stays — including the KITAS second-home visa route sometimes discussed alongside a five-year horizon — involve property arrangements foreigners frequently misjudge: Indonesia restricts direct foreign land ownership, so most long-stayers use leasehold ("hak sewa") arrangements or nominee/corporate structures, both of which carry legal risk if not set up properly with a licensed notary (notaris) and reviewed by independent counsel. Monthly living costs in Bali can be strikingly low outside the main tourist corridors, but the up-front legal and notary costs of securing a leasehold property are a real, and often underestimated, expense.

## Matching the island to what actually matters to you

Time zone alignment is one of the most underrated factors in choosing an island base: Barbados suits anyone who needs to stay in sync with US or Canadian business hours, while Madeira and Malta both sit comfortably within Central European working hours despite their island remoteness — useful if your clients or employer are EU-based. Bali, by contrast, runs many hours ahead of both Europe and the US East Coast, which works well for people serving Asia-Pacific markets or who simply want a genuine break from synchronous meetings, but it's a real friction point for anyone needing regular overlap with a European or American team.

## Healthcare and insurance across all four

None of these four islands should be assumed to offer automatic public healthcare access to newly arrived remote workers — Madeira, as part of Portugal, offers the best long-term path into public coverage once you're a contributing tax resident, while Malta's Nomad Residence Permit holders and Barbados Welcome Stamp holders are both generally expected to carry private international health insurance as a condition of the visa itself. Bali's healthcare infrastructure for serious conditions often means a medical evacuation to Singapore or Australia, making comprehensive private coverage with evacuation benefits a near-necessity rather than an optional extra for long-term residents there.
