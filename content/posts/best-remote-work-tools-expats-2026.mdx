A nomad stack is not “ten apps I like.” It is five problems that break a remote job when you change countries: **getting paid**, **getting online on day one**, **not leaking the company on café Wi-Fi**, **not forcing every teammate into your 3 a.m.**, and **not accidentally overstaying Schengen**. Pick tools against those jobs. Ignore award lists.

## Money: local details without a local bank

**Wise** is the default for many people who invoice in one currency and spend in another: multi-currency balances, **local account details in 8+ currencies**, and a card that converts at a mid-market rate without the theatre of a hotel cashier. **Revolut** is the usual alternative when you want in-app budgeting and card controls in the same pocket.

The problem they solve is not “fintech is cool.” It is **salary arriving in EUR while rent is due in THB**, plus ATM fees that eat the geo-arbitrage you moved for. Pair the account with a rule: never accept dynamic currency conversion at the terminal. The app does not save you if you tap “charge in USD” in a Lisbon supermarket.

Keep a second rail (backup card, small cash in a major currency) for the week a card is frozen. Tools fail at borders.

## Connectivity: eSIM first, local SIM later

**Airalo** and **Holafly** exist so you have a working data line **before** you have an address, a tax ID, or a shop that will sell a prepaid SIM to a tourist. That first week is when you still need 2FA, Slack, and maps. An eSIM is the bridge, not a forever plan: once you can show a passport at a carrier store, a local SIM is usually cheaper per gigabyte.

Coworking **day passes and multi-city memberships** are the other connectivity layer. A network pass is useful if you actually change cities monthly. It is wasted money if you will sit in one apartment for a year. Buy the problem you have.

The first-week failure mode is always the same: you land, the hotel Wi-Fi is captive, the local SIM shop wants a residence certificate, and your employer’s 2FA SMS never arrives. An eSIM plus a downloaded authenticator backup is how you still ship on day one. Once you have a local number, port the 2FA you can and keep the eSIM as a roam backup for the next border.

## Security: VPN and a password manager as job equipment

Public coworking Wi-Fi is a hostile network with good coffee. A **VPN** is how you reach home-country banking, streaming, and tax portals that geoblock, and how you stop the café from seeing the session. Treat it as mandatory if you handle **corporate or client data**, not as a piracy toy. Split-tunnel if your employer already routes you through a corporate VPN; double-VPN is how calls die.

**1Password** or **Bitwarden** is how you survive device and network churn. Unique passwords, a shared vault for a partner, and recovery codes stored *not* only on the laptop you might leave in a Bolt. People who “remember their passwords” are the people who reset them in a panic in a language they do not speak. Turn on travel-mode or a hidden vault before a border crossing if your threat model includes device inspection.

## Async work: stop scheduling the planet around you

Multi-timezone teams die on calendar Tetris. **Loom** (or any short recorded walkthrough) replaces a meeting that existed only because you could not write. Slack and **Notion** written updates — with a timestamp and a decision — let London sleep while Mexico City ships.

Video tools that **record and transcribe** are for the same reason: a colleague eight hours away watches the standup later. If your culture still requires everyone live, no app will fix it. The tool only works if the team agrees that async is allowed. Write the decision in Notion the same day as the Loom; a 12-minute video with no written outcome is just a meeting that cannot be searched.

## Day-count and tax-residency tracking

Schengen **90/180** (and analogue clocks elsewhere) is arithmetic that humans lose at the third weekend trip. A dedicated **day tracker** is cheaper than a fine or a refused entry. Manual spreadsheets drift; specialised apps do not forget the airport day.

This is not a substitute for a tax advisor. It is how you notice you are about to become resident somewhere you did not plan. Log the day you land, not the day you remember. The same log is what a CPA asks for when two countries both think you lived there. A calendar of Airbnb bookings is not a legal day count.

Build the stack around **Wise/Revolut, eSIM, VPN plus a password manager, Loom-and-transcript async, and a stay-day tracker**. That is the 2026 nomad toolkit. Everything else is preference.
