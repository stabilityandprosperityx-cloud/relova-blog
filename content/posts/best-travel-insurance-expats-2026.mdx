Travel insurance and health insurance for expats are not the same product, and conflating them is the single most common mistake in this category — a plan built for occasional trips will not hold up as your only coverage once you're actually living somewhere full-time. The four names that dominate this space split cleanly into two tiers: budget nomad-focused plans and full international private medical insurance, and the price gap between them reflects a real difference in what's covered, not just marketing.

## SafetyWing is a four-week product, not a monthly one

SafetyWing's Nomad Insurance Essential plan is commonly quoted around **$56 for a four-week period** for travelers under 40 — which sounds like a monthly price but isn't quite one, since 13 four-week cycles don't line up with 12 calendar months and the difference compounds over a year. It's a genuinely useful, low-cost option for short-term travel and basic emergency coverage, but it's built for people bouncing between countries, not for someone who needs comprehensive ongoing care in one place. SafetyWing's Complete plan, aimed at closer-to-full health coverage, starts noticeably higher — commonly quoted from roughly **$177 a month** and up, priced individually based on a health questionnaire, age, and coverage level.

## World Nomads sits in the middle, built for trips, not residency

World Nomads quotes commonly land in the **$100-200 a month** range depending on destination, age, and coverage tier, and it's explicitly positioned as travel insurance for people on the move — trip cancellation, gear coverage, and adventure-activity add-ons are its strengths, not long-term outpatient care or maternity coverage. It's a reasonable choice for a nomad genuinely hopping between countries every few weeks, and a poor fit for someone settling into a single city for a year or more.

## Cigna Global and Allianz Care are the real international health plans

Cigna Global commonly runs **$150-400 a month** and Allianz Care roughly **$200-500 a month**, both scaling heavily with age, deductible choice, and whether you add maternity, dental, or repatriation cover — these are full international private medical insurance products, not travel insurance with a health add-on. If you're actually relocating rather than traveling, one of these two (or a comparable full IPMI provider) is the more honest fit despite the higher sticker price, because they're built to cover ongoing, non-emergency care the budget nomad plans explicitly exclude or cap.

## The Schengen visa requirement is a hard number, not a suggestion

Any Schengen short-stay visa application requires travel medical insurance with coverage of **at least €30,000**, valid across all Schengen states, covering emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, and repatriation in case of death. This is a fixed EU-wide minimum, not a per-country variation, and consulates do check the policy document for that exact figure before approving a visa — a policy that says "comprehensive coverage" without stating the €30,000 threshold explicitly can get an application flagged. If you're insurance-shopping specifically for a Schengen application, confirm your chosen policy's certificate states the €30,000 minimum in writing, not just that it meets "EU requirements" in vague marketing language.

## Match the plan to what you're actually doing

If you're moving between countries every few weeks, SafetyWing Essential or World Nomads covers the real risk at a real price. If you're settling somewhere for a year-plus, price out Cigna Global or Allianz Care properly rather than defaulting to the cheaper nomad plan and discovering its outpatient or chronic-condition limits the first time you need ongoing care.
