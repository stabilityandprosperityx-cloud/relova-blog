Canada stopped running [Express Entry](/blog/move-to-canada-guide-2026) as one general pool competing on a single score years ago, and by 2026 that shift has fully reshaped who gets invited. [Express Entry](/blog/move-to-montreal-guide-2026) now runs category-based draws alongside general ones — French-language proficiency, healthcare and social services, STEM, trades, education, and Canadian-experience senior managers and physicians each get their own invitation rounds with their own cut-offs. As of mid-September 2026 the pool held roughly 226,800 candidates, and cut-offs split wildly by category: a French-speaking candidate could clear the bar around 382–420 CRS, a healthcare worker around 467–475, while a general Canadian Experience Class draw still needed 507–523. If your profile fits a category — most people never check — that's the fastest lane in the system, sometimes by 100+ points versus the general pool.

## [Express Entry](/blog/move-to-vancouver-guide-2026) is now a portfolio of programs, not one queue

The three Express Entry-managed programs (Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Worker, Federal Skilled Trades) feed the same pool, but which draw picks you up depends on your NOC/TEER occupation code, language scores, and Canadian work history. A profile with zero Canadian experience and no French competes only in general or occupation-specific draws, where scores regularly sit above 500. Standard processing from Invitation to Apply to a decision is about six months, and once ITA lands you have 60 days to submit a complete application — medical exam, police certificates, and proof of funds all need to be ready before that clock starts, not after.

## Provincial nomination is still the biggest single lever

A Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) nomination adds **600 points** to your CRS score outright — enough to jump almost any queue. The trade-off is a longer, two-stage process: you apply to a specific province's stream (Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, and Manitoba run the highest-volume ones), wait for provincial approval, then get invited into Express Entry with the nomination attached. Combined PNP-then-Express-Entry timelines commonly run 12–18 months rather than Express Entry's six, but for anyone stuck below the general cut-off, it's often the only realistic route to an ITA. Provinces set their own occupation-in-demand lists and update them without much warning, so check the specific province's current list before assuming your job qualifies — a listed occupation this year can disappear from next year's stream entirely.

## Regional and francophone routes worth checking before you assume you don't qualify

Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot successor programs and the Atlantic Immigration Program target candidates willing to settle outside Toronto, Vancouver, and Montreal, often with lower effective score requirements in exchange for a job offer or community endorsement tied to that region. Francophone-outside-Quebec candidates get their own category draw, which in 2026 has consistently cleared at CRS scores 100+ points below general draws — a meaningful advantage for anyone with conversational French willing to invest in TEF/TCF test prep before applying.

## What actually delays applications

The application itself rarely stalls on immigration processing — it stalls on document readiness. Police certificates from every country you've lived in for six-plus months since age 18 can take weeks to arrive by mail, medical exams must be done through an IRCC-approved panel physician (not just any doctor), and proof of funds needs to show the required settlement amount sitting in an account for a continuous period, not just on the application date. Start gathering these the moment you enter the pool, not after an ITA arrives — the 60-day submission window is unforgiving if you're waiting on a certificate from a country with slow record offices.

Canada PR in 2026 rewards specificity: know which category draw you actually fit, chase a provincial nomination if your general-pool score is short of the cut-off, and have your document file built before the invitation — not after.
