**Canggu vs Ubud vs Seminyak Bali 2026** is three different islands in one island. **Canggu** has Asia’s densest nomad coworking stack (Dojo Bali, Canggu Coworking) and the worst peak traffic on Jl. Batu Bolong and Jl. Pantai Berawa (**16:00–19:00**). **Ubud** is quieter, cheaper, and better for deep work—with weaker internet. **Seminyak** is the polished restaurant/nightlife strip, least oriented to remote work. ## Neighborhood profiles and 2026 rents

One-bedroom villa rents vary considerably even within a single area: Canggu runs $800–1,500 a month, with Pererenan and Berawa usually landing under the pricier Batu Bolong strip; Ubud runs $400–900, genuinely the most affordable of the three; and Seminyak runs $1,200–2,500, the priciest by a wide margin. Comfortable all-in monthly budgets follow the same order — Canggu $1,200–2,200, Ubud $1,000–1,600, Seminyak $1,500–3,000. Internet averages about 50–100 Mbps in Canggu, 20–60 Mbps in Ubud, and is often genuinely unstable in Seminyak's beach-club-heavy stretches, where infrastructure was built for tourism rather than remote work. The bigger story is the trajectory: Canggu rents have climbed roughly 30–50% since 2020 as the area's popularity outpaced new construction, which means the "cheap paradise" reputation that drew the first wave of nomads there no longer really applies — Canggu today prices closer to a mid-tier European city than the bargain it once was.

## Visas: E33G, Second Home, and B211A

The visa picture is the same across all three areas, since it's set nationally rather than by region, but it's worth understanding because it shapes who actually settles in each place long-term. Indonesia's E33G Remote Worker visa, introduced in April 2024, requires roughly $60,000 a year in documented income, runs for one year initially, and can typically be extended once for a two-year total before requiring a fresh application from outside the country — exact renewal mechanics are reported inconsistently across sources, so confirm directly with Indonesian immigration before relying on this figure. Indonesia's Second Home visa is a different animal entirely: 5–10 years of residency in exchange for roughly $130,000–133,000 parked on deposit, a wealth-gated option that skews toward retirees and remote entrepreneurs with real capital rather than typical salaried remote workers. B211A, the 60-day social/business visa extendable toward 180 days, remains the workhorse for shorter stays and is what most Canggu and Seminyak visitors actually use, even though it exists in a legal grey zone for remote work rather than explicitly authorizing it.

## Tax residency after 183 days

The one rule that applies uniformly and catches people off guard is tax residency. Spend 183 days in Indonesia within any 12-month period and you become a tax resident on your worldwide income, not just Indonesian-sourced income, with progressive rates running up to 35%. This is a hard trigger that doesn't care which visa you're on or which of the three areas you're based in — anyone planning to stay in Bali long-term should structure their remittances, entity choices, and home-country tax filings with that 183-day line in mind well before they cross it, ideally with a tax adviser familiar with both jurisdictions.

## Matching the area to how you actually work

Canggu's budgets ($1,200–2,200 for a comfortable solo month) and internet (50–100 Mbps) support Asia's largest concentrated nomad community and its dense coworking stack — the trade-off is worse traffic, higher noise, and the sense of a place that's outgrown its infrastructure. Ubud runs cheaper ($1,000–1,600) with weaker internet (20–60 Mbps, adequate for most workflows but not for heavy video), and its identity leans wellness and nature rather than nightlife, which is exactly why it suits people doing genuinely deep, focused work over people who need constant social stimulation. Seminyak sits at the other extreme — the most expensive of the three ($1,500–3,000), with often-unstable beach-club Wi-Fi and minimal dedicated nomad infrastructure, because it's built around dining, nightlife, and short-stay tourism rather than long-term remote work. If your priority is community and coworking, Canggu; if it's cost and focus, Ubud; if wifi and productivity aren't the point of your trip, Seminyak.
