Co-living pitches itself as the friction-free landing pad — furnished, community-programmed, and bookable without a local guarantor or a deposit the size of a used car. That is a real value proposition for the first one to three months somewhere new. It is a much weaker one once you pass six months, when the nightly-rate-style pricing model stops competing with a normal lease.

## Selina's brand survived; its ownership and footprint did not

If you last checked Selina's numbers a couple of years ago, update them: the company declared insolvency and was delisted from Nasdaq in **July 2024** after a liquidity collapse, and its assets were sold roughly five weeks later to **Collective Hospitality**, a Singapore-based hospitality group, in a deal covering around **100 properties** operated under long-term leases. That is down meaningfully from Selina's earlier peak footprint, and the buyer has stated a multi-year turnaround plan rather than aggressive re-expansion, so treat any current "160+ locations" figure as outdated and check Selina's own site for the live property count before booking — coverage in a specific city is no longer something you can assume. Where it still operates, monthly pricing commonly runs **USD 800–2,500** depending on the city and unit type, with coworking access typically bundled in.

## Outpost still leans into Southeast Asia's community-first model

**Outpost**, concentrated in Bali (Canggu) and Chiang Mai, has kept a more consistent model through the same period — villa-style co-living with structured community programming (group dinners, coworking, organized activities) rather than a hotel-adjacent feel. Pricing commonly runs **USD 600–1,200** a month depending on room type and season, and it remains one of the more reliable options for nomads who specifically want the social infrastructure, not just a furnished bed.

## Hmlet works as a landing pad in Singapore and Hong Kong, not a long-term plan

**Hmlet**'s Singapore and Hong Kong inventory runs **SGD 2,500–4,500** a month with cleaning and utilities bundled — genuinely useful for a 30-to-90-day landing period while you sort out a proper lease and, in Singapore's case, the paperwork a private rental often demands. Past the six-month mark, the math flips: a normal unfurnished or lightly furnished lease in the same building class is reliably cheaper once you're willing to buy your own furniture and manage your own cleaning.

## The co-living premium is real and worth pricing explicitly

Once you normalize for bundled utilities, cleaning, and furniture depreciation, co-living operators commonly carry a **20–40% premium** over an equivalent private lease in the same neighborhood. That premium buys flexibility (short notice periods, no deposit, no furniture to sell when you leave) rather than square footage — it is the right trade for an uncertain first stretch in a new city and the wrong one for anyone who already knows they're staying a year or more.

## Smaller regional operators are worth checking before you default to the big names

Selina and Outpost dominate the search results, but a growing tier of smaller, city-specific co-living operators — often a single well-run property rather than a chain — has filled gaps left by the bigger brands' consolidation, particularly in Latin America and parts of Southeast Asia. These tend to have less polished booking sites and no venture-scale marketing budget, which also means less pressure to hit occupancy targets by overselling amenities that don't exist yet. Checking a destination's local nomad Facebook groups or Reddit threads for current recommendations is a better signal in 2026 than trusting a "best co-living" ranking article that was last meaningfully updated before Selina's ownership changed.

## Read the exit terms before the amenities list

The genuine risk with co-living, beyond price, is operational: a property can lose its lease, change management, or shut down with less notice than a traditional landlord would typically give, since the underlying real estate is usually leased by the operator rather than owned outright. Before booking anything beyond a one-month trial stay, check the cancellation and relocation policy specifically — reputable operators will rehouse you at a sister property or refund the unused balance if a location closes, but this is not universal, and it is a far more relevant question for a six-month booking than for a two-week one.
