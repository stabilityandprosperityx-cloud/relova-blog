Every one of these four programs is still running in 2026 — that's less common than you'd think in this category, where "pay you to move" headlines routinely outlive the actual program by a year or two. What none of the viral coverage tells you is the compliance clause: every program here has a clawback or repayment trigger if you leave early, and that clause is usually the part that determines whether the offer is actually worth taking.

## Calabria's small-village money, with a fast deadline

Several Calabrian municipalities under **2,000 residents** offer up to **€28,000** to applicants under 40 who commit to relocating their primary residence there, typically paid either as a roughly €20,000 lump sum or as a monthly stipend of up to €1,000 spread over two to three years. The catch that trips people up: you generally must complete the move to the qualifying municipality within **90 days** of approval — a tight window if you're also handling a visa, an international move, and setting up a life in rural southern Italy simultaneously. Each participating comune publishes its own specific terms through the Calabria Region's official channels, and they vary enough between towns that "Calabria pays €28,000" is a category description, not a single offer — check the specific municipality's call for applications before assuming the headline figure applies to your situation.

## Ireland's island grant is a renovation subsidy, not income

Ireland's "Our Living Islands" scheme offers up to **€60,000** for renovating a vacant property, or **€84,000** for a derelict one, on roughly 23 qualifying offshore islands including Arranmore, Tory, Clare Island, and the Aran Islands — a 20% premium over the equivalent mainland vacant/derelict property grants. It comes with a genuinely long **ten-year clawback**: sell the property or stop using it as your home (or as a long-term rental through the Residential Tenancies Board — holiday lets are explicitly excluded) within that decade, and Ireland can reclaim the grant. It's also worth being blunt about the uptake: as of early 2025 only around 29 applications had been filed across every qualifying island, which tells you either the eligible property stock is scarce or the bureaucracy is a genuine deterrent — likely both. And critically, this grant confers zero immigration status on its own; a non-EU applicant still needs a separate valid visa or residency basis to actually live in Ireland.

## Albinen still pays Swiss francs to fight depopulation

The Swiss mountain village of Albinen, in canton Valais, continues offering **CHF 25,000** per adult plus **CHF 10,000** per child to new residents who commit to staying at least ten years and either build or substantially renovate a home there. It's a genuinely small, specific program — don't read it as representative of Switzerland's broader immigration posture, which remains tightly quota-gated for non-EU nationals (see our [Zürich and Geneva guide](/blog/move-to-zurich-geneva-guide-2026) for that reality). Albinen's incentive solves a rural depopulation problem in one village; it doesn't touch the separate, much harder question of whether you're legally allowed to live and work in Switzerland at all.

## Tulsa Remote is the US benchmark for a reason

Tulsa, Oklahoma's remote-worker program still offers **$10,000** to relocate and work remotely from the city, and unlike many copycat US city programs launched in its wake, it has remained active continuously and recently drew fresh attention from a study on its return on investment for the city itself. It requires proof of remote employment or self-employment income from outside Oklahoma, a minimum one-year commitment to Tulsa, and the standard US work authorization any remote worker already needs — it's not an immigration program, just a relocation subsidy for people already eligible to live and work in the US.

## The clause that actually matters

Read every one of these for its specific repayment trigger before treating the headline figure as free money: Calabria's is a fast relocation deadline, Ireland's is a ten-year property-use clawback, Albinen's is a ten-year residency commitment, and Tulsa's is a one-year minimum stay. None of these programs are scams, but all of them are structured to claw back the payment if you treat the destination as a short-term arbitrage rather than an actual move.
