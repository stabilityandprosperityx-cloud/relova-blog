More than **55 countries** now run some form of remote-worker or digital-nomad residence. Income floors stretch from effectively **$0** (Georgia, Mauritius) to roughly **$7,500–7,800/month** (Dubai, Iceland). The right pick is rarely “the cheapest visa” — it is the combination of income proof, tax treatment, family add-ons, and whether you care about a path to permanent residence.

## Snapshot comparison (2026)

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Italy (remote / DNV-style, stabilized 2026) | **€28,000/year** | Residence if docs clear | Some new-resident reliefs; Mezzogiorno schemes near **7%** for 10 years | Possible but paperwork-heavy |

Family filings commonly lift the income test by **25–75% per dependent**; Croatia is lighter — roughly **~10%** per family member in common guidance. Always verify the consulate checklist for your nationality.

## Tax regimes that change the math

Croatia’s **0%** foreign-income treatment under the **183-day** line is powerful for short-cycle nomads who can leave and re-enter cleanly. UAE and Costa Rica also sit in the **0% foreign income** conversation for many remote profiles.

Spain’s **Beckham Law** is the opposite trade: stay longer, accept a flat **24%** (or **15%** in some cases) on qualifying income up to **€600,000**, and buy into a deeper local life. Italy’s reduced base (**50%** reliefs for some new residents) and southern **7%/10-year** offers, plus Greece’s **7%/15-year** new-resident schemes, reward people who actually relocate their tax residence — not tourists with a coworking pass.

## Dubai’s 2026 paperwork trap

Dubai’s Virtual Working Programme remains a high-income product (~**$7,500/month**). From **27 January 2026**, bank statements must cover **six months** (was three). Incomplete packets are treated as automatic refusals — do not apply “almost ready.”

## Profile-based picks

**Lower-income freelancer / early nomad.** Favour **Georgia** or Balkan short-stay logic (Croatia if you clear **€3,295** and accept the 18-month ceiling). Prioritise burn rate and clean exits over citizenship marketing.

**High-income household.** **Spain** (if you want EU life and community) or **Dubai** (if you want tax simplicity and can document six months of banking). Model dependent uplifts early — family deltas of 25–75% change feasibility fast.

**EU citizenship on the horizon.** **Portugal** is still the conversation starter, but update your timeline for the **May 2026** nationality law: many non-EU/non-CPLP paths now run **10 years**, with **7 years** for EU/CPLP nationals. Spain’s longer DNV + community stay remains a serious alternative if Iberian Spanish life fits better than Lisbon rents.

**US citizens.** The **2026 FEIE** exclusion is **$132,900** of foreign earned income — useful, not magical. You still file US returns every year regardless of where you sleep.

Pick the visa for the life you will actually live for 12–24 months, not the Instagram city. Income floors move with local minimum wages; re-check Spain and Portugal numbers before you transfer application fees.


Application hygiene beats brand marketing. Consulates increasingly treat incomplete bank evidence as refuse-first (Dubai’s six-month statement rule from **27 January 2026** is the clearest example). Mirror that discipline everywhere: align employment letters with the exact income floor, translate contracts before you book biometrics, and do not assume a tourist entry can be “quietly converted” in-country.

Renewals and exit rules also shape which program is livable. Croatia’s **18-month** ceiling plus mandatory time outside before reapplication is a feature for rotating nomads and a bug for anyone who wants one continuous address. Spain and Portugal lean toward longer residence stacks if you keep filing clean renewals — but Portugal’s **May 2026** nationality reform means citizenship marketing from older blog posts is outdated. Re-read the clock before you choose a country “for the passport.”
