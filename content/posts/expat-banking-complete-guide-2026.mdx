## Wise, Revolut, and N26 in plain language

**Wise** holds **40+ currencies** with local details in **8+** (UK sort code, EU IBAN, U.S. ACH, etc.). FX fees typically sit around **0.33–0.67%** at the mid-market rate with no markup and no monthly fee. It is an **EMI, not a bank**—funds are safeguarded, but **not** covered by a national deposit-insurance scheme.

**Revolut** tiers (Free / Premium / Metal) matter. On Free, mid-market conversion is capped near **£1,000/month**; above that expect about **+1%**, plus another **~1%** weekend markup on Free. In the UK and parts of the EU it operates as a licensed bank: UK deposits are covered by **FSCS up to £120,000** (from **1 December 2025**); EU deposits via the Lithuanian scheme up to **€100,000**.

**N26** is a German neobank with a real **German IBAN**—critical where landlords or employers reject foreign IBANs. It is **not available in the UK** after Brexit. For how local accounts get opened, see [open a bank account abroad](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/how-to-open-bank-account-abroad-2025) and [Portugal banking](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/open-bank-account-portugal-2026).

## The stack most expats actually use

Recommended default: **Wise** for international transfers and multi-currency balances + a **local bank** in your host country for rent, salary, and domestic payments. Local banks often add **1.5–3%** FX markup—use them for domestic rails, not for converting your life savings. Details on remittances: [send money internationally](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/send-money-internationally-expat-2026).

## Large transfers and U.S. reporting

For one-off property or large remittances (**$10,000+**), specialists such as **OFX, TorFX, or Currencies Direct** frequently beat both retail banks and consumer apps. U.S. citizens: **FBAR** applies when foreign accounts aggregate over **$10,000**; Wise and Revolut report under **FATCA** as compatible platforms—plan filings, do not treat “app money” as invisible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Wise a bank with deposit insurance?**

No. Wise is an **EMI**. Money is safeguarded, but it is **not** covered like a bank deposit under FSCS/EDIS-style schemes.

**When does Revolut actually insure my money?**

When balances sit with its **licensed bank** entities—UK **FSCS £120,000** (from Dec 2025) or EU **€100,000** via Lithuania. Product wrappers vary by country.

**Why do people still open a local bank?**

Rent, payroll, and some utilities demand a **local IBAN**. N26’s German IBAN helps in the eurozone; UK residents cannot use N26 post-Brexit.

**What should I use for a property transfer?**

Compare **OFX / TorFX / Currencies Direct** against Wise for tickets of **$10,000+**—the spread often dominates the headline fee.

**What must U.S. expats remember?**

Aggregate foreign accounts over **$10,000** trigger **FBAR**, and FATCA-compatible platforms share data with the IRS.
