## Visa-Free Entry, But No Special EU Privileges

[Georgia](/blog/georgia-digital-nomad-tax-visa-deep-dive-2026) isn't an EU member and isn't in the Schengen Area, so EU citizens don't get the automatic, borderless entry they're used to within the bloc — but the entry terms are still generous: essentially all EU passports receive the same up-to-**365-day** visa-free stamp on arrival that most other nationalities get, which makes [Georgia](/blog/retire-to-georgia-country-guide-2026) one of the more relaxed non-EU destinations available for extended stays without any formal visa application. That said, a handful of things genuinely differ for EU citizens versus, say, an American or a Brit, and they're worth knowing before you assume "EU" status carries any special privilege here — mostly, it doesn't, with a few specific exceptions.

## Your EHIC Doesn't Work Here

The most common misconception EU citizens bring with them is assuming their **EHIC (European Health Insurance Card)** works the way it does when traveling within the EU. It doesn't — EHIC is only valid within the EU/EEA and Switzerland, and Georgian hospitals and clinics have no reciprocal arrangement with it whatsoever. Anyone arriving from an EU country needs to buy private international health insurance from day one, exactly as any other nationality would; the EHIC habit of assuming healthcare access "just works" abroad needs to be actively unlearned here.

## SEPA Doesn't Reach Georgian Banks

Banking has a similarly counterintuitive gap: **SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area)** transfers, the low-cost, near-instant euro transfer system EU citizens rely on constantly at home, are not available to or from Georgian bank accounts, since Georgia sits outside the SEPA zone entirely. Practically, this means EU citizens can't simply SEPA-transfer euros into a Georgian account the way they'd move money between, say, a German and a French bank. **Wise** has become the standard workaround, moving EUR into GEL (or vice versa) reliably and at reasonable rates without needing SEPA rails, and most long-term EU residents in Tbilisi end up running a Wise-plus-local-bank combination for exactly this reason.

## Exit Tax Rules Vary Sharply by Member State

Where EU citizenship does matter is on the way home, specifically around exit tax and residency-break rules, and this varies sharply by member state, so treat any of the following as a starting point for professional advice rather than a final answer. **Germany** has exit tax provisions (Wegzugsbesteuerung) that can trigger a deemed disposal of certain assets — broadly, unrealized capital gains on qualifying shareholdings — when a German tax resident with assets above roughly **€500,000** relocates to a jurisdiction Germany considers low-tax, which Georgia's 1% Individual Entrepreneur regime can arguably qualify as depending on your structure; this is a genuinely complex area and worth dedicated advice from a German tax adviser before you move, not after. **France** applies its own strict scrutiny of residency ties when someone claims to have left French tax residency, looking closely at where your family lives, where your primary home is, and the center of your economic interests — French authorities are known for pushing back hard on residency claims that look opportunistic. **Estonia**, by contrast, tends to apply a comparatively lighter exit process, partly because its e-Residency system already assumes a globally distributed user base and its tax framework is less aggressive about clawing back departing residents' unrealized gains.

## EU Expat Communities in Tbilisi

On the ground, EU nationalities have formed small but real expat pockets in Tbilisi — German and French communities are each informally estimated in the low thousands, enough to support language meetups, church or cultural groups, and a reasonable social network, though nowhere near the scale of the American or Russian expat presence in the city.

## The Georgian Tax System Doesn't Care About Your EU Passport

Practically, once you're actually living and working in Georgia, the tax mechanics are identical regardless of your EU passport: registering as an Individual Entrepreneur to access the roughly **1%** small-business tax rate on qualifying turnover works the same for a German freelancer as for anyone else — EU status doesn't grant any preferential Georgian tax treatment, only easier visa-free entry and (for some member states) more complex exit-tax homework back home.

## Driving Licences and Easy Trips Back to Schengen

One genuinely useful EU-specific perk: an EU driving licence is generally usable in Georgia for the duration of your visa-free stay, and Georgia's location makes short trips back into the Schengen Area for renewals, banking errands, or visits home straightforward from Tbilisi's growing set of European flight connections — a logistical convenience non-EU nationalities on some routes don't share quite as easily.
