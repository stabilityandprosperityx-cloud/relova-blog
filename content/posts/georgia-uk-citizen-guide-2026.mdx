British passport holders get the same up-to-**365-day** visa-free entry to [Georgia](/blog/how-to-move-to-georgia-country) that most nationalities receive, a benefit that survived Brexit cleanly since it was never an EU arrangement in the first place — [Georgia](/blog/retire-to-georgia-country-guide-2026)'s visa-free list is set independently and has included the UK since long before the UK left the EU. What's genuinely UK-specific about this move sits almost entirely in healthcare, pensions, and banking, not in the entry rules themselves.

The healthcare point is the one that catches UK travelers most often, because the post-Brexit UK issued the **GHIC (Global Health Insurance Card)** as a partial successor to the EHIC, and there's a lingering assumption that "some card" will cover you somewhere. It won't help in Georgia. GHIC only provides reciprocal healthcare access within the EU (broadly following the old EHIC framework for a subset of EU countries), and Georgia sits entirely outside both the EU and any UK reciprocal healthcare agreement. There is no fallback here — anyone relocating from the UK needs private international health insurance from the day they land, full stop, and the assumption "I'll sort it out like I would in Spain" simply doesn't transfer. Private cover in Georgia is genuinely affordable relative to UK private insurance, commonly running **€30–80 a month** for solid coverage, but it has to be arranged proactively rather than assumed.

On National Insurance, the advice is the same as for any UK citizen moving abroad long-term: voluntary **Class 2 contributions**, priced at roughly **£3.45 a week**, are a cheap way to keep your NI record intact and protect your eventual State Pension entitlement while you're not paying into the UK system through employment. This is easy to let lapse once you're focused on settling into a new country, and the cost of catching up years later — if it's even possible — is far higher than paying as you go.

The pension situation for UK citizens in Georgia is actually more favorable than in some other popular retirement destinations, and worth flagging as a genuine positive rather than a caveat: the UK State Pension is paid into Georgian accounts and, per current DWP rules, generally **remains uprated** — unlike the "frozen pension" situation UK pensioners face in a number of other non-reciprocal countries (Canada and Australia being the most commonly cited examples), Georgia isn't currently on that frozen list under standard DWP treatment. This is genuinely worth verifying with DWP directly before relying on it for retirement planning, since these classifications can shift, but as of current guidance it's a point in Georgia's favor for UK retirees specifically.

Banking works reasonably smoothly for most UK expats: **Starling** and **Monzo** generally function fine for holding and spending GBP while traveling or living abroad, and most UK expats in Tbilisi keep one of these running alongside a **Wise** account for actually converting GBP into GEL at a reasonable rate — UK high-street banks tend to apply worse FX margins and sometimes flag foreign transactions more aggressively than the newer digital-first banks do.

Driving is worth planning ahead of time rather than assuming: a UK driving licence is generally recognized for driving in Georgia during your visa-free stay window, though anyone planning to stay longer-term or convert to a local licence should check the current bilateral recognition rules directly, since these arrangements can be updated and aren't guaranteed to persist indefinitely post-Brexit in the way EU licence recognition might.

The British expat community in Tbilisi is real but modest — informally estimated at somewhere around **three to five thousand** people, enough to support a handful of social groups and meetups but nowhere near the scale you'd find in more established UK expat hubs like Spain or Portugal. On the tax and registration side, once you're set up, the Individual Entrepreneur small-business regime at roughly **1%** on qualifying turnover works identically for a British freelancer as for anyone else — there's no UK-specific wrinkle there, just the standard HMRC Statutory Residence Test question of whether you've genuinely become non-resident for UK tax purposes, which is worth running properly regardless of destination.
