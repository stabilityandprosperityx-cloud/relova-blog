Gran Canaria earns the "miniature continent" cliché honestly — you can wake up in a genuine city, drive forty minutes, and be in a landscape that looks nothing like where you started. That range is the whole pitch, and it means the area you pick matters more here than on most Spanish islands.

## Las Palmas is a real city, not a resort town

**Las Palmas de Gran Canaria**, the capital, is the one part of the island that functions as a genuine year-round Spanish city rather than a seasonal beach economy: proper public transport, a working professional job market beyond tourism, and restaurants that don't shut for the off-season. A one-bedroom here typically runs **€650–1,000** a month, the island's highest band, and Las Canteras beach sits inside the city itself — an unusual amenity for an urban rental market at this price point. It is the pick for anyone who wants island life without giving up city infrastructure.

## The south is built for a different kind of long-term stay

**Maspalomas**, anchoring the island's southern resort belt, runs **€600–950** for a comparable one-bedroom and offers the most reliably sunny, driest microclimate on the island — a genuine draw for anyone relocating partly for weather consistency, since Las Palmas and the north catch noticeably more cloud cover off the trade winds. **Puerto de Mogán**, the quieter premium village further west, comes in cheaper at **€550–800** despite a strong reputation, mostly because it trades big-resort infrastructure and public transport for a small-harbor, slower pace that suits retirees and remote workers more than anyone needing daily amenities within walking distance.

## The Canary Islands run a lighter tax regime than mainland Spain

Gran Canaria, like the rest of the Canary Islands, sits outside the EU VAT zone and charges **IGIC** at a general **7%** rate instead of mainland Spain's 21% IVA. That structural gap covers everyday purchases — groceries, dining, most retail — and is the main reason a comparable rent band goes further here than in an equivalent mainland Spanish city. It applies uniformly regardless of residency status, so it benefits a two-week visitor and a ten-year resident equally; it is not a personal perk tied to your visa.

## The visa math is identical to the rest of Spain

Gran Canaria offers no separate immigration track from the mainland — non-EU citizens relocating without local employment use the same **non-lucrative visa (NLV)**, with the same 2026 income floor: **400% of Spain's IPREM**, which is frozen at €600 a month for 2026, putting the threshold at **€28,800 a year (about €2,400 a month)** for the main applicant plus **€7,200 a year** per additional dependent. Private health insurance with no co-pays is a standard supporting requirement, since the NLV does not grant access to Spain's public health system on its own. A realistic monthly budget covering rent, insurance, and daily living for a single remote worker lands around **€1,600–2,400**, with the top of that range driven mainly by whether you're in Las Palmas proper or leaning on a car to live comfortably in the south or west.
