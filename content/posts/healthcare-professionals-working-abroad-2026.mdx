Licensing, not visa paperwork, is what actually delays overseas-trained nurses and doctors — and 2026 has made the UK route noticeably harder, not easier, through simple test-centre attrition. The clinical exam is the bottleneck in every major destination; the salary you're chasing at the other end is usually the easy part to research and the licensing pathway is the part guides skip.

## The UK's OSCE bottleneck just got worse

Registering with the Nursing and Midwifery Council means clearing document verification, an English test, a computer-based test (CBT), and then the practical OSCE exam — and as of 2026, two of the UK's approved OSCE test centres have shut their doors: Oxford Brookes stopped new bookings on 31 July 2026, and Northumbria University closed in February 2026. That leaves three approved centres nationwide — the University of Northampton, Ulster University in Derry~Londonderry, and Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust — for the entire flow of overseas nurses trying to register. The OSCE itself costs **£794** for a first attempt and **£397** for a partial resit (seven or fewer failed stations), you get three attempts total with at least ten days between sittings, and a third failure means restarting the whole NMC application after a six-month wait. Book your slot the moment you're cleared to sit it — with a third of the national testing capacity gone, waiting lists at the remaining three centres are the real 2026 constraint, not the exam content.

## Germany splits the language bar in two

Foreign nurses don't need C1 German on day one, whatever some forums claim — **B1** is generally enough to start as a nursing assistant or trainee under a recognition-partnership arrangement (in place since March 2024 for non-EU nurses), with **B2** required for full registered-nurse licensure (Anerkennung) typically within about 18 months of arrival. Going from B1 to B2 commonly takes another three to six months of intensive study, longer if you're doing it part-time around a job. Pay reflects the tier: nursing assistants at B1 level commonly earn around **€2,000 a month**, while fully licensed, B2-qualified registered nurses move up to roughly **€2,800 a month** — a real jump, but one that requires actually clearing the B2 bar and the Anerkennung paperwork, not just showing up with a foreign diploma.

## Australia's AHPRA route runs on its own clock

The Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency assesses international nursing and medical qualifications against Australian competency standards before you can register, and depending on your training country this can mean anything from a straightforward document-based assessment to a full bridging program or supervised practice period. Unlike the UK's single centralized OSCE bottleneck, AHPRA's process varies enough by source country and profession that a blanket timeline is misleading — check your specific qualification's assessment pathway directly on AHPRA's international practitioners portal rather than assuming a colleague's experience from a different country maps onto yours.

## What the salary numbers don't include

Headline salary ranges you'll see quoted for UK, German, and Australian nursing roles are gross figures before the actual cost of getting registered: exam fees, English or German test fees, document translation and authentication, potential bridging coursework, and months of reduced or no income while you clear the licensing steps. None of that shows up in a "GBP 28,000-40,000" recruiter range, and it's the real reason overseas licensing takes people a year or more from decision to first paycheck rather than the few months a sponsorship offer letter implies.

## Recognition of prior experience isn't automatic anywhere

Years of clinical experience in your home country count for something in interviews, but none of these three systems shortcut the formal exam or Anerkennung process because of seniority — a twenty-year ICU nurse still sits the same OSCE as a new graduate, and a consultant physician still goes through the relevant national medical council's assessment. Build your timeline around the licensing exam as the fixed point, not around when you'd like to start earning a Western salary.
