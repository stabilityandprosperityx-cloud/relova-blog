Homeschooling legality is one of the most country-dependent decisions an expat family will make, and it's worth researching before you pick a destination rather than after — some countries treat it as a routine parental right, others treat it as functionally illegal, and getting this backwards can mean arriving somewhere only to discover your plan isn't viable.

**Germany** sits at the restrictive end of the spectrum: homeschooling is generally illegal there, rooted in the country's compulsory schooling laws (Schulpflicht), and families who attempt it can face genuine fines or, in more serious enforcement cases, legal action from authorities. This surprises a lot of expat families who assume homeschooling is a settled, universally accepted right the way it functions in much of the English-speaking world — in Germany, it specifically is not, and this needs to factor into any decision to relocate there with homeschooling plans intact.

**Portugal**, by contrast, generally permits homeschooling, though it isn't entirely unregulated — families typically need to register with the relevant educational authority and children are usually required to sit standardized exams at set intervals to demonstrate they're meeting curriculum benchmarks. This "registered but permitted" model is fairly common internationally and strikes a middle ground between Germany's restriction and fully unregulated systems.

The **UK** runs a process most homeschooling families find manageable: if a child is already enrolled in a school, parents typically need to formally deregister them and notify the local authority of their intent to home-educate, after which oversight is generally lighter-touch than in many other countries — there's no mandatory curriculum or standardized testing requirement nationally, though individual local authorities can request to confirm that a "suitable education" is taking place. This makes the UK relatively appealing for expat families who want flexibility without heavy bureaucratic oversight, assuming residency and visa status otherwise support the move.

For families who decide international school is the better fit, or who use it as a fallback while sorting out homeschooling logistics, costs run substantial: **$8,000–25,000 per child per year** is the commonly cited range, with the wide spread reflecting differences between a modest bilingual local-international school and a top-tier British or American curriculum school with extensive facilities and a strong university placement record. Within that, exam costs are a separate line item worth budgeting for regardless of which schooling path you choose — **Cambridge IGCSE** exam fees, for instance, commonly run around **$200 per subject**, and a student sitting eight or nine subjects (a typical IGCSE load) can rack up a meaningful exam bill on top of any tuition, whether they're enrolled in a school or studying independently and sitting exams as a private candidate.

The practical planning sequence that works best is to decide on your destination country's homeschooling legality first, since it can rule out an otherwise-appealing country entirely (Germany being the clearest example), then decide between homeschooling and international school based on cost, your own capacity to teach or manage a curriculum, and how important local social integration is for your children. Families who do choose homeschooling abroad often end up blending it with local community activities, sports clubs, or part-time enrollment in a local school for social contact, rather than running a fully isolated homeschool model — worth considering even in countries where full-time homeschooling is legally straightforward.

One more practical wrinkle worth flagging: even where homeschooling is legal in your destination country, check whether your specific visa category has any dependent-schooling requirements attached to it — some family or dependent visas in certain countries are conditioned on children being enrolled in a recognized school, which can override the general national homeschooling rules for your specific situation. This is exactly the kind of detail that's easy to miss when researching a country's homeschooling laws in the abstract rather than checking against your actual visa category.
