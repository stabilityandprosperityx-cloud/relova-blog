House sitting is a **barter**, not a salary. You look after a home — and often pets — while the owners travel; they give you a place to sleep. Money usually does not change hands. The United States is the awkward exception, where some sits are paid; treat that as a local quirk, not the global model.

Used well, sitting can erase rent for weeks or months. Used badly, it is unpaid labour on a tourist stamp plus a destroyed sofa. This guide covers the mechanism, the platforms, how to get a first review, and the legal and practical checks before you click confirm.

## How the exchange actually works

Owners post a sit: dates, city, pets, chores, house rules. Sitters apply. If both sides agree, you occupy the property for the listed window. Length ranges from a **long weekend** to **several months**. Peak demand still clusters around northern-hemisphere summer and Christmas, when owners leave and everyone else wants Lisbon or the Cotswolds.

You are not a tenant with a lease you can extend. You are a licensee with a checklist: feed the cat, water the tomatoes, take the bins out, do not throw a party. Good sits feel like housesitting for a friend. Bad sits feel like an unpaid kennel with Wi-Fi.

Competition is real. On the largest networks, popular sits cap applications — **TrustedHousesitters** often limits you to **five applications per listing**. Spray-and-pray wastes those slots. A short, specific message that names the dog and the dates beats a generic “I love travel.”

Never treat sitting as covert remote work income. Owners are not your employer. If a listing offers cash in the US, still ask how that sits with your visa and tax residence. Everywhere else, assume **no pay**, and budget food, local transport, and the membership fee as your cost of “free” housing.

## Which platforms to use in 2026

**TrustedHousesitters** is the global default: live since **2010**, **100+** countries, about **280,000** members (owners and sitters) by late **2025**. Annual membership starts around **€119**. That fee is the price of being in the room where most international sits are filled.

Regional boards matter if you want density in one country: **House Sitters UK**, **Aussie House Sitters**, **House Sitters Canada**, **Kiwi House Sitters**, **House Sitters America**. Fees and review cultures differ. A strong regional profile can beat a thin global one for local winter sits.

Pick one global network plus one regional site if you have a target country. Paying for five platforms does not multiply offers; it multiplies passwords. Read cancellation rules: last-minute owner cancellations are more common than sitters admit in public reviews.

## How to land a first sit when you have no reviews

Owners filter for **platform reputation**, not CVs. A first-time sitter with zero reviews loses to a mediocre sitter with eight five-stars. The workaround used by people who actually break in:

Start **in your own city or country**. A weekend sit for a neighbour’s cat is a real review. Repeat once. Then apply abroad.

Write a profile that is boring in the right ways: pet experience with specifics, remote-work hours (so owners know you will be home), emergency contacts, and a video so they can see you are a person. Skip travel-influencer adjectives.

When you apply, answer the listing: breed, medication, garden, parking. Offer a **video call** before anyone confirms. Platforms recommend it because the disasters happen when sitters discover three dogs, a rooster, and a septic tank on night one.

Expect rejection on the first glamorous coastal sits. Those listings hit the five-application cap in hours. Build reviews on ordinary suburban sits, then use them as proof for the places you actually want.

## Legal grey zones — and what to ask before you confirm

In most countries house sitting is **not employment**. There is no wage, so it is usually **not “work”** for visa categories that ban local jobs. That does not make a six-month chain of sits a substitute for a residence permit. If you stay past the tourist clock, you still need a lawful status. Ask the immigration rules of *that* country, not a Facebook comment.

Before you confirm, run a live call covering:

- Pets: diet, meds, vet, who pays emergencies
- House: keys, alarm, neighbours, bins, parking, weak Wi-Fi
- Insurance: whose policy covers you breaking a window
- Exit: cleaning standard, early-return clause if the owner’s flight changes

If the owner refuses a call, walk. The membership fee is cheaper than a sit that strand you with a sick animal and no vet.

House sitting in 2026 is a reputation market with a €119 ticket. Get local reviews first, treat visas as separate from the barter, and do the video call — that is the whole method.
