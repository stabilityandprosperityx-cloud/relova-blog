Remote apartment hunting sounds efficient until you meet local landlord culture. In most markets, a **permanent unfurnished lease without an in-person viewing** is rare or risky. The pattern that works: book **Airbnb / serviced housing for 2–3 months**, land, view units in person, then sign the long lease.

## What you can realistically do remotely

Research neighbourhoods, commute times, and school catchments. Shortlist agencies. Join local Facebook/Telegram housing groups for signal, not for wiring deposits. Schedule video walkthroughs. Gather a document pack: passport, income proof, employer letter, and — where required — guarantor papers. Reserve a **serviced apartment or Boardinghaus** so you arrive with a legal temporary address.

You can also pre-compare energy labels and typical utility ranges so the advertised rent is not the only number in your model.

## What almost always needs your body on site

Final viewings of unfurnished stock, negotiation of inventory lists, and signature rituals that landlords refuse to digitise. Germany is the textbook case: remote permanent leases are nearly impossible; landlords expect a meeting. Use the 2–3 month serviced stay to complete **Anmeldung**. Critical nuance: a signed **Mietvertrag** alone is **not** enough to register — you need the landlord’s **Wohnungsgeberbestätigung**, which you present with your passport at the Bürgeramt to receive the **Meldebescheinigung**.

Spain and France are somewhat more open to remote applications, yet both frequently demand a **garant/guarantor** or several months’ rent prepaid. That is flexibility with strings, not a free remote market.

## France: the distance-application packet

Expect income evidence, prior-landlord references (or a letter confirming on-time payment if you still live abroad), and guarantor ID + income + formal guarantee letter. If you are paid biweekly (common for US employees), French payslips are monthly — explain the cadence explicitly so underwriters do not misread your income as half of reality.

## Fraud controls before any wire

**Never transfer a deposit or first month** before a personal viewing and a signed contract. The classic scam is the “landlord abroad who will mail keys after payment.” US FTC patterns suggest about **1 in 3** remote renters encounter fake listings when searching from afar — not unique to cross-border moves, but proof the attack surface is real.

Minimum hygiene: a **live** video tour (not a pre-recorded clip), reverse-image search on listing photos, and verification that the person collecting money matches the name on the lease entity. Ask for the energy rating and recent utility bills so monthly all-in cost is visible before you commit.

## A sane remote-to-local sequence

1. Reserve 2–3 months temporary housing  
2. Arrive with fintech IBAN and document pack  
3. View 5–10 units in person in week one  
4. Sign only after viewing; register address with the correct landlord certificate  
5. Release the temporary booking once keys and registration clear  

Housing is the relocation step where optimism is most expensive. Pay for temporary certainty; earn the permanent lease with your presence.


Agency vs private landlord trade-offs: agencies cost fees but sometimes accept foreign documents more readily; private landlords can be cheaper and more flexible once trust exists — which usually requires your physical presence. In competitive cities, prepare a one-page renter CV (job, income, references, move-in date) so you can send it within minutes of a viewing.

If you must pay anything before arrival (some serviced apartments), use platforms with buyer protection and never personal wire transfers to individuals. For long-term deposits after viewing, prefer escrow or agency-held deposits where local practice allows. Document every payment with receipts tied to the lease entity name.


Bring or arrange a local mobile number quickly; many landlords and portals reject foreign numbers for viewing confirmations. A cheap local eSIM on landing day is part of the housing toolkit, not a tourist nice-to-have. Screenshot confirmations — chat histories disappear when groups purge listings.


Document decisions as you go: which visa category you chose and why, which temporary address you booked, which school waitlists you joined. Future-you will need that trail when a consulate, landlord, or insurer asks for a timeline. Relocation amnesia is real after the adrenaline fades — write the rationale while it is fresh, in the same folder as your scans.
