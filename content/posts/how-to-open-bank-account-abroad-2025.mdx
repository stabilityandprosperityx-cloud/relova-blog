You do not need EU citizenship to open a bank account in most of the European Union — but you **do** need to survive **AML/KYC**: identity checks plus a credible source-of-funds story. The winners usually open a fintech euro account **before** the flight, then graduate to a local traditional bank once residence paperwork exists.

## Before you move: get a working account in 24–48 hours

Fintech rails such as **Wise**, **Revolut**, and **N26** can open a usable euro account online in **24–48 hours** without a branch visit. That gives you SEPA receipts, landlord transfers, and a place to land salary or client payments while NIE numbers, registrations, and in-person appointments crawl.

Typical starter document set for a non-resident EU account later: passport, proof of address (even a temporary rental or booking), completed **FATCA/CRS** tax-residency forms, and — for a business account — company registration papers. Have PDFs ready in both English and the destination language when possible.

**United States caveat for inbound planners:** traditional US banks almost always want a verifiable US address, which blocks many non-residents. Digital multi-currency accounts close that gap when you need dollar rails alongside euros.

## Country notes that change your sequence

### Spain — NIE first for most high-street banks

Spanish retail banks usually want an **NIE** (*Número de Identificación de Extranjero*) before they open a full account. You can start the NIE at a Spanish consulate abroad or via an *oficina de extranjeros* in Spain (form **EX-15**, passport, fee). Expect **2–6 weeks**. Meanwhile, **N26/Revolut/Wise** bridge rent and deposits so you are not stuck cash-only while the NIE prints.

### France — the legal backstop if a bank says no

Under **Article L. 312-1**, any resident of France with valid ID and proof of address has a right to a basic bank account. If a bank refuses, it must issue a free written refusal letter — that letter triggers the *droit au compte* procedure through the **Banque de France**. Residents should use that path; tourists without residence should not expect the same guarantee.

### UAE — residency still gates real current accounts

Non-residents are mostly steered to savings products. A full current account almost always needs UAE residency, an in-person branch visit, and often a minimum balance around **AED 3,000–10,000**. Online-only onboarding for outsiders is uncommon — plan a branch day after your residence visa, not before.

## After you arrive: graduate from fintech to local

Week one: keep the fintech account funded and linked to your phone number that still receives SMS. Book the NIE / registration / residence appointments that unlock traditional banks. Bring the same KYC pack plus your lease and visa stamp.

Week two to six: open the local account for payroll, utilities, and anything that rejects fintech IBANs. Keep the fintech account open for FX and cross-border clients — closing it the day your local IBAN arrives is a common unforced error.

Business founders should separate personal and company accounts early; mixing client wires into a tourist-era Revolut balance creates compliance headaches later when you apply for residence renewal.

## Practical rule that saves the first month

Successful movers open **Wise/Revolut (or similar) at home**, land with a live SEPA account on day one, then treat the traditional bank as a second milestone tied to local ID documents — NIE in Spain, French residence proof, UAE Emirates ID, and so on. Banking is a sequence, not a single appointment. Plan the sequence, and landlords stop asking you to “just pay cash until the account works.”


Source-of-funds questions are not personal insults — they are how banks keep correspondent relationships. Be ready to show recent pay slips, client contracts, or sale-of-property paperwork that matches the money you are about to receive. Inconsistencies between your visa story (“remote employee of a US company”) and your first wire (“cash deposit from a friend”) create freezes that take weeks to unwind.

If a landlord insists on a “local IBAN only,” ask whether Wise/Revolut local account details satisfy that rule in your city; many do for SEPA, some still do not for certain utilities. Keep screenshots of account details and a paper IBAN letter in your relocation folder. Banking downtime on week one is usually a documentation gap, not a conspiracy against foreigners.
