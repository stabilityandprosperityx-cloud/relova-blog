**Irish citizenship by descent 2026** turns on one administrative chain: who was born on the island of Ireland, who already held Irish citizenship abroad, and whether each generation was entered in the **Foreign Births Register (FBR)** *before* the next child was born. Miss that sequencing and the line stops—even if your great-grandparents were Irish. From 2026, FBR applications are handled **centrally in Dublin**, not by embassies.

## The three descent levels

1. **Parent born on the island of Ireland** — you are generally an Irish citizen by birth on the bloodline; **FBR is not required** for that claim.  
2. **Parent is an Irish citizen but was born outside Ireland** — you usually need **FBR registration** before you can treat yourself as Irish for passport purposes.  
3. **Irish-born grandparent** — you claim through FBR as well, registering the foreign-born parent/yourself according to the statutory chain.

Passport applications come **after** the FBR certificate; they are a separate process.

## The registration-chain rule

Citizenship only continues down the line if **each next generation is registered before the following child is born**. If your parent registers in the FBR only now, and you were already born earlier, **you do not inherit through that registration**. Plan FBR filings around family births, not after the fact.

## What great-grandparents cannot do

You generally **cannot** claim directly through a great-grandparent. The workable paths are a **parent** or **grandparent** born in Ireland (or an already-Irish parent born abroad who was correctly registered in time). A great-grandparent line only helps if the intervening parent was already on the FBR **before you were born**.

## FBR process, timelines, and dual citizenship

Since 2026, all FBR applications are processed **centrally in Dublin**; consulates no longer decide them. Expect **9–12 months** in typical guidance (some simpler files finish closer to **six months**). Ireland does **not** require you to renounce other citizenships. For people registered after 1986, citizenship runs from the **date of registration**, not from your birth date—important for voting, EU free-movement timing, and how you explain status to banks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need the Foreign Births Register if my parent was born in Ireland?**

Usually no. A parent born on the island of Ireland is the cleanest path; FBR is for foreign-born links through an Irish citizen parent or Irish-born grandparent.

**Can I claim through a great-grandparent in 2026?**

Not directly. You need a parent or grandparent on a qualifying path—or a parent who was already registered in the FBR before you were born.

**What is the registration-chain rule?**

Each generation must be registered **before** the next child is born. Late registration by a parent does not pull already-born children into citizenship through that act.

**How long does FBR take, and where is it decided?**

From 2026, files are decided **centrally in Dublin**. Plan on **9–12 months**, with some simpler cases nearer six months.

**When does Irish citizenship start after FBR, and can I keep another passport?**

For post-1986 registrations, citizenship starts on the **registration date**, not your birth date. Ireland allows dual citizenship; the Irish passport is a separate application after the FBR certificate.
