Indexes are useful until you confuse **statute** with **Tuesday night**. The **Spartacus Gay Travel Index 2026** and the **ILGA-Europe Rainbow Index 2026** score legal protection, not whether your neighbourhood is liveable. Use them as a filter, then pick a **city** and a **community**, and plan transit through the **60+** jurisdictions that still criminalise same-sex relations.

## What the rankings measure — and what they do not

Spartacus 2026 puts **Iceland** first; **Malta** and **Spain** share second; **Belgium, Canada, Germany, and Portugal** share fourth. ILGA-Europe’s top European tier — **Spain, Belgium, Iceland, Malta, Denmark, Norway, Finland, Portugal, Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, Luxembourg** — is the cluster that combines same-sex marriage, employment and housing non-discrimination, legal gender recognition, and hate-crime rules.

That is the **paper** stack: marriage, discrimination bans, gender-marker rules, criminal law. It is not a measure of church attitudes in a village, of how police behave at 2 a.m., or of whether a landlord in a small town will rent to you. Two countries can share a Rainbow score and feel different in the second city, not the capital.

**Malta** is the concrete example of “strong law plus usable process.” Legal gender change can proceed by **self-declaration** — no medical diagnosis, no surgery, no court. Conversion therapy has been **banned since 2016**. There is also a **Nomad Residence Permit** for remote workers, which matters if your move is a visa problem first and a rights problem second. Even there, read **Gaymalta.com** (or the current local equivalent) for events and businesses rather than assuming Valletta nightlife equals Gozo village life.

Canada’s high Spartacus placement is national law with **provincial texture**. Germany’s is federal law with eastern-western social gaps. Portugal’s marriage-and-adoption frame still meets rural variance. The index got you onto the shortlist. It did not choose your street.

## Countries moving up — and countries moving down

**Poland** is the 2026 headline improvement: from **118** to **59** after political change that improved legal protection for trans people. That is a large jump. It is not an instruction to treat every voivodeship as Amsterdam. Use it as “the legal floor rose”; still check the city you would actually live in.

**The United States** is the headline decline: **35th in 2023** to **50th in 2026**, one of the sharpest drops since the index began in **2012**. Federal headlines hide **state** splits. A move to one US metro is not a move to “America.” If the US is on your list, score the **state** the way you would score a country.

Elsewhere, treat “rainbow marketing” from tourism boards as advertising. Corporate Pride in a Gulf posting does not rewrite the penal code — the same warning this blog has used for years, still true.

## How to choose a destination in practice

1. **Screen out criminalising jurisdictions**, including **transit**. Sixty-plus places still criminalise same-sex relations. A cheap fare with a long layover can be a legal risk, not a travel hack.
2. **Use the 2026 indexes** to build a long list (Iceland, Malta, Spain, the ILGA top tier, Canada).
3. **Drop to city scale.** Look for a living scene: recurring events, clinics that know trans care, lawyers who have done partnership recognition, schools with explicit policies if you have kids.
4. **Watch the trend line.** Poland’s rise and the US drop are 2026 facts; they change risk calculations even if your friends’ 2019 recs have not.
5. **Match the visa.** Malta’s nomad permit is a real product. Canada still has **no** digital-nomad visa. A perfect Rainbow score with no legal stay is a holiday.

Healthcare and documents travel with you. If you need ongoing gender-affirming care, map clinics and prescription continuity *before* you give notice at home — an ILGA score does not refill a medication. If you are married or raising children, check how the destination records foreign marriages and parental rights; the top-tier countries above generally do, but the paperwork still has a queue. For dual citizens, run the criminal-code check on *both* passports’ typical transit hubs, not only the destination.

Legal protection and social ease are related and not identical. Rank the law, then walk the neighbourhood — and never treat an index as a substitute for the criminal code of a country you are only flying through.
