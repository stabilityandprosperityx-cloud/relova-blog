**Setting up in [Lisbon](/blog/lisbon-american-taxes-banking-2026)** has a correct order of operations, and doing the steps out of sequence is the single most common reason newcomers lose weeks they didn't need to lose.

## Get your NIF before you try to do anything else

The Número de Identificação Fiscal (NIF) is Portugal's tax identification number, and almost nothing else works without it — you cannot sign a lease, open a bank account, get a phone contract, or register with most utility providers without one. Non-residents can obtain a NIF through a Portuguese tax office (Finanças) if applying in person, or more commonly before arrival through a fiscal representative service, which typically charges EUR 150–300 a year to act as your registered contact with Portuguese tax authorities until you have a residence address of your own. Getting the NIF sorted before landing, rather than after, removes the single biggest bottleneck in the first two weeks.

## Book your AIMA appointment the day your visa is approved, not after you land

AIMA (Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo), which replaced SEF for residence permit processing, has run sustained appointment backlogs, and expat reports commonly cite waits of two to eight weeks just to get a booking slot, on top of however long the permit itself then takes to process. Because your visa sticker has a limited validity window in which you must complete this in-person appointment, the practical move is to book the AIMA slot as early as the system allows — ideally the same week your visa is stamped — rather than waiting until you have unpacked and settled in. Bring every original document plus copies (passport, proof of address, NIF, health insurance, proof of income) since incomplete files are a common reason appointments get rescheduled rather than completed.

## Opening a bank account is faster with a NIF and an address in hand

Portuguese banks — Millennium bcp, Novobanco, and Activobank among the more foreigner-friendly options — generally require your NIF, passport, and proof of Portuguese address (a signed lease or a utility bill in your name) to open a standard current account. Without a fixed address yet, some banks will open an account against a temporary or hotel address with additional verification, though options narrow and fees can be higher. Once the account is open, MB Way — Portugal's near-universal mobile payment and transfer app, linked directly to your bank account and IBAN — becomes the default way locals and residents split bills, pay small vendors, and send money to each other, and getting set up on it early smooths a surprising amount of daily social friction.

## SIM cards and a temporary flat come before the permanent lease hunt

NOS, Vodafone, and MEO are the three major carriers, and NOS commonly advertises entry-level plans around EUR 15 a month for roughly 20GB of data — enough for most remote-work needs alongside home or coworking wifi. Most newcomers book a furnished short-term flat (a month to six weeks) through Airbnb or a mid-term platform like Flatio or Spotahome for the first stretch, which buys time to view long-term rentals in person rather than signing a year-long lease sight unseen from another country. STCP, [Lisbon](/blog/move-to-lisbon-honest-guide-2026)'s bus network, offers monthly passes around EUR 40 for unlimited travel across the integrated [Lisbon](/blog/lisbon-freelancer-setup-guide-2026) transport zones (bus, metro, and tram), which is generally cheaper than the combined cost of separate metro and bus passes and worth setting up in the first week if you are not immediately buying a car or scooter.

## A realistic first two months costs more than the brochure budget

Between the fiscal representative fee, health insurance required for the visa, the AIMA appointment fee, a security deposit plus first month's rent (commonly one to two months' rent combined as upfront deposit in Lisbon), and basic furnishing for an unfurnished flat if that is the route chosen, first-time movers should budget for meaningfully more than a single month's steady-state cost of living in the first eight weeks. Treating that stretch as a distinct, larger line item — rather than assuming month one looks like month six — avoids the most common cash-flow surprise new residents report.
