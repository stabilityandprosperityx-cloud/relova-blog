Building a genuinely location-independent business is a different project from becoming a location-independent freelancer, even though the two get lumped together constantly in relocation content. A freelancer is selling their own time and skills under their own name, with registration requirements covered in our [freelancing abroad guide](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/freelancing-abroad-self-employed-expat-2026). Running a business — an agency, a SaaS product, anything with its own legal identity, its own payment processing, and eventually its own team — means making deliberate decisions about entity structure, how money actually moves between clients and contractors across borders, and how you manage people you may never meet in person. Getting these choices right early saves a disproportionate amount of pain later, since untangling a badly chosen entity structure after the fact is far more expensive than picking well from the start.

## Choosing where to incorporate

The entity decision usually comes down to a trade-off between cost, credibility, and administrative complexity, and the right answer depends heavily on where your clients and eventual team are based. Estonia's e-Residency program and its OÜ company structure remain a popular default for genuinely distributed digital businesses, with state registration fees around €190 and a distinctive tax feature: corporate tax is deferred until profits are actually distributed as dividends, which can be a real cashflow advantage for a business reinvesting heavily in its early years. A UK LTD is strikingly cheap to set up — registration runs around £12 — and carries a level of international credibility and banking ease that punches well above its cost, making it a common choice for agencies whose clients skew toward the US, UK, or EU and value a recognizable jurisdiction. A Wyoming LLC is the go-to for lean, US-facing digital agencies, with annual costs running near $100 and a straightforward pass-through tax structure, though non-US founders should get specific advice on how a US LLC interacts with their personal tax residency before assuming it is the cheap, simple option it looks like on paper. UAE free zones are the pick for founders prioritizing zero corporate tax on qualifying income and no personal income tax, with typical free-zone licensing starting around AED 12,500 a year — but the setup and compliance overhead is genuinely higher than the other three options, and it usually only makes sense once revenue has reached a level where the tax savings clearly outweigh the added administrative cost.

## Payment processing across borders

Payment rails are where a lot of otherwise well-planned location-independent businesses hit an unexpected wall, because processor requirements are often tied to the entity's country of incorporation in ways that are not obvious until you try to onboard. Stripe supports businesses incorporated in roughly forty-seven countries, making it the default choice for most founders — but coverage is not universal, and it is worth confirming your specific entity jurisdiction is supported before committing to an incorporation choice around it, rather than assuming Stripe support afterward. Mercury, a popular US-focused business banking option, generally requires a US-registered entity, which rules it out for founders going the Estonian, UK, or UAE route unless they also maintain a US subsidiary. Airwallex has built out particularly strong coverage for founders who need to receive and pay out across many currency corridors simultaneously — genuinely useful if your clients or contractors are spread across several continents rather than concentrated in one region, since currency conversion fees and payout speed vary meaningfully between providers at that scale.

## Managing a distributed team and contractors

Once the business has enough revenue to bring on help, the entity choice starts to matter for a second reason: how you pay and manage people across borders. Independent contractor agreements are the simplest route for early hires, but contractor misclassification risk varies significantly by country — some jurisdictions apply strict tests for what counts as genuine self-employment versus disguised employment, and getting this wrong can create real liability. Employer-of-record services have become the standard workaround for hiring genuine employees in countries where you have no local entity, handling local payroll, tax withholding, and compliance on your behalf for a per-employee fee — worth the cost once you are hiring people who need employee-level benefits and protections rather than a straightforward contractor relationship.

## Realistic revenue timelines

Expectations matter here as much as the mechanics. SaaS products typically need six to eighteen months to reach meaningful monthly recurring revenue, reflecting the build-test-iterate cycle most software products go through before finding real product-market fit. Service-based agencies, by contrast, can often reach positive cashflow in one to three months, since they are selling existing skills and capacity rather than waiting on a product to mature — a meaningful difference if you are planning your personal runway around when the business needs to become self-sustaining. As a rough visa-planning benchmark, most digital nomad and remote-work visa programs are comfortably cleared by $3,000–5,000 in monthly net income, a useful target to keep in view as you plan both the business's growth and your own relocation timeline.
