**Staying connected to home** while living abroad is less about sentiment and more about a handful of administrative deadlines that quietly lapse if nobody is watching them from the other side of a border.

## US filers: the $10,000 FBAR line is an aggregate, not a per-account limit

Any US person with signature authority over foreign financial accounts — bank, brokerage, or certain pension accounts — whose combined balances exceed $10,000 at any point during the calendar year must file an FBAR (FinCEN Form 114), even if no single account ever crosses that figure on its own. This is a reporting requirement, not a tax, and it is separate from whatever income tax is owed on the accounts — but the penalties for non-willful failure to file start in the thousands of dollars per violation, which makes it one of the more consequential paperwork items expats overlook simply because $10,000 in combined foreign balances is an easy threshold to cross without noticing, especially once a local salary account, a savings account, and a pension fund are all counted together.

## UK expats can buy back pension years even after leaving the country

British nationals living abroad can make voluntary Class 2 or Class 3 National Insurance contributions to protect or top up their UK State Pension record, and Class 2 rates in particular are considerably cheaper than the domestic Class 3 rate if you qualify (generally by having worked in the UK immediately before leaving and being employed or self-employed abroad). Gaps in a NI record directly reduce the eventual State Pension amount, and HMRC allows backdated voluntary contributions for past gap years up to a limit that has periodically been extended — worth checking current HMRC guidance directly, since the backdating window has changed more than once in recent years and acting before a deadline closes can meaningfully change the payout decades later.

## Absentee voting is not a one-time registration

US citizens abroad register through the Federal Post Card Application (FPCA), but most states require re-registering or reaffirming that registration annually or with each federal election cycle rather than treating it as a permanent, set-and-forget status — a lapsed registration is one of the most common reasons expats miss a ballot despite having voted from abroad before. UK citizens who have been abroad long enough face a related issue in reverse: the 15-year rule that historically cut off overseas voting rights was repealed under the Elections Act, extending eligibility, but registration still needs periodic renewal through the UK's overseas elector system rather than lasting indefinitely from a single sign-up.

## A home-country mailing address solves more problems than it sounds like it should

Mail forwarding and scanning services — plans commonly starting around $15 a month for a real (non-PO-box) US address that scans incoming mail and forwards physical items on request — solve a specific problem: banks, the IRS, state DMV renewals, and some voter registration systems still expect a domestic mailing address on file, and losing that address entirely can quietly break re-verification processes for financial accounts opened before you left. Keeping one active, even a low-cost virtual one, avoids a cascade of account-flagging problems that are considerably more annoying to fix retroactively than to prevent.

## Driving licenses do not travel as far as you'd expect

A home-country driving license's validity abroad depends entirely on the specific pairing of issuing country and host country rules, not on any general international standard — some countries honor a foreign license for a fixed tourist window (often 90 days to a year) before requiring a local conversion or retest, while others require an International Driving Permit alongside the original license from day one. Because this varies so much by country pair, it is worth checking the host country's specific rule before driving on a foreign license past any grace period, since the consequence of getting it wrong is frequently an invalidated car insurance claim rather than just a traffic fine.
