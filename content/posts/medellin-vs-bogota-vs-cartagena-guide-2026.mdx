**Medellin vs Bogota vs Cartagena 2026** is climate first, then cost. Medellín’s “eternal spring” sits about **1,500–2,300 m** with **18–28°C** year-round and the strongest expat infrastructure of the three. Bogotá at **2,600 m** runs **7–18°C**, often cloudy and wet—the top reason new arrivals bounce in month one, even though it wins for business, museums, and serious Spanish study. Cartagena is sea-level Caribbean heat: **28–35°C** with humidity that makes A/C mandatory. ## Climate and who leaves early

Medellín’s mild range suits first-time movers. Bogotá’s cool altitude shocks people who expected “tropical Colombia.” Cartagena works for short visits; full-time heat and humidity raise bills and fatigue. Use Cartagena for **2–3 day** trips more than as a first long base.

## Cost of living and electricity

Medellín is often **20–30% cheaper** than Cartagena overall; a Laureles one-bed can run **30–40%** under Getsemaní. Without A/C, Medellín power is often **COP 80,000–150,000/month**; Cartagena with A/C can hit **COP 200,000–500,000+**. El Poblado has surged since 2020—furnished two-beds **$1,200–2,200/month**, mid-U.S.-city territory—while Laureles stays cheaper and still solid. Bogotá typically costs **10–25% more** than Medellín at a comparable lifestyle.

## Safety, healthcare, and internet

Medellín’s homicide rate fell from **381 per 100,000 in 1991** to about **13** today—a historic turnaround. Bogotá safety is neighborhood-dependent: Chapinero Alto, Usaquén, and Zona Rosa are stronger; be more careful south and in parts of the center. Cartagena’s tourist zones see more pickpocketing than headline violence. Healthcare: Medellín is a medical-tourism hub (Pablo Tobón Uribe, Las Américas); serious Cartagena cases often transfer to Medellín or Bogotá. Fiber in Bogotá and Medellín expat districts is comparable; Cartagena is usable but a step behind.
