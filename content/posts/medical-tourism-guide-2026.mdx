The global medical-tourism market is estimated around **$84.5 billion** in 2026, with other methodologies stretching as far as **$109–312 billion**. About **1.4 million Americans** leave the country for care each year and spend on the order of **$8.5 billion**. Typical savings versus US, UK, or Western European list prices sit in the **40–80%** band, and higher in some cardiac and dental categories. Cheaper is a price. It is not automatically a quality insult — and it is not automatically safe.

This is not a monthly cost-of-living article. It is a procedure, a country, a clinic standard, and a companion.

## Why the same surgery can cost a fraction — without meaning “worse”

US and UK hospital prices bundle labour, insurance friction, liability, and a domestic market that will pay. High-volume hospitals in Turkey, Thailand, India, and Mexico run the same named procedures on international protocols at a different cost base. English-speaking surgical teams in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Bangkok, Istanbul, and Monterrey exist because this is an export industry, not a back-alley rumour.

The quality filter is not the country’s GDP. It is **which hospital** and **which surgeon**. A Joint Commission International (**JCI**) accreditation is the practical international floor. “A friend saved 70%” is not a credential. Volume destinations got that way because they specialise, not because they are uniformly excellent.

## Which country for which procedure

**Turkey** — about **2 million** medical travellers a year, often the volume leader: dentistry, hair transplant, cosmetic surgery. **LASIK** quotes around **$1,200** versus about **$2,500** in the US.

**Thailand** — on the order of **2.5 million** including expat and tourist care: cosmetic surgery and wellness, plus a serious hospital system in Bangkok. A **hip replacement** around **$12,000** versus about **$40,000** in the US (**~70%** off).

**Mexico** — about **1.3 million**: dentistry and bariatric surgery, popular because many Americans can **drive in the morning and drive home the same day** for some dental work. **Gastric bypass** commonly **$6,000–8,500** versus about **$25,000** in the US. Implants can show **up to 80%** savings versus US list.

**India** — cardiac surgery, orthopaedics, oncology: the high-ticket, high-complexity cases. **Coronary bypass** often **$7,900–10,000** versus **$123,000–130,000** in the US (savings cited up to **~92%**). The English-speaking hubs are **Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad**.

**South Korea** — cosmetics and dermatology. **Costa Rica** — dentistry packaged with a recovery that looks like eco-tourism. Match the specialty, not the Instagram beach.

## The 2026 price gaps people actually quote

Across categories, **dental implants** average about **$1,500** abroad versus about **$4,500** in the US (**~67%**). Hip, bypass, bariatric, and LASIK numbers above are planning figures, not invoices. Currency, implant brand, nights in ICU, and whether the quote is cash-package or à-la-carte will move them.

Add **flights, a hotel for recovery, a companion’s ticket, and time off work**. Then add a **15–20% buffer** on the quoted procedure price for the thing that was not in the brochure: an extra night, a different implant, a complication, a PCR test nobody mentioned. People who budget the surgery to the dollar are the people who put a revision on a credit card.

## How to not gamble with anaesthesia in another country

Choose **JCI-accredited** (or equivalent national accreditation you have actually checked) clinics. For anything with **general anaesthesia**, a **hospital overnight**, or **more than 48 hours of recovery**, take a **companion**. Postoperative cognitive fog, language barriers at discharge, and the logistics of getting a barely mobile patient through an airport are how solo trips become emergencies. “I travel alone for work” is not a clinical argument.

Get the operative report, implant stickers, and a written aftercare plan you can hand to a doctor at home. Confirm what happens if you need a revision — in that country and in yours. Insurance at home may refuse a complication from elective care abroad; know that before you fly.

Medical tourism in 2026 is a **$84.5 billion** market with **40–80%+** discounts that are real at JCI hospitals and dangerous at the cheapest WhatsApp quote. Pick the country for the procedure, fly with a person, and keep 15–20% in reserve. The savings only count if you get home intact.
