“Move abroad with no money” is a slogan. “Move abroad with a **work-for-keep roof**, an **entry-level paid job**, or a **working-holiday visa**” is a plan. People do leave with around **$6,000** — forum reports are consistent on that number — if they pick one of those three tracks instead of a silent Airbnb and a hope. Canada-style proof-of-funds tables do not apply here; this is the **low-capital** stack.

## Track 1: Work exchange (housing and food, not a wage)

**WWOOF** (World Wide Opportunities on Organic Farms) is the largest organic-farm exchange: **130 countries**, **12,000+** farms, **100,000+** participants. High-volume destinations include **Portugal, France, Italy, Costa Rica, Australia, New Zealand**. **Worldpackers** and **HelpX** run a similar barter for eco-villages, hostels, and community projects.

You work set hours; you get a bed and usually meals. **Cash typically does not change hands**, which is why people call it “not a job.” Immigration officers may still see labour. Many volunteers enter on **tourist** stamps. That can be lawful for a short stay and unlawful if you chain months. Check the **specific country**, not a WWOOF FAQ written for another jurisdiction.

This track minimises rent. It does not pay for flights, insurance, or the next sit. Budget the $6,000 (or whatever you have) for **tickets, insurance, a buffer between hosts**, and the membership fees — not for “zero spend.”

Insurance is the line work-exchange blogs skip. A farm injury or a missed connection between hosts is how a $6,000 plan becomes a family emergency call. Buy travel medical that matches physical work, and keep a refundable buffer equal to a flight home. WWOOF membership is not that buffer.

Start with one confirmed host before you fly, plus a backup. Farm work is physical; hostel reception is social. Pick the one you can sustain.

## Track 2: Teach English (the paid floor)

If you need **wages**, English teaching is still the most repeatable low-capital job abroad. **EPIK** (South Korea) and **JET** (Japan) often **pay the flight** and **subsidise or include housing**. Typical ESL pay across **Korea, China, Thailand, Spain** sits around **$1,100–3,000 a month** depending on hours, city, and whether housing is included.

Baseline: a **TEFL** certificate (courses from about **£200**) and, for the better public programmes, usually a **bachelor’s degree** (not always mandatory in the private mill market). Apply on the programme calendar, not on a whim in August.

This is a **job**. Visas are work visas. Do not confuse it with work exchange. The capital you need is enough to reach the interview, fly if the school does not, and survive until first payday — which is why $6,000 shows up as a workable number when housing is included.

Private language mills can hire faster and pay worse. Read the contract for housing deductions, weekend hours, and whether the flight is reimbursement after 12 months. EPIK and JET are slower and more documented; that is the trade. A £200 TEFL is the ticket into the conversation, not a guarantee of a Seoul apartment.

## Track 3: Working holiday, au pair, cruise, hostel jobs

**Working-holiday visas** (usually **18–30/35**) are the cleanest **legal** short-work route **without a prior job offer**. They are age-gated and nationality-gated. They generally **do not** demand the large settlement piles of skilled-migration programmes. Use them as a year of paid work plus travel, not as a secret PR.

Other live-in formats:

- **Au pair** — childcare for room, board, and pocket money
- **Cruise contracts** — cabin, food, and movement between countries included; the job is the point
- **Hostel work** — similar barter to HelpX, often in cities

Stack a working holiday with hostel shifts, or EPIK with a later WWOOF summer — sequentially, with the right stamp each time.

The $6,000 example works when **housing is solved by the track** (farm, school apartment, ship, host family) and the cash is **logistics plus buffer**. It fails when you spend it on a month of capital-city Airbnb “to look for something.”

Pick **one** track, confirm the visa theory for that country, then buy the ticket. Work exchange, EPIK/JET-style teaching, or a working-holiday/au pair/cruise contract — those are the 2026 low-capital doors. Motivation is not a fourth door.
