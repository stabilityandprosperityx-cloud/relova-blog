The [Algarve](/blog/lisbon-vs-porto-vs-algarve-guide-2026) is Portugal's south coast, and it draws a genuinely different crowd than Lisbon or Porto — retirees, golfers, and remote workers chasing 300-plus days of sun a year rather than the startup and café culture that defines the two big cities. The region's income anchors follow the same national visa framework as the rest of Portugal: the **D7 visa**, aimed at people with passive or pension income, currently uses a baseline around **EUR 870 to 920 a month** tied to the Portuguese minimum wage, which rises slightly each year, while the **D8 digital nomad visa**, built for people earning active remote income, sits at roughly four times that figure — commonly cited around **EUR 3,480 to 3,680 a month**. Because both thresholds move with Portugal's annual minimum-wage adjustment, always confirm the exact current number with AIMA or a licensed immigration lawyer before filing rather than trusting a fixed figure from any single source, including this one.

## Which visa route actually fits the Algarve lifestyle

The D7 is by far the more common route into the Algarve specifically, because so much of the region's foreign population is retirees living on pensions, savings, or investment income rather than active freelance work — exactly the profile the D7 was designed around. The D8 works for remote employees and freelancers who want to base themselves on the coast while keeping a job or client base elsewhere, though the income bar is meaningfully higher and demands more documentation of ongoing active income rather than a lump-sum asset test. Whichever route you choose, apostilles and certified Portuguese translations of your core documents — birth certificate, marriage certificate if relevant, criminal record check — are the step that most reliably delays applications, so it's worth starting that paperwork chain well before you book a consulate appointment or AIMA slot.

## Faro, Lagos, Tavira, and where people actually settle

The Algarve isn't one town — it's a string of very different ones stretched along roughly 150 kilometers of coastline, and the right base depends heavily on what you're looking for. **Faro**, the regional capital, is the most practical choice for anyone who wants an international airport a short drive away, reasonably priced 1BR rents commonly around **EUR 600 to 900 a month**, and a functioning year-round town rather than a seasonal resort. **Lagos**, further west, has the strongest concentration of a genuinely international retiree and remote-worker community, with a walkable historic center, a real English-speaking social scene, and beach access that draws comparisons to more expensive parts of Spain — rents there tend to run toward the upper end of the region's **EUR 700 to 1,100** average for a one-bedroom. **Tavira**, to the east near the Spanish border, offers a quieter, more traditionally Portuguese alternative with fewer tourists and correspondingly lower prices, appealing to people who found Lagos too polished or too dominated by other foreigners.

## An established retiree community, and what that means day to day

Across the region, the retiree and remote-worker population has grown large enough to support English-language medical clinics, international social clubs, and golf communities that rival anything in southern Spain, while the area also averages more than 300 sunny days a year and offers direct flights from Faro to over 30 European cities in season — a real advantage for anyone planning to travel back and forth regularly. That density of foreign residents is a double-edged feature depending on what you want: it means genuine community, easier social integration, and services built with English speakers in mind, but it also means parts of the Algarve, particularly around Lagos and the golf-course developments near Vilamoura, feel noticeably less "authentically Portuguese" than smaller towns further inland or east toward Tavira. Golf itself is a real draw here, with dozens of championship-level courses concentrated in a relatively compact stretch of coastline, drawing a specific retiree demographic that overlaps heavily with the D7 visa applicant profile. Fiber internet is solid in the main towns, though it thins out noticeably once you move into the rural interior or smaller villages between the coastal towns, which is worth checking directly with a property before committing if reliable connectivity is essential to your work.
