Almaty's pitch is unusually blunt for Central Asia: citizens of over **60 countries**, including the US, UK, EU, and most of the Gulf, can enter **visa-free for up to 30 days**, and Kazakhstan layered a genuinely no-income-requirement remote-work track on top of that in the **B9-1 Digital Nomad Residency** pilot, run through **Astana Hub**. The catch is in the fine print most listicles skip: B9-1 is explicitly scoped to IT professionals — software developers, designers, DevOps engineers, cybersecurity specialists — and was launched as a **pilot program originally slated to run through the end of 2025**. Whether it has been formally extended into 2026 is exactly the kind of detail that changes month to month with a pilot scheme; confirm current status directly with Astana Hub before you build a relocation plan around it, because "no income requirement" for a program built to sunset is not the same guarantee as a standing law. Processing, when the program is live, runs roughly **22 business days** end to end: five for document verification and interview, ten for IT-specialization accreditation, seven more for the invitation letter.

## What the B9-1 route doesn't cover

There's no publicly disclosed application fee baked into most guides, which itself is a signal to call the consulate rather than assume it's free. The initial grant is a **one-year single-entry e-visa** that converts to a paper visa with multiple entry once you're in-country, and Kazakhstan frames the longer game as a path toward **residence permits extending up to ten years** for people who stay engaged with the program. Outside the pilot, Kazakhstan's standard **10% flat personal income tax** applies to residents, and that rate is worth knowing even if your remote-work status is ambiguous — tax residency in Kazakhstan generally triggers at 183 days present in a 12-month period, the same threshold most countries use, and it operates independently of your immigration status.

## Medeu rents versus the "cheap Central Asia" myth

Medeu, the upscale foothill district that gives Almaty its mountain-backdrop reputation, runs **roughly $440–775 a month** for a one-bedroom — genuinely cheaper than equivalent Western European capitals but not the rock-bottom figure some digital nomad forums imply. A realistic all-in nomad budget, including coworking, groceries, and casual dining, sits closer to **$800–1,500 a month** once you're not living like a backpacker. Almaty's appeal is less about rock-bottom prices and more about getting mountain access (Shymbulak ski resort is a 20-minute drive from the city center), a genuinely walkable historic core, and a currency — the tenge — that's stayed relatively soft against the dollar, stretching a foreign income further than the sticker prices suggest at first glance.

## Winter air quality and the practical trade-off

The detail that catches first-time arrivals off guard isn't the visa — it's Almaty's winter air quality, which regularly ranks among the worst of any major city globally during the November–February heating season, driven by coal-fired heating in a basin that traps particulate matter against the mountains. If respiratory health is a factor, budget for a serious air purifier and plan any lease around floor level and building age, not just proximity to Medeu's ski lifts.

## Language and the practical daily-life gap

Russian remains the dominant language of daily business and administration in Almaty despite Kazakh being the sole state language, and English fluency, while growing in the startup and coworking scene clustered around Astana Hub-affiliated spaces, is far from universal outside those pockets — grocery shopping, utility setup, and most landlord interactions will go more smoothly with at least basic Russian or a reliable translation app on hand. This is a genuinely different linguistic environment from the English-forward nomad hubs of Southeast Asia or the Balkans, and worth factoring into any decision that assumes "digital nomad hub" implies English-language ease everywhere.

Almaty in 2026 is a **visa-free-for-30-days, IT-scoped nomad pilot, $440–775-for-Medeu** proposition — genuinely appealing if your profession fits the B9-1 box and you've confirmed it's still running, less compelling as a generic "cheap Central Asia" default.
