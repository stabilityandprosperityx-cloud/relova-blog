Jordan's residency-by-real-estate route was revised in **July 2026**, and the new criteria split by both location and seller type in a way older guides don't reflect. Buying property **outside Amman** now qualifies at **JOD 150,000 (roughly $212,000)**. Inside Amman itself, the threshold depends on who you're buying from: **JOD 200,000 (~$282,000)** through a licensed developer, or **JOD 300,000 (~$423,000)** if the purchase is from a private seller. Whichever tier you use, the property must be **held, unmortgaged and unsold, for five years**, in exchange for a **five-year renewable residence permit** — a meaningfully longer commitment than most Gulf-adjacent golden-visa programs, which typically only require a shorter holding period. For anyone not buying property, employer-sponsored work permits remain the standard route into Jordan, and NGO- or embassy-adjacent hiring, common in Amman given the concentration of international organizations, tends to run its own separate hardship-allowance and housing-stipend conventions that differ meaningfully from corporate-sector packages — worth clarifying explicitly in an offer letter rather than assuming parity with a private-sector role.

## Income tax bands and the ASEZA outlier

Resident income tax in Jordan is progressive, starting at **5% on income between JOD 5,001 and 10,000** and climbing to a top marginal rate of **25% above JOD 25,000**, with an additional **1% national contribution tax** and **1% solidarity surcharge** layered on top for most brackets. Non-resident expats are taxed only on **Jordan-sourced income** — foreign employment income drawn while on a tourist-class stay sits in genuinely gray territory that's worth a local advisor's read before assuming it's untouched. One structural outlier worth knowing: **Aqaba**, Jordan's Red Sea port city administered under the **Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority (ASEZA)**, offers **0% personal income tax** for zone residents — a real option for anyone whose work doesn't require being in Amman specifically. VAT nationally sits at a flat **16%** on most goods and services.

## Amman rent by district, and the Aqaba alternative

**West Amman**, the more affluent, diplomat- and NGO-heavy side of the city, runs **$425–775 a month** for a furnished one-bedroom and **$565–1,060** for a two-bedroom. **East and Central Amman** is considerably cheaper, at **$210–395** for a one-bedroom, reflecting older building stock and a more local, less internationally-oriented character. Outside the capital, **Aqaba** one-bedrooms run **$280–565**, and **Irbid**, Jordan's northern university city, drops to **$170–355** — genuinely the country's budget option if proximity to Amman isn't a requirement. A comfortable single-person monthly budget in West Amman, all-in with groceries, transport, and utilities, lands around **$1,200–2,000**. Leases typically run one year with **2–3 months' deposit** up front, a heavier initial cash requirement than many first-time movers budget for.

## Regional context belongs in the plan, not just the pitch

Jordan's stability relative to its neighbors is a genuine selling point, and weekend access to Petra (roughly three hours south) and the Dead Sea (under an hour from Amman) is a real quality-of-life draw. But regional tension in the broader Levant is not background noise for a family risk register — travel advisories and insurance terms for the wider region shift with the news cycle in ways that don't necessarily track daily life in Amman itself but do affect flight availability, insurance premiums, and family comfort with a longer-term stay.

## Utilities, water, and a detail worth planning around

Amman's water supply runs on a scheduled rotation in many neighborhoods rather than continuous 24-hour service — most residential buildings, including newer West Amman apartments, rely on rooftop storage tanks that are refilled on a municipal schedule, and understanding your specific building's tank capacity and refill day is a genuinely practical first-week task, not a minor curiosity. Electricity and gas heating costs also swing seasonally more than newcomers expect, given Amman's elevation brings genuinely cold winters despite the desert-adjacent regional reputation.

Amman in 2026 is a **JOD 150,000-to-300,000 property route, 5%-to-25% progressive tax (0% in Aqaba), $170–775-for-a-one-bed** market. Get the Amman-versus-outside-Amman property tier right before you make an offer — a $70,000 gap sits between the cheapest and most expensive qualifying purchase.
