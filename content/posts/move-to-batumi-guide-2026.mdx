## Visa-free stays and the Turkey border run

U.S., UK, EU, and many other passports get **365 days** nationwide. The **Sarpi** border to Turkey is about **20 minutes** by taxi—popular for visa-run resets, though repeated runs can draw questions. English is thinner than in Tbilisi, especially among older residents; younger people usually manage better.

## Seasonal rents and when to arrive

Low-season one-beds often **$300–500/month**; **June–September** can jump **50–100%** toward **$600–900** as landlords switch to nightly Airbnb rates. Arrive in **October–November** when prices drop and landlords prefer annual leases. Comfortable solo budgets average **$900–1,400/month**; lean living can start near **$800**. Popular areas: Old Town (quieter, historic) and Old/New Boulevard (seafront, louder in summer).

## Climate, English, and coworking

Humid subtropical weather runs **5–10°C warmer** than Tbilisi year-round—up to about **+38°C** in summer, mild and rainy in winter. Many expats split seasons: **autumn–spring in Batumi, summer in Tbilisi** to dodge tourist crush and capital cold. Coworking is smaller than Tbilisi’s scene but growing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Batumi’s visa different from Tbilisi’s?**

No—same national **365-day** visa-free rules for qualifying passports.

**When should I look for a long lease?**

**October–November**, after summer Airbnb demand cools and landlords want annual tenants.

**How much do summer rents jump?**

Often **50–100%**—low-season **$300–500** one-beds can approach **$600–900**.

**Is Batumi cheaper than Tbilisi?**

Typically **10–15%**, especially housing and food—before summer spikes.

**Can I do a quick visa run?**

Sarpi (Turkey) is about **20 minutes** by taxi, but do not treat endless resets as risk-free.
