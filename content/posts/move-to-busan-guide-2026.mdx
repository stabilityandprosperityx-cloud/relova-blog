South Korea's **F-1-D "[workation](/blog/south-korea-digital-nomad-visa-guide-2026)" visa** — the country's digital nomad product — sets its income bar as a multiple of national income data rather than a flat number, which means the actual figure shifts with the underlying statistic. The standard requirement is roughly **twice the previous year's GNI per capita**, landing around **₩88–105 million annually (roughly USD 66,000–76,000)**. There is a meaningful carve-out worth knowing about if you qualify for it: applicants aged **18–34** based outside greater [Seoul](/blog/seoul-neighborhoods-expat-guide-2026) — Busan included — may clear the bar at the lower **one-times-GNI rate**, closer to **USD 37,000**. The visa grants an initial **one-year** stay with renewal options up to a **three-year maximum** total, and mandates private health insurance with a minimum **₩100 million (~USD 75,000)** in coverage for treatment and repatriation. Because GNI per capita is a figure that updates with each year's national statistics, treat any specific won amount as this year's snapshot rather than a fixed number to plan five years around.

## Haeundae surf money versus Gwangalli bridge views

Busan's two flagship beach neighborhoods price differently for reasons that map directly onto lifestyle. **Haeundae** — Korea's most internationally known beach, backed by high-rise towers, a dense tourist and hospitality economy, and the closest thing Busan has to a foreign-resident hub — runs **KRW 700,000–1,200,000** for a typical one-bedroom. **Gwangalli**, a short distance down the coast and famous for its nightly Gwangan Bridge light display, tends to run slightly cheaper at **KRW 600,000–1,000,000** while still delivering genuine beach-adjacent living with a marginally more local, less tourist-saturated feel. Both undercut equivalent Seoul neighborhoods substantially, which is the core financial case for choosing Busan over the capital if your work does not specifically require Seoul.

## The F-1-D's tax election is worth understanding before you file

Once you cross **183 days** of physical presence in a given year, Korean tax residency generally follows, which would normally mean worldwide income taxed at Korea's progressive rates (roughly **6% to 45%**). F-1-D holders, however, may be able to elect a **flat 19% rate (20.9% including the local surtax)** on foreign-sourced employment income instead of the progressive schedule — a potentially significant saving for higher earners, though eligibility and application specifics are exactly the kind of thing that should go through a Korea-qualified tax adviser rather than a blog post, since the election mechanics and deadlines carry real consequences if filed incorrectly.

## Korean study is not optional the way it can feel elsewhere in Asia

Busan trades Seoul's density and intensity for a genuinely different pace — surf mornings at Songjeong or Haeundae before work are a real, common routine here in a way they simply aren't in Seoul — but that shouldn't be mistaken for an English-first environment. Long-term comfort in Busan, more than in Seoul's more internationally staffed corporate and expat-dense districts, tends to require real Korean study: banking, lease negotiations, and most local-market interactions assume Korean fluency or a dedicated translator, and Busan's foreign-resident population, while real and growing, is smaller and less English-service-oriented than Seoul's.

## Seomyeon, KTX geography, and the Fukuoka ferry option

**Seomyeon**, Busan's central shopping and transit hub, offers a third pricing tier at **KRW 550,000–900,000**, trading beach proximity for the city's best subway connectivity and shopping density — a sensible base if your priority is transit access over ocean views. Busan's KTX high-speed rail connection to Seoul runs roughly **two and a half hours**, making occasional Seoul trips genuinely practical rather than an all-day commitment. For business or travel pulling toward Japan specifically, the Camellia Line ferry connects Busan to Fukuoka in a few hours, a routing option worth knowing about if client meetings or personal ties sit on the Japanese side of the strait rather than requiring a flight through Incheon.
