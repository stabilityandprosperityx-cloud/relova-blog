**[Move to Cape Town](/blog/cape-town-vs-nairobi-vs-accra-expat-2026)** and you are choosing one of the most livable cities on the African continent, but South Africa's immigration system is not the fastest in the world, and the city's own infrastructure quirks catch newcomers who only budgeted for rent.

## Finding a legal route to stay

South Africa does not run a single obvious "expat visa." Retirees are the clearest case: the retired person visa asks for a documented, guaranteed monthly income, and that figure is commonly cited near ZAR 37,000 (roughly USD 2,000, though the rand exchange rate moves this around constantly). That income has to come from a pension, annuity, or retirement fund rather than active work, and it needs to be proven with formal letters from the paying institution, not bank statements alone. Remote workers have a newer option: South Africa introduced a remote work visitor visa endorsement in 2024 aimed at digital nomads employed by foreign companies, with an income threshold pitched well above the median local salary. Because both routes have shifted in the last two years, treat any number you read — including the ones here — as a starting point to confirm with the Department of Home Affairs or a South African immigration attorney before you commit to a lease or a flight.

## What rent and daily costs actually look like

Cape Town's rental market varies more by suburb than almost any other city on this list. A one-bedroom in the City Bowl or Southern Suburbs commonly lands between ZAR 12,000 and 20,000 a month, while Sea Point and the Atlantic Seaboard — the stretch most newcomers gravitate to for the ocean views and walkability — often runs ZAR 15,000 to 25,000 for the same square footage. Deposits typically equal one to two months' rent, and agents will want proof of income even for a short lease. Add private medical aid (South Africa's public healthcare system is not built for expat use), a car or reliable rideshare budget since public transport is patchy outside the city bowl, and a real contingency line, because currency swings against the rand can quietly erode a budget that looked fine on paper six months earlier.

## Load-shedding, water, and the practical friction

The single biggest quality-of-life variable in Cape Town has nothing to do with visas or rent: it is the power grid. Rolling blackouts, known locally as load-shedding, hit Stage 6 severity in prior years, meaning scheduled multi-hour outages several times a day. Reliability improved materially through 2024 and into 2025 as Eskom brought more generation capacity online, but outages have not been eliminated, and any serious move plan should assume you will need a backup — an inverter, solar setup, or at minimum a power bank routine — particularly if you work remotely and cannot simply wait out an outage. The 2018 water crisis ("Day Zero") also left a lasting culture of water conservation; low-flow fixtures and metered usage are normal, not a hardship sign.

## Neighborhoods, safety, and settling in

Sea Point and Green Point suit people who want walkable cafés and ocean air within reach of the CBD; the Southern Suburbs (Newlands, Claremont, Rondebosch) suit families and anyone prioritizing schools and quieter streets, at the cost of a longer commute into the city. Security is a genuine and constant topic in Cape Town in a way it is not in most European or Southeast Asian destinations on this site — gated complexes, alarm response services, and situational awareness about which areas to avoid after dark are standard practice rather than paranoia, and most long-term expats adapt within a few months. Banking setup is straightforward once you have a residential address and visa documentation; the major banks (FNB, Standard Bank, Absa) all serve foreign nationals, though account opening can take longer than in the EU.

Build in at least 15 to 25 percent budget slack beyond your headline rent and visa-income figures — deposits, medical aid premiums, and backup power equipment add up faster than glossy relocation guides suggest, and adjudicators on the visa side benchmark income proof strictly against the published thresholds at the time you file, not the figure you read in a blog post months earlier.
