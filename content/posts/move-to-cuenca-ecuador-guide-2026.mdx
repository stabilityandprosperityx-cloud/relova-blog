Cuenca is Ecuador’s **third city**, a **UNESCO** historic centre, and about **8,000 feet** up in the Andes. The climate pitch is “eternal spring”: roughly **48–70°F** year-round, with a wetter stretch **January–May**. US dollars are legal tender, which is why American retirees can budget without a peso soap opera. The visa floors are among the lowest in large Latin American countries. Safety is **not** the 2018 brochure. Hold both facts.

## Why people pick Cuenca specifically

The colonial grid, churches, and walkable centre are the architecture argument. The altitude is the health argument: milder heat than the coast, thinner air than you notice on day one (give yourself a week). Rain from **January to May** is the calendar argument — pack a jacket, not a monsoon plan. Dollarization is the finance argument: rents, groceries, and clinic quotes in **USD** match a US Social Security deposit without a blue-dollar detour.

English-speaking density helps the first months: estimates put on the order of **10,000 Americans** across Cuenca, Quito, and coastal **Salinas**. English-speaking doctors, agents, and clubs exist. They are a ramp, not a reason to skip Spanish for anything official. The UNESCO core is also a tourist core; live one barrio off the postcard if you want sleep.

## Jubilado versus Rentista

**Jubilado (pensioner)** wants about **$800 a month** in pension income — often cited as the **lowest major LATAM pension-visa floor** — plus **$250 a month per dependant**. Temporary residence is typically **two years**; after **21 months** you can file for permanent residence. **Citizenship after three years** of legal residence is one of the faster Latin American clocks. Confirm current Ministerio rules; this is not a naturalisation kit.

**Rentista** uses the same **$800 a month** idea for **passive income that is not a pension**. Same family of stay, different proof packet. If your income is a mix of pension and dividends, get the category right before you pay a “visa runner.”

Government fees are small: about **$50** to file, **$270** on approval, **$15** for the **cédula**. The expensive part is the income proof and apostilles, not the stamps. Two years temporary, then the permanent-residence file after 21 months, then a citizenship conversation at three years — those clocks only run if the visa was the right class and the renewals were clean. Compared with pension-visa floors elsewhere in the region, **$800** is the number that still makes Cuenca a default on retiree spreadsheets. Verify it the month you file; floors move.

## What a couple actually spends

A couple living comfortably is often **$1,400–2,000 a month**, with other estimates spanning **$1,200–2,200**. The spread is lifestyle and whether you import a US shopping list. Private care is commonly described as **10–20% of US prices** for comparable outpatient work — still not free, still worth a second opinion on surgery. A single person cooking at home lands nearer the bottom; a couple that eats out and keeps a car lands nearer $2,000.

Legal residents can join **IESS**. The contribution is **17.6%** of declared income, with the 2026 minimum base the **Salario Básico Unificado of $482**, so a floor contribution around **$84.83 a month**. That is social insurance math, not a US Medicare substitute. Medicare generally does not travel. Many still keep international cover during the first year, especially if they bounce between Cuenca and the US for family.

## Safety without the paradise filter

Since **2022**, cartel-linked violence has worsened nationally, **especially on the coast**. Cuenca and Quito are still the two cities most often named as **practical, relatively safer bases**. The US State Department level is **Level 2 (exercise increased caution)** — not a do-not-travel ban, and not Level 1 either. Street sense, night taxis, and not flashing cash still apply. Do not sell Cuenca as a locked garden. Do not treat Salinas as interchangeable with Cuenca because “Ecuador is one country on a map.”

Cuenca in 2026 is **UNESCO + dollars + an $800 visa floor + a three-year citizenship conversation**, with a **Level 2** security overlay. If the $800 pension is real and the altitude agrees with you, it remains one of the more coherent retiree files in the Andes. If you need coastal humidity and beach towns, read the 2022-onward crime trend before Salinas becomes the plan.
