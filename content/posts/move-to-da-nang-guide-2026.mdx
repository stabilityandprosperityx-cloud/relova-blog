**Move to Da Nang, Vietnam**, and you get one of Southeast Asia's best value-for-money beach cities — a genuine mid-size Vietnamese city with a proper airport, a long stretch of coastline, and living costs well below [Ho Chi Minh](/blog/move-to-ho-chi-minh-city-guide-2026) City or Hanoi. The one thing it does not offer, despite how it is sometimes marketed, is a dedicated [digital nomad visa](/blog/move-to-chiang-mai-guide-2026).

## The visa reality: no nomad visa, so plan around what exists

As of 2026, Vietnam still has no formal digital nomad visa program. What remote workers actually use instead is a patchwork of existing categories. The most common route is the 90-day multiple-entry e-visa, which costs around USD 50, is available to nearly all nationalities, can be applied for entirely online, and permits unlimited border crossings during its validity — a real advantage over the cheaper 90-day single-entry e-visa (around USD 25), which only works for a one-time stay. Because e-visas cannot be extended from inside the country, long-term nomads run the well-established "visa run" cycle: exit before the 90 days lapse, typically to Cambodia, Thailand, Laos, or the Philippines, and re-enter on a fresh e-visa, with processing usually taking three to five working days. Some nationalities also get a shorter 45-day visa-free entry that cannot be extended or renewed on the spot. A sponsored business visa is the other real option if you can arrange local sponsorship, and it offers longer validity without the repeated exit cycle, but it requires a Vietnamese entity willing to sponsor you. Overstaying carries daily fines starting around USD 20 and risks a re-entry ban, so track your dates carefully regardless of which route you choose — Vietnam's rules here are enforced, not theoretical.

## An Thuong rent and what a realistic budget looks like

An Thuong, the beachside neighborhood that has become Da Nang's de facto nomad and expat hub, offers some of the best value housing in the country: one-bedroom apartments commonly list between USD 300 and 550 a month, a price point that undercuts most comparable Southeast Asian beach destinations by a wide margin. A realistic solo remote-worker budget covering rent, food, a scooter, coworking access, and basic health insurance typically starts around USD 700 to 1,200 a month, scaling up with how much Western-style dining and imported goods you want versus local street food and markets. Scooters are the default transport here — cheap to rent, easy to navigate the flat coastal roads, and largely unavoidable given the limited public transit outside the central bus routes.

## Typhoon and rainy season planning

The detail that matters most for anyone choosing where to live in Da Nang is timing relative to the weather. Rainy season runs roughly from September through December, with the heaviest typhoon risk concentrated in October through December, when central Vietnam sits directly in the path of storms tracking in off the South China Sea. This affects more than just your umbrella budget — it shapes which buildings and neighborhoods are worth renting in, since flooding risk varies by elevation and drainage quality, and some coastal or low-lying areas see genuine disruption during peak storm weeks. If you have flexibility, arriving outside this window (roughly February through August) gives you a calmer first impression of the city and more time to scout housing before storm season complicates viewings and moves.

## Settling in beyond the basics

Da Nang's healthcare infrastructure, while not on par with Bangkok's international hospitals, has improved steadily, with several private clinics serving the expat community for routine care; anything more serious typically means a trip to Ho Chi Minh City or Bangkok, so travel or international health insurance is worth carrying rather than relying solely on local facilities. Internet is fast and reliable by regional standards, which is a large part of why the city built a nomad following in the first place, and the coworking scene, while smaller than Chiang Mai's or Bali's, has grown enough to offer real options in and around An Thuong and the city center.
