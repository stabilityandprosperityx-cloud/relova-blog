Fukuoka has run a national-strategic-zone **Startup Visa** since 2015 — the city government sponsors a preliminary residence status (typically issued in six-month blocks, renewable up to a total of one year) so a founder can incorporate and start trading before they qualify for a real work visa. It was a workaround built for exactly this: it lets you skip the usual "you need a visa to start a company, but need a company to get a visa" loop that traps first-time founders elsewhere in Japan. The catch is that it is a bridge, not a destination — you need to convert to a **Business Manager visa** before the clock runs out, and that conversion got dramatically harder in 2026.

## The Business Manager capital bar just tripled

Japan's Business Manager visa requirements changed on **10 October 2025**: the minimum capital requirement rose from **¥5 million to ¥30 million** (roughly $200,000 at recent rates), applicants now need either three-plus years of documented experience as a business manager or C-suite executive, or a relevant master's/doctorate, and the company must employ at least one full-time worker who can show Japanese-language ability. A qualified third party (an administrative scrivener or similar) now has to certify the business plan unless the company already operates at listed-company scale. Existing visa holders get a **three-year transition window** to raise capital to the new threshold, but anyone converting from the Fukuoka Startup Visa in 2026 is applying under the new rules from day one. If you were mentally budgeting ¥5 million, revisit the plan before you file anything — this is the single fact in this guide most worth re-verifying with an immigration scrivener the month you apply, since further adjustment is plausible.

## Tenjin rent versus Hakata rent

Housing is the one part of Fukuoka living that has not moved much. A one-bedroom in **Tenjin**, the retail and nightlife core, commonly lists between **¥65,000 and ¥100,000 a month** (roughly $435–670), with the wider band reflecting building age and whether the unit is a step from the subway or a ten-minute walk. **Hakata**, built around the Shinkansen station, tends to run slightly cheaper at **¥60,000–95,000**, and it is the better base if your work involves regular Tokyo or Osaka travel — the bullet train dominates that commute over flying once you count airport transfer time. Deposits, key money (*reikin*), and a guarantor company fee typically add three to five months of rent up front, which is the line item first-time movers from North America or Europe most often underbudget.

## The Busan ferry changes the geography

The JR Beetle jetfoil to Busan, South Korea, is commonly quoted at around **three hours** door to port, which is genuinely faster and cheaper than flying once you factor airport check-in windows on both ends. For anyone with Korean clients, family, or a visa-run habit, that changes Fukuoka's mental map: it is closer to Busan than to Tokyo in travel time. Itoshima's beaches sit roughly thirty minutes west by car, which matters less for logistics and more for the fact that Fukuoka is genuinely one of the few Japanese startup hubs where a surf morning before a client call is a realistic Tuesday.

## Why founders pick Fukuoka over Tokyo or Osaka

The pitch is not that Fukuoka is cheaper than Tokyo, though it is — it is that the city government has spent a decade actively courting founders, running English-language business support desks and a startup café network that Tokyo's much larger ecosystem doesn't bother replicating for a single-founder shop. If your business plan genuinely needs Tokyo-scale capital or a Tokyo-scale client base, that gravity is real and Fukuoka's visa runway won't fix it. If it does not, the six-month preparation window is real breathing room — as long as you build the ¥30 million conversion into the plan from month one rather than discovering it in month five.
