Gdańsk, with Gdynia and Sopot, forms the Baltic **Tri-City** — one of Poland’s most liveable coastal options for expats who want EU access without Warsaw prices. Poland has **no dedicated digital-nomad visa**, so you win or lose on Blue Card, self-employment, or short Schengen stays. Structure the move as **why Gdańsk over Warsaw/Kraków → visas and 2025–26 labour reforms → life on the ground**.

## Why Gdańsk instead of Warsaw or Kraków

Living costs run about **10–20% lower** than Warsaw and Kraków. Central one-bedroom rents usually match or undercut Kraków levels while giving you sea air and a three-city labour market linked by rail. Solo budgets in Poland often start near **€1,400/month**; Gdańsk typically lands a bit under Warsaw for the same standard.

The Tri-City also spreads lifestyle options: Gdańsk for the historic core and larger-city services, Sopot for the pier and denser leisure strip, Gdynia for a more modernist port-city feel. You are not trapped in a single neighbourhood brand the way smaller Polish cities can feel.

Career density in some corporate headquarters still favours Warsaw. If your role is remote or your employer accepts a Tri-City base, the cost and coastal quality-of-life trade usually favours Gdańsk. If you need daily face time in a capital HQ, calculate train and flight friction honestly before you romanticise the Baltic. Kraków wins on tourist energy and some creative scenes; Gdańsk wins on coast-plus-value for people who want an ordinary European week with water nearby.

Visit in late autumn if you can. Baltic winters are dark and windy; a July city break is a weak predictor of January mood.

## Visas and work: Blue Card, JDG, reforms

Main lanes in 2026:

- **EU Blue Card:** from **9 February 2026** the salary floor is **PLN 13,355.34 gross/month** (also discussed around **€27,000/year** in some summaries). Processing is often about **8 weeks**. Permanent residence commonly follows roughly **five years**, usually with Polish at **B1**.
- **JDG** sole proprietorship: register online via **CEIDG** in about **24 hours** if you already have a lawful basis to stay and operate. Popular with freelancers who can invoice properly and handle ZUS/tax compliance.
- Short scouting: standard Schengen **90/180** if you are only testing the city.

Labour-market reform matters. From **June 2025**, many categories dropped the labour-market test in favour of a **National Occupational Priority** list — IT, healthcare, green energy, and construction among the highlighted fields. Ordinary work-permit fees jumped about **four times** in **December 2025**, which makes Blue Card and priority occupations more important relative to legacy permit routes than they were two years ago.

If you are remote for a foreign company, do not assume “laptop in a cafe” equals a Polish right to stay. Match the immigration basis to the employment contract before you ship furniture. Employers hiring into Poland should confirm whether Blue Card salary thresholds and priority-occupation rules apply to your role — the reform environment rewards clean classification.

## Life on the ground: transit, health, budget

The **SKM** suburban rail links Gdańsk Główny and Gdynia in about **30 minutes**, every **5–15 minutes** at peak. A Tri-City monthly pass costs about **PLN 130** — one of the cleaner commuting deals in European coastal cities of this size. That pass is why living in Gdynia while coworking in Gdańsk (or the reverse) is normal rather than heroic.

Healthcare: legal residents enter **NFZ** with a mandatory contribution around **9%** of wages. Most expats add private cover (**€300–800/year**) for Medicover, LuxMed, or Enel-Med appointment speed. Budget both lines if you care about wait times. Register with a clinic early; do not wait for the first fever on a Sunday.

Language and admin improve with basic Polish for clinics and municipal offices; English carries farther in IT than in every neighbourhood office. Open banking and PESEL-related steps in the order your employer or lawyer sequences — skipping ahead creates loops.

Gdańsk suits EU-bound remote and tech workers who can clear Blue Card thresholds or run a clean JDG — and who prefer Tri-City coastal living over capital rents. Lock the immigration lane first; the SKM pass comes after.

One more practical note: Tri-City leases and employer address letters should tell a consistent story. If your Blue Card or JDG paperwork lists Gdańsk while you actually live in Sopot, keep the documents aligned with reality. Polish admin is manageable when your file is coherent; it becomes slow when your addresses, contracts, and declarations disagree.
