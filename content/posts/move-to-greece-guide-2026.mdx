Greece sells sunlight and islands effortlessly, but **how to move to Greece in 2026** still runs on paperwork: income proofs, insurance wording, property contracts, and tax elections that do not forgive casual guesses. This guide focuses on the two routes most planners actually compare, plus the national cost and tax picture — for Athens specifically, see the dedicated [Athens guide](/blog/move-to-athens-guide-2026), and for island-specific logistics, see the [Greek islands guide](/blog/move-to-greek-islands-guide-2026).

## [Digital Nomad Visa](/blog/athens-vs-lisbon-expat-guide-2026) versus Golden Visa

Greece's [Digital Nomad Visa](/blog/nomad-visa-europe-comparison), introduced in 2021 and updated since, is commonly summarized with an income requirement around €3,500 a month for the primary applicant, aimed at remote employees and freelancers working for employers or clients outside Greece. It requires proof of ongoing foreign-sourced income, a clean criminal record, and comprehensive health insurance meeting the consulate's specific wording — treat the income figure as a planning anchor and confirm the live threshold, contract requirements, and tax treatment with Greek counsel before filing, since program details have moved since launch. The Golden Visa, Greece's real-estate-linked residency-by-investment program, is widely quoted at a €250,000 minimum property investment for the standard tier, though the country has introduced higher thresholds (up to €800,000) in high-demand zones like central Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, and Santorini in recent revisions — verify which zone classification applies to any specific property before assuming the lower figure still holds. Golden Visa grants five-year renewable residence and Schengen access but, notably, does not require you to actually live in Greece full-time, which is part of why it appeals to investors who want residency optionality rather than a primary home.

## The tax angle that draws retirees and remote earners

Greek tax policy has actively courted two groups since the early 2020s. Foreign retirees can apply for a flat 7 percent income tax rate on foreign-sourced pension income for up to 15 years, provided they shift full tax residency to Greece and were not Greek tax residents for five of the prior six years — a real incentive, but one gated by specific eligibility windows that a Greek tax advisor needs to confirm against your nationality, pension type, and any applicable tax treaty before you assume it applies automatically. Separately, foreign employees and self-employed people who become Greek tax residents can, under certain conditions tied to staying at least two years, access a 50 percent income tax reduction for up to seven years — a meaningful discount for remote workers weighing Greece against lower-tax destinations elsewhere in Europe. Neither regime is self-executing; both require formal application and ongoing compliance with residency-day and income-sourcing rules.

## What a national cost baseline looks like

Outside Athens (covered separately) and the islands (also covered separately), mid-size mainland cities like Thessaloniki, Patras, and Larissa offer meaningfully lower costs than the capital while retaining real infrastructure — hospitals, universities, and functioning public transport — without the seasonal tourism premium that distorts island pricing. A realistic national planning range for a comfortable single-person budget, covering rent, food, insurance, and local transport outside the most expensive Athens neighborhoods and peak-season island destinations, lands broadly in the €1,000 to 1,600 a month range, though this swings meaningfully by city and season. Long-stay visa categories require comprehensive health insurance matching consulate wording as a hard gate, not an optional extra, and if you're self-employed, understanding how SEPA banking and Greek social contribution obligations intersect with your specific visa category is worth a dedicated consultation rather than an assumption carried over from another EU country's system.

## Banking, deposits, and a realistic first 90 days

Use standard apartment-hunting discipline for deposits — landlords commonly ask for one to two months upfront, and confirming lease terms in writing before wiring anything protects against the informal arrangements that still show up in parts of the Greek rental market. Pair that with early banking setup, since opening a Greek account typically requires your tax number (AFM) and residence documentation in hand. Your first 90 days should prioritize locking address proof, obtaining your AFM as required for your visa category, and confirming insurance continuity before you spend energy optimizing which neighborhood has the best view — the paperwork sequencing determines whether your later months run smoothly or get consumed by rework.
