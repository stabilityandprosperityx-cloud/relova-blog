## Which island fits long-term life

Crete is the practical year-round base. Corfu rewards English-speaking social life but expect winter service cuts. Rhodes sits between tourism and remote-work cafés. Quieter Naxos, Syros, and Ikaria often price one-beds **€350–600**. Island rents are rising about **~5%/year** in 2026 messaging, closer to **7%** in parts of Corfu and Crete.

## Visas, tax perks, and Golden Visa

Greece’s **Digital Nomad Visa** needs about **€3,500/month after tax**, a clean criminal record, health insurance, and work for a foreign employer or your own business outside Greece. Nomads who become tax residents (**183+ days**) for at least **two years** can get a **50% income-tax reduction for seven years**. Retirees may use a **7% flat tax on foreign pensions for 15 years** if they move tax residency to Greece and were not Greek tax residents for five of the prior six years—see also [passive income visas](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/passive-income-visa-countries). **Golden Visa** property investment often starts at **€250,000** for five-year renewable residence and Schengen access. Citizenship commonly follows about **seven years** of residence.

## Healthcare and language reality

Registered residents with an **AMKA** number use public care via **EOPYY/ESY**. Private consults run about **€60–150**; basic private insurance is often near **€300/year**. Greek is not always mandatory for new visa categories, but bureaucracy without it is slow.
