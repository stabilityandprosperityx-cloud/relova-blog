## The E-Visa Route Into Vietnam

Ho Chi Minh City runs at a different pace than [Hanoi](/blog/move-to-hanoi-guide-2026), and it shows up in the small details before you even unpack: the e-visa fee, the district you pick, and how quickly you learn to cross a street without hesitating. Vietnam's e-visa is commonly priced around USD 25 and is generally issued for stays of up to 90 days, single or multiple entry, and it can typically be applied for online well before your flight. As with the rest of Vietnam, there's still no dedicated digital nomad visa, so most remote workers stay on tourist or e-visa status and either do periodic visa runs to Cambodia or Thailand, or work with a local agent to explore a business or work-permit route if they've landed employment with a Vietnamese or foreign-invested company. Renewal rules and processing times shift often enough that it's worth confirming current requirements with an immigration service rather than relying on last year's forum post.

## Thao Dien, Binh Thanh, and District 1 Rents

Housing splits along familiar lines. Thao Dien, in District 2 (now formally part of Thu Duc City), is the expat and international-family hub — leafy streets, international schools, Western-style cafes and gyms, and a large community of long-term foreign residents — with one-bedroom apartments generally running USD 600 to 1,100 depending on building quality and whether it's serviced. Binh Thanh, just across the river, offers a more local, more affordable alternative with a fast-growing food and nightlife scene of its own, and comparable one-bedrooms often land USD 350 to 600. District 1 remains the tourist and business core, convenient but noisier and typically pricier per square meter than either of the above. Most newcomers spend their first couple of weeks in a short-let near District 1 to get their bearings, then commit to a year-long lease once they've tested their commute and noise tolerance against their actual daily routine.

## Getting Around: Motorbikes, Grab, and the New Metro

Daily life in a city of roughly 9 million people is inseparable from the motorbike. Public transit exists but remains limited relative to the city's size — a single metro line opened in recent years, with more under construction — so most residents, expat and local alike, either buy or rent a motorbike, or lean heavily on Grab (Southeast Asia's dominant ride-hailing and delivery app) for both transport and food. Buying a used motorbike typically costs several hundred dollars and can be resold when you leave; renting monthly is simpler but adds a recurring line to your budget. Either way, plan for the adjustment period: Saigon traffic looks chaotic to newcomers but follows its own logic, and most people find their confidence within a few weeks of daily riding or careful Grab use.

## Monthly Budget and Private Healthcare

On cost of living, a comfortable nomad or early-career expat budget in HCMC generally runs somewhere in the same range as Hanoi's — rent plus food, transport, and basic entertainment before health insurance or coworking fees — though Thao Dien living pushes the top end higher than a Binh Thanh or District 7 setup would. Add 15 to 25 percent on top of your projected number for the first month or two while you cover deposits, SIM and banking setup, motorbike costs, and the unavoidable extra Grab rides while you're still learning the city's geography. Private healthcare (FV Hospital, Family Medical Practice, and similar international clinics) is reliable and English-friendly but meaningfully cheaper with insurance arranged before you land than paid out of pocket after a clinic visit. Vietnam's cost advantage over most Western cities is real, but it compounds fastest for people who get their district, transport, and insurance decisions right in the first month rather than defaulting to the most convenient tourist-facing option.

## Coworking Around Thao Dien and District 1

Coworking and cafe culture are well developed here compared to much of the rest of Vietnam, with a genuine density of remote-worker-friendly spaces concentrated around Thao Dien and District 1, so anyone needing a reliable desk outside their apartment generally has good options within a short Grab ride.
