Ireland's main route for skilled non-EU workers just got a salary increase, and the government has signaled more are coming — worth knowing before you benchmark an offer against outdated numbers.

## The Critical Skills Employment Permit, and the March 2026 threshold change

The Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP) is Ireland's flagship route for skilled non-EEA workers with a job offer in an eligible occupation. As of 1 March 2026, Ireland's Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment raised the general minimum salary threshold from EUR 38,000 to EUR 40,904 for roles requiring a relevant degree, part of a phased, multi-year roadmap of gradual increases the government announced rather than a one-off jump. Standard employment permits (for roles without a relevant degree requirement) rose to roughly EUR 68,911. A separate, lower threshold applies to graduate-level Critical Skills roles, commonly cited around EUR 36,848. Because these figures are explicitly described as provisional pending confirmation for future years, applicants and employers should check the current published thresholds on the enterprise.gov.ie site before finalizing an offer letter, rather than relying on a screenshot from last year.

## What it costs to live in Dublin vs. Cork

Dublin remains Ireland's most expensive city by a wide margin, with one-bedroom apartments in decent, well-connected areas commonly running EUR 2,000-2,800 a month — a figure that has pushed a growing number of arrivals to consider Cork, Ireland's second city, where comparable one-bedrooms run closer to EUR 1,500-2,200. Cork's job market is smaller but has real depth in pharma, tech, and life sciences given the concentration of multinational manufacturing operations in the region, making it a genuine alternative rather than just a cheaper fallback.

## Why the corporate tax rate matters even if you're not a founder

Ireland's 12.5% corporate tax rate on trading income (still broadly the headline rate for most companies, though a 15% minimum now applies to the largest multinationals under OECD global tax rules) is the reason so many of the world's biggest tech and pharma firms run European headquarters from Dublin — which in turn is why the Critical Skills occupation list leans so heavily toward software, data, engineering, and life sciences roles. Understanding this pipeline helps explain both where the jobs cluster and why competition for Dublin housing near those employer campuses is especially tight.

## Long-term status: naturalization and descent

Standard naturalization in Ireland generally requires accumulating the equivalent of five years of reckonable residence (with specific rules about which years count and how much continuous presence is required in the final year), after which you can apply for citizenship by naturalization. Separately, Irish citizenship by descent is available to people with an Irish-born parent automatically, and in some cases can extend to a grandparent or further back through the Foreign Births Register if the family chain is properly documented — a route worth investigating in parallel with any work-permit plan if you have Irish ancestry, since it can bypass the employment-permit process entirely.

## Housing supply is the real bottleneck

Ireland's well-documented housing shortage means the Dublin and Cork rent figures above are a starting point, not a guarantee — competitive listings in popular areas can attract dozens of applicants within days, and it's common for arrivals to need temporary accommodation (an Airbnb or short-term let) for the first few weeks while they secure a longer lease in person. The Residential Tenancies Board (RTB) governs tenant and landlord rights nationally, and registering a tenancy with the RTB is a landlord obligation worth confirming before signing, since it affects your legal protections as a tenant. Given the tight market, many employers sponsoring Critical Skills Employment Permit holders now offer relocation support or temporary housing assistance as a recruiting differentiator — worth asking about directly during salary negotiations rather than assuming it's standard.

## Healthcare while you wait for eligibility

Ireland's public healthcare system (the HSE) is available to residents, but a Critical Skills Employment Permit alone doesn't grant immediate free access to all services — many newcomers carry private health insurance during their first months, both as a practical bridge and because some visa or permit renewal processes expect proof of health coverage on file. Private insurance in Ireland is relatively affordable by international standards and widely used even among long-term residents as a supplement to public care, particularly for faster specialist access.
