Kraków is Poland’s **second city** and former royal capital: compact old town, real winter, and a serious **IT / shared-services** stack. **Goldman Sachs, HSBC, Capgemini** and peers run centres here. You come for that labour market and for costs that still undercut Western Europe, not for a digital-nomad visa — **Poland does not offer one**.

## Why Kraków (and not only Warsaw)

Warsaw is the political and corporate default. Kraków is the livable second node: walkable core, tourist density you learn to route around, and salaries that are national rather than “capital plus 20%.” Official average pay in the city (January **2026**) sits around **11,086 PLN gross a month**. Comfortable single living is often cited at **6,000–7,000 PLN** (~**€1,370–1,600**); a couple at **9,000–10,000 PLN**. Versus Dublin or large Western capitals, that is about **32–40% cheaper** on a typical basket.

You still compete for central flats with students, tourists, and the SSC workforce. The city’s size is the feature: weekends in the Tatras, weekdays in an office or a café with fibre.

## Visas and work: Blue Card or JDG

Two mainstream tracks:

**EU Blue Card.** From February **2026** the Polish salary floor is **PLN 13,355.34 gross per month**. Processing is often around **eight weeks**. Five years on the card is the usual conversation toward permanent residence — confirm current URZĘD dates, do not screenshot a 2024 blog. You need a qualifying job and a qualifying employer. The Blue Card is not a freelance loophole.

**JDG (Jednoosobowa działalność gospodarcza)** — a one-person firm you can register **online in 24 hours** via **CEIDG** if you already have the right to reside and work. It is the freelancer/contractor wrapper, not a visa by itself. Non-EU citizens still need an underlying stay basis. Do not confuse a CEIDG login with a residence card.

There is **no Polish digital-nomad visa**. Remote employees of foreign companies either sit on a local contract/Blue Card path, a national D visa / karta czasowego pobytu tied to work or business, or they are stretching a tourist stay they should not stretch.

## What you actually pay, by area

Central one-bedrooms: **3,200–4,200 PLN** (~**€750–990**). Outside the core: **2,200–3,200 PLN** (~**€520–750**). Studios on the edge from about **1,800 PLN**.

**Krowodrza** and other student belts are where **flatshares** still move the needle: sharing can save on the order of **€6,200 a year** versus flying solo on a central one-bed. That is the difference between “Kraków on a junior remote salary” and “Kraków on a Western net.”

Walk Kazimierz for atmosphere and noise; walk newer blocks if you need a desk and a washing machine that is not in the basement of a 1890 tenement. Budget winter heating as a line, not a surprise.

Language is the other practical layer. English is common in SSCs and tourist streets; NFZ letters, housing cooperatives, and some clinics are not. Budget a few hours of Polish for admin, or a bilingual accountant if you go JDG. The 24-hour CEIDG registration is the easy part; VAT, ZUS, and invoices are the part that eats evenings if you pretend they are optional.

## Healthcare in practice

Legal residents pay into **NFZ** at **9%** of salary. That is the public floor. Most expats still buy private cover in the **€300–800 a year** band for **Medicover / LuxMed**-style networks, because public queues and English-language access are the actual pain, not the existence of a public system.

Register with NFZ when your residence basis allows it; keep private insurance through the first winter. Dentists and specialists are where people upgrade.

If you are arriving without a Blue Card offer, do not treat JDG as a back door into the labour market. You still need a legal stay. Recruiters in the SSC world hire into employment contracts first; the Blue Card salary floor of **PLN 13,355.34** is the number to test against your offer letter, not against the city average of **11,086 PLN**. An average salary job will not carry a Blue Card by itself.

Kraków in 2026 is a Blue Card or JDG city with **11k PLN** average gross, **6–7k PLN** comfortable single spend, and no nomad stamp. Use the metro of offices, not the postcard, as your reason to come.
