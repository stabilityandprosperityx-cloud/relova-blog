Since Brexit, UK citizens have needed a visa to live in Lisbon long-term, and the two routes people actually use are the **D7** (passive income — pensions, dividends, rental income) and the **D8** (remote work / digital nomad, for employed or self-employed applicants earning above the Portuguese minimum wage). The D7's income bar is tied to that minimum wage: roughly **€820 a month** for a single applicant as the current reference point, plus **50%** more for a spouse or adult dependent and **30%** per child — figures that move whenever Portugal's minimum wage is revised, so confirm the live number at the consulate rather than trusting a fixed figure. Alongside monthly income, D7 applicants are generally expected to show around **€9,840–11,040** in accessible savings, roughly twelve months' worth of the income floor. The application-to-residence-card pipeline commonly runs **6–9 months** start to finish: up to four months at the consulate stage, an AIMA appointment within 120 days of arrival, and a residence card that should follow within about three weeks of that appointment, though delays through AIMA's backlog are common enough to budget for.

## The National Insurance clock that is actually resetting

This is the detail most Lisbon-for-Brits content gets wrong or leaves out entirely, and it is time-sensitive in a way that deserves attention now: the UK's existing arrangement letting people abroad pay the cheap **voluntary Class 2 National Insurance rate** — roughly **£180 a year** — is lapsing on **5 April 2026**. From the 2026/27 tax year, expats filling NI gaps move to **Class 3**, which costs roughly **£900 a year**, a five-fold jump for the same qualifying year toward the UK State Pension. There is a transitional window: you can still apply to buy 2024/25 or 2025/26 years under the old rules if you apply by **5 April 2026** and pay by **5 April 2027**. Newer applicants will also face a tighter eligibility bar going forward — generally **10 years** of UK residency or NI contributions, up from the current three-year minimum. If you have gaps in your NI record and were planning to "get to it eventually," eventually needs to happen before this window closes, not after.

## Pension payment and uprating, without the myths

The UK State Pension is payable in Portugal, and — because Portugal is treated favorably under the post-Brexit Withdrawal Agreement for people who were already resident or covered before the cutoff, alongside the ongoing UK-EU social security coordination — it continues to be **uprated annually** for eligible claimants rather than frozen, unlike the fate of UK pensions paid in countries with no such arrangement (Canada and Australia being the famous frozen-pension cases). This is genuinely good news relative to the "frozen pension" horror stories that circulate about Brits retiring outside these coordinated systems, but it depends on your specific NI record and residency history qualifying under the applicable rules — this is a case where a five-minute call to the UK Pension Service or a cross-border pensions adviser is worth far more than a forum thread.

## Driving, banking, and the parts that are just admin

A UK driving licence can be exchanged for a Portuguese one within a defined window after establishing residency — commonly cited as roughly **two years**, though the exact exchange deadline has shifted before and is worth reconfirming with IMT (Portugal's transport authority) at the time you apply, since bilateral licence-exchange terms are exactly the kind of administrative detail that changes without much fanfare. Opening a Portuguese bank account (needed for the D7/D8 income deposit and later for AIMA) is now routinely done before departure through several banks with UK-facing digital onboarding, which removes one of the classic first-week bottlenecks. None of this replaces proper cross-border tax and pension advice — a D7 or D8 visa is a stay basis, not automatically the correct tax election for your specific pension income or NHR-successor regime status, and those figures are worth paying a qualified adviser to confirm before you file.
