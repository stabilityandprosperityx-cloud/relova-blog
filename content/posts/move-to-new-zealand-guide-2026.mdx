**How to move to New Zealand in 2026** starts with an honest read of the country's points-based Skilled Migrant Category, which remains the primary route for skilled professionals seeking permanent residence rather than a temporary work stint. Applications are commonly modeled around needing 160 or more points in current planning discussions, drawn from a combination of skilled employment (or a job offer), work experience, qualifications, and age — younger applicants with in-demand qualifications score meaningfully higher, and New Zealand periodically adjusts which occupations count as "skilled" enough to qualify, so checking the current Green List (occupations facing genuine shortages) against your profession before investing time in an application is worth doing early rather than late.

For people not ready to commit to the points-based route, the Working Holiday visa remains the lower-friction entry point for eligible nationalities (typically ages 18–30 or 18–35 depending on the bilateral agreement), with an application fee commonly listed near NZD $280 — a genuinely useful way to spend up to a year working and living in New Zealand while deciding whether a longer-term move makes sense, without committing to the more demanding Skilled Migrant paperwork upfront.

On cost of living, Auckland — New Zealand's largest city and main economic hub — runs a one-bedroom rental around NZD $2,200–2,800 a month in most decent neighborhoods, reflecting genuine housing supply pressure that's persisted for years despite periodic government intervention. Wellington, the capital, is somewhat gentler on the wallet at roughly NZD $1,800–2,400, while offering a famously walkable, hilly, creatively dense city that many expats find easier to build a social life in than Auckland's more sprawling geography. Both cities require budgeting well above headline "New Zealand cost of living" averages that often lean on smaller-town data — a common trap for people planning around national statistics rather than city-specific rental markets. For reference, New Zealand's minimum wage sits around NZD $23.15 an hour, useful context when sanity-checking any job offer against local living costs.

Healthcare in New Zealand runs on a public system (funded through general taxation) that residents and eligible visa holders can access, though most newcomers on temporary visas need private health insurance until they qualify for public coverage, and it's worth confirming your specific visa category's healthcare access rules before assuming public coverage kicks in immediately on arrival. Banking is comparatively straightforward by international standards — major banks generally allow account opening once you have a visa and local address, though some require an in-person branch visit rather than fully remote setup.

Citizenship planning commonly follows permanent residency by around five years of qualifying residence, though exact rules around time spent in-country and other conditions are worth confirming with Immigration New Zealand directly, since residency-to-citizenship rules have shifted over time and vary somewhat by which visa route got you to permanent residence in the first place.

A sensible approach: spend the first phase of planning confirming which visa category you genuinely qualify for and building a document matrix with certified translations and issue dates in order, then use a scouting trip (or thorough remote research) to settle on Auckland versus Wellington versus a smaller center before committing to a lease. Build in a 15–25% contingency on top of whatever budget model you land on — New Zealand's remoteness means shipping, flights, and setup costs run higher than many people expect from a wealthy English-speaking country. Owning or leasing a car is close to essential outside the very center of Auckland or Wellington, since public transport, while improving, doesn't yet match the density and reach of comparable European or East Asian cities, and importing a vehicle carries its own compliance costs worth researching before shipping anything from overseas rather than simply buying locally on arrival.
