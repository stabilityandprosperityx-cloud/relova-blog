For now, Oman remains genuinely tax-free on personal income — but that changes on a fixed, already-legislated date, which makes 2026 an unusually interesting year to plan around. Under **Royal Decree No. 56/2025**, Oman enacted a **Personal Income Tax Law**, a first for any Gulf state, that introduces a **flat 5% tax on annual income above OMR 42,000** (roughly **USD 109,000**) starting **1 January 2028**. Until then — through all of 2026 and 2027 — the 0% headline still holds for everyone. If your income sits meaningfully below that OMR 42,000 threshold, the practical effect on you may be minimal even after the law takes effect; if you are a senior executive or high-earning consultant, this is worth modeling now rather than discovering it as a surprise deduction in two years.

## Omanisation and what it actually means for your job offer

Most expat work in Oman still runs through employer sponsorship, and that sponsorship is shaped by **Omanisation** — sector-specific quotas requiring companies to employ a minimum share of Omani nationals before they can bring in foreign hires for remaining roles. Quotas vary meaningfully by industry, with sectors like banking, insurance, and certain retail categories carrying higher Omanisation targets than construction or specialized engineering roles where local talent supply is thinner. In practice this means your actual path in isn't a generic "Oman work visa" so much as a specific employer's specific sector-quota headroom — a job offer from a company that has already cleared its Omanisation ratio moves faster than one still working through it. Expats have historically made up somewhere around **40%** of Oman's total workforce, concentrated heavily in construction, oil and gas services, and domestic work — treat any single percentage as a snapshot, since Oman's own labor-nationalization push has been actively shifting that mix.

## Tax-free today, a five-percent ceiling from 2028

Because the new tax only bites above OMR 42,000 a year and only starts in 2028, most people relocating for 2026 or 2027 arrival dates get a genuine multi-year runway of zero personal income tax before any of this applies to them. The law does carve out several exemptions worth knowing even for higher earners: foreign-sourced income gets a one-time **two-year exemption** for new tax residents, gains on a primary residence held two years or more are exempt, and a secondary-residence exemption applies once in a lifetime. Tax residency itself triggers at **183 days** in a calendar year, whether consecutive or accumulated — the same test used across most of the region.

## Muscat rents without the Dubai brunch tax

Living costs in Muscat commonly run **30–40% below comparable Dubai spending**, and that gap holds up reasonably well once you strip out the specific expenses that inflate Dubai budgets — brunch culture, marina-adjacent premium rent, and a denser luxury retail scene that makes casual spending easy to lose track of. One-bedrooms in **Qurum**, Muscat's most beach-adjacent and internationally popular district, run roughly **OMR 200–350 a month**; **Al Mouj (The Wave)** and similar family-oriented developments track close behind; and **Ruwi**, the older commercial core, offers the cheapest end of central housing stock at roughly **OMR 120–220**, trading beach proximity for walkable, functional urban density and easier access to Muscat's older souks.

## Weekend geography that actually beats mall culture

Oman's genuine advantage over its Gulf neighbors is geographic: **Wadi Shab**, roughly a two-hour drive from Muscat, delivers canyon pools and hiking that no amount of Dubai mall infrastructure replicates, and the broader Hajar Mountains and coastal wadis give weekend life an outdoor dimension that Gulf expat life often lacks entirely. Banking and rental onboarding, though, still assume paper-heavy documentation — attested degree certificates and signed tenancy contracts show up repeatedly as friction points for new arrivals, so start collecting and getting documents attested before you land rather than scrambling for embassy authentication appointments once you're already trying to open an account.
