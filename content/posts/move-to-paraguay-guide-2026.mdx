Paraguay's pitch is genuinely simple compared to most Latin American residency routes: no minimum income requirement, no property purchase mandated, and a bank deposit you get back. That last point is the part most marketing pages bury — the roughly **$5,000** deposit required to open a temporary residency application sits in a Paraguayan bank account as proof of solvency, and it can be withdrawn once your residency is actually approved, which is a meaningfully different arrangement than a non-refundable government fee.

## What "three months" and "five years" actually mean

Temporary residency applications commonly process in **two to four months**, and once approved, the cédula (national ID card) itself takes roughly another **45 days**. That's the fast, genuinely achievable part. What relocation marketing routinely compresses is the path from there to citizenship: temporary residency runs for **two years**, after which you can apply for permanent residency, and citizenship eligibility generally requires a further **three years** on top of that — putting the realistic total closer to **five years** of Paraguay-based residency before naturalization, not the "three years to citizenship" figure some packages advertise, which appears to conflate residency milestones with citizenship. Citizenship processing itself can then add up to another two years, and typically requires demonstrating real physical presence — commonly cited as six to nine months a year in-country — plus Spanish or Guaraní language ability. Anyone shopping based on a "fast citizenship" pitch should read the actual year-by-year breakdown before paying for a package built around a compressed number.

## Legal packages and what they're actually charging for

All-in legal service packages for Paraguay residency commonly run **$5,500-8,000**, covering document preparation, local legal representation, and processing support — on top of, not instead of, the refundable ~$5,000 deposit. That combined figure (package fee plus deposit) is the honest first-year cash outlay, and it's worth getting an itemized breakdown from any firm you're considering rather than a single bundled number, since the deposit portion comes back to you and the service fee doesn't.

## Territorial tax, without the oversimplification

Paraguay's territorial tax system means foreign-sourced income is not subject to Paraguayan income tax — the core of the "0% foreign income" pitch, and it's substantively true. Domestic Paraguayan-sourced income is taxed at **8%** for amounts under roughly 120 times the monthly minimum wage, rising to **10%** above that threshold — genuinely low rates by regional standards, but not zero if you earn anything locally. There's no wealth, inheritance, or gift tax either, which is a real structural advantage over most Latin American and many European systems, but it doesn't mean Paraguay is a no-tax jurisdiction outright — get advice on your specific income sources before assuming the territorial framing covers everything you earn.

## Asunción rent and what a modest budget covers

A one-bedroom in Asunción commonly runs **$250-450** a month, genuinely low by regional and global standards, and it's the core of Paraguay's cost-of-living pitch for anyone earning foreign income while living locally. That said, Asunción is a smaller, less internationally connected capital than Buenos Aires or Santiago — fewer direct international flights, a smaller expat community, and less English spoken outside of a small professional and diplomatic circle — trade-offs worth weighing against the tax and cost advantages rather than assuming the low rent tells the whole story.
