Penang draws a different crowd than Kuala Lumpur — older, food-obsessed, more likely to be retired or running a location-independent business than chasing a corporate role — and the island's post-restructuring [MM2H](/blog/move-to-kuala-lumpur-guide-2026) program plus its genuinely cheap cost of daily living are why it keeps showing up on shortlists.

## [MM2H](/blog/retire-to-malaysia-guide-2026) after the restructuring: what the tiers actually require

Malaysia's My Second Home program was substantially restructured in recent years into a tiered, deposit-heavy structure. The **Silver** tier requires a **USD 150,000** fixed deposit and grants a 5-year renewable visa; **Gold** requires **USD 500,000** for a 15-year visa; **Platinum** requires **USD 1,000,000** for a 20-year visa and is the only tier that currently permits the holder to work locally. All tiers under-50 generally require a minimum 90 days of physical presence in Malaysia per year to maintain status, while applicants over 50 are typically exempt from that minimum-stay requirement — a meaningful distinction for retirees versus working-age applicants weighing which tier fits their actual travel pattern. Because MM2H's structure has been revised more than once in recent years, verify the live deposit figures and stay requirements against the official MM2H portal at filing time rather than a program description that may predate the most recent adjustment.

## Housing: Georgetown versus the newer expat belt

**Georgetown**, the UNESCO World Heritage old town, remains the character-heavy, walkable choice — a one-bedroom there typically runs **MYR 1,200–2,000** a month (roughly USD 260–430), reflecting its heritage-shophouse density and tourist-adjacent energy more than a premium finish. **Tanjung Tokong**, closer to the Gurney Drive commercial strip and popular with expat families for its newer condo stock and proximity to international schools, runs somewhat higher at **MYR 1,500–2,500**. **Batu Ferringhi**, the beach strip on the island's north coast, sits in a similar band to Tanjung Tokong but trades city-center convenience for sea views and heavier tourist-season traffic. None of these prices include the deposit and stamp duty conventions Malaysian landlords typically expect upfront — usually two months' deposit plus half a month's rent as an agency fee, standard practice islandwide.

## The bridge and the ferry are not interchangeable commutes

The **Penang Bridge**, at roughly 13.5 km, connects the island to the mainland (Seberang Perai) and carries the bulk of daily car and motorbike commuter traffic, which backs up predictably during peak hours — a real consideration if your MM2H property search extends to mainland Penang for lower prices, since the bridge commute back to Georgetown can eat more time than the map distance suggests. The passenger ferry between Georgetown and Butterworth remains the calmer, more scenic alternative for anyone not driving, though it is not a practical daily commute option for most jobs given its schedule and capacity limits.

## Daily costs stay genuinely low

Penang's hawker food culture is not a tourist gimmick — it is how most residents, local and expat, eat most meals, and a proper plate of char kway teow or laksa typically runs **MYR 5–15** (roughly USD 1–3), a price point that keeps daily food costs remarkably low even for people who eat out constantly. That said, private healthcare (a real consideration since MM2H requires proof of health insurance or sufficient means) and imported goods carry Western-adjacent pricing, so a realistic Penang budget blends genuinely cheap local living with a handful of expenses that don't discount the way street food does.
