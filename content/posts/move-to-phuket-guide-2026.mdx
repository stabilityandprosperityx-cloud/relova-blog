## Visa routes that fit Phuket

**DTV** is the practical remote-worker default in 2026. **LTR (Long-Term Resident)** runs up to **10 years** for income from about **$80,000/year** or investments from **$500,000**, with a preferential **17%** tax rate. **Non-Immigrant O-A** (age **50+**) needs about **THB 800,000** in the bank **or** income from **THB 65,000/month**—it is not a work visa. **Thailand Elite** memberships last **5–20 years** from about **600,000 THB** entry and do not require ongoing income proof the same way—but they are not a remote-work permit by themselves.

## Budgets, neighborhoods, and schools

Comfortable solo living spans roughly **$1,200–2,500/month** depending on housing tier. A good-location studio/one-bed is often **12,000–25,000 THB/month (~$330–700)**. International schools run about **THB 200,000–450,000/year** per child (other quotes **$5,500–21,000**). Popular bases: **Bang Tao/Laguna** (families, international schools), **Rawai/Nai Harn** (quieter expat pockets), **Kathu** (more inland value).

## Banking, insurance, and property rules

Bank account opening tightened: many branches want a long-term visa (LTR/Smart/Non-B) plus a Certificate of Residence—**DTV** holders often need an agent. Many visas require insurance, commonly framed as **400,000 THB** inpatient / **40,000 THB** outpatient minimums. Foreigners may own condominium units (building foreign ownership capped at **49%**) but **not** land directly; villas usually sit on **30-year** renewable leases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best Phuket visa for remote workers in 2026?**

Usually **DTV**: ~**$280**, ~**$15k–16k** bank evidence, **180+180** days, remote work allowed without a Thai work permit.

**How much should a single person budget?**

About **$1,200–2,500/month**, with decent one-beds often **12,000–25,000 THB**.

**Can DTV holders open a Thai bank account easily?**

Not always—rules tightened; many banks prefer longer visas plus a Certificate of Residence. Budget agent help if needed.

**Can foreigners buy a villa on Phuket?**

You can own a **condo** unit (within the 49% building cap) but not land outright; villas are typically **30-year** leases.

**Where should families look?**

**Bang Tao/Laguna** for schools; **Rawai/Nai Harn** for quieter expat life; **Kathu** for lower rent inland.
