Quito sits at 2,850 meters, and that single fact shapes more of your first month than any visa form does. New arrivals commonly get winded climbing a flight of stairs, sleep poorly for the first week or two, and find that a glass of wine hits differently than it does at sea level. It passes — most people adjust within two to four weeks — but budget for a slow start rather than hitting the ground running. Quito's payoff for the altitude tax is real: it has Ecuador's deepest job market, the country's main international airport (Mariscal Sucre, UIO), the strongest healthcare system in the country, and a far larger expat and diplomatic community than [Cuenca](/blog/move-to-cuenca-ecuador-guide-2026), which pulls most of the retiree traffic instead.

## Where to live and what it costs

**La Mariscal** is the classic first landing spot — the nightlife-and-hostel district bordering Parque El Ejido, walkable to language schools, coworking spaces, and the U.S. embassy. One-bedrooms run roughly USD 350–600/month, and it's noisy on weekends. **La Floresta**, just south, trades some of that energy for a more residential, art-gallery, specialty-coffee feel at a similar or slightly lower price point (USD 300–550), and has become the preferred base for remote workers who want quiet evenings without leaving the center. **Cumbayá**, a valley suburb about 20–30 minutes east of the historic center (lower altitude, warmer, greener), runs higher — USD 400–700 for a one-bedroom, more for family-sized housing — and is where a large share of international-school families and corporate transferees settle, partly because the altitude drop makes daily life gentler on newcomers.

## The Rentista visa and other residency routes

Ecuador's most common route for people living on passive income is the **Rentista visa**, which requires proof of recurring non-employment income — pensions, dividends, rental income, or annuities — equal to at least three times the country's **Salario Básico Unificado (SBU)**, the unified basic wage that Ecuador resets each year. With the 2026 SBU at USD 482/month, the Rentista threshold works out to roughly **USD 1,446/month**, a meaningfully higher bar than the flat figures often quoted online from a few years back. Applicants who prefer to route through work or investment instead can look at the **Professional visa** (tied to a registered professional qualification) or the **Investor visa** (a real-estate or bank-deposit threshold, also indexed to the SBU). All applications route through Ecuador's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Cancillería) visa portal, and in-country processing typically runs a few weeks to a couple of months once documents — apostilled income proof, a clean criminal record certificate, and a local address — are in order.

## Health, altitude, and daily logistics

Quito's two flagship private hospital systems, Hospital Metropolitano and Hospital de los Valles, both handle English-speaking patients regularly and are the default recommendation for expats setting up a local doctor relationship. Private health insurance premiums for a healthy adult typically land in the USD 60–150/month range depending on age and coverage, and are worth arranging before — not after — you need them, since pre-existing condition exclusions are standard. On altitude specifically: hydrate more than feels necessary in the first two weeks, go easy on alcohol, and don't schedule strenuous activity (or a Cotopaxi hike) for your first few days in the city.

## Quito vs. Cuenca: which fits which profile

The comparison expats run most often is Quito against Cuenca, Ecuador's other major expat hub, and the honest answer depends on what you're optimizing for. Cuenca has the larger, more established retiree community, a more walkable historic center, and a gentler cost of living overall; Quito has the deeper job market, the country's main international gateway airport, better private healthcare infrastructure, and access to a wider range of professional and business opportunities. If you're still working — remotely or for a local employer — Quito's connectivity and pace generally serve that better. If retirement and a slower, more social expat scene are the goal, Cuenca's lower altitude (2,550m versus Quito's 2,850m) and established [Pensionado](/blog/retire-to-ecuador-guide-2026)-visa community make the adjustment easier on both budget and body.
