Romania’s **digital nomad visa** (Law **22/2022**, from January 2022) is still open in 2026 — one of the earlier Eastern European products that did not quietly expire. The income test is **three times the national average gross salary** at filing, about **€3,700–4,100 a month** (~**18,500 RON**) at the start of 2026, with **six months** of proof. On **renewal** the bar drops to **one times** average salary. That step-down is the part forums skip. It is also why some people wait to file until their trailing six months look clean rather than padding a weak month with a one-off invoice.

## Filing threshold versus renewal threshold

Bring six months of remote-employment or business income that clears the 3× line. Contracts, bank statements, and tax certificates in a language the IGI will accept matter more than a pretty coworking photo. Insurance must cover at least **€30,000**. The medical certificate (“Nomad Checkup”) is routinely done at private networks such as **Regina Maria, Medicover, or Medlife** in Bucharest; the residence-card fee is about **€170**. Do the checkup in-country if that is what the current checklist wants; do not assume a PDF from your home GP will pass.

Renewal at 1× average is why some people file at the high bar knowing year two is easier — if the programme still exists in that form. Always re-check the order in force the month you apply. A 2022 Telegram screenshot of “€3,000 is enough” is not 2026 law.

Flat **10%** personal income tax is the resident headline. Local business wrappers are **PFA** (sole trader) or **SRL** (company) with a more optimised corporate rate if you actually operate in Romania — that is a tax-advisor file, not a visa stamp. The nomad visa is a stay basis; it is not automatically a 10% deal on foreign payroll unless you have actually become a Romanian tax resident under the rules that apply to you.

## Schengen since 1 January 2025

Romania is a **full Schengen member** as of **1 January 2025**, including land borders. A Romanian residence card is a **free-movement credential across 29 Schengen states**, not a Romania-only cage. That is the 2026 upgrade versus the 2022 launch: you are not collecting extra stamps to visit Vienna for a client meeting. It also means your **90/180** tourist arithmetic is the wrong mental model once you hold the card — you are a resident of a Schengen state, with the duties that come with that, not a backpacker collecting days.

Permanent residence is typically discussed at **five years** of legal stay; **EU citizenship** at **ten**. Those clocks assume clean renewals, not creative overstays. Ten years is a long time to discover you never filed a tax return you were supposed to file.

## City budgets

Comfortable Bucharest, rent included, is often **€1,000–1,800 a month**. **Cluj-Napoca** and **Timișoara** run **10–20% cheaper**. Cluj is the other IT gravity well — useful if you want a smaller centre and still want English in offices. A one-bed in prestige Bucharest pockets (**Floreasca, Dorobanți, centre**) is **€450–800**. Cluj is similar or a shade less. International health insurance is **€50–120 a month** on top of the visa minimum — the €30,000 policy is the legal floor, not necessarily the plan you want after a ski weekend.

The average local net wage is not your wage; do not use it as a lifestyle target if you are paid in euros from abroad. It *is* a reminder that a €800 Floreasca one-bed is a local luxury, not a “cheap Eastern Europe” default.

## First-month cash

Budget **€3,000–4,500** beyond flights: two to four weeks of short-let, deposit, and a relocation fixer if you do not speak Romanian. That buffer is how people avoid signing a bad annual lease in the first 72 hours. Add the residence-card fee and the checkup so the “visa is cheap” story does not eat the emergency fund.

Romania in 2026 is **3× to file, 1× to renew, Schengen-wide movement, 10% PIT, and a Bucharest envelope under €1,800 if you are not collecting designer rents**. The visa is still open. The paperwork is still Romanian. Start the six-month income file before you book the one-way ticket.
