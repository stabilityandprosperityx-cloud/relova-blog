Scotland does not run its own immigration system, and that's worth stating plainly because a genuine, ongoing political push might make it look otherwise if you only skim headlines. Immigration remains a **reserved power held by Westminster**, not the Scottish Parliament — every visa route covered in our general [UK moving guide](/blog/move-to-uk-guide-2026), including the £41,700 Skilled Worker salary floor, applies in Scotland exactly as it does in England. What's different about Scotland is the cost of living relative to what that same UK visa and salary actually buy you, plus a live political proposal worth watching rather than a working program.

## A Scottish visa is a proposal, not a route you can apply for

As of August 2026, Scotland's government has published proposals for a Scottish Visa, a Rural Visa, and a Scottish Graduate Visa, framed as part of a "100 Days" policy push and aimed at addressing rural depopulation and skills gaps specific to Scotland. None of these exist as an actual application route today — they require UK government sign-off because immigration is reserved, not devolved, and Westminster has not adopted them. If you see content claiming Scotland has "its own visa" in 2026, treat it as premature at best; the accurate framing is that Scotland is actively lobbying for one, and the outcome of that lobbying is genuinely uncertain and worth checking for updates before you plan around it.

## Edinburgh's rent versus what it buys

A one-bedroom flat in Edinburgh commonly runs **£1,000-1,300** a month — real money, but a clear step down from London's £1,800-2,800 range for equivalent space, and that gap alone is why Edinburgh pulls a disproportionate share of UK-wide remote and hybrid workers who don't need to be in London five days a week. New Town and Stockbridge sit at the premium end of that range with Georgian architecture and walkability to the city centre; areas further out trade some of that character for lower rent. Edinburgh's economy leans heavily on financial services, law, and the August festival season's tourism surge — worth knowing if you're job-hunting locally rather than relocating with a remote role already secured.

## Glasgow trades some polish for more room and lower rent

Glasgow's one-bedroom average sits close to **£900** a month, with a typical range of roughly **£800-1,150** — genuinely cheaper than Edinburgh, and noticeably so once you're outside the West End, which carries its own premium for proximity to the university and the city's strongest concentration of cafes and independent shops. Glasgow's creative-industries and startup scene has real momentum, and the city's student population keeps a steady churn of rental stock moving, which can work in your favor if you're flexible on exact neighborhood and timing.

## NHS Scotland, IHS, and the surcharge you still owe

NHS Scotland is free at the point of use once you're legally resident — but getting there still means paying the UK-wide **Immigration Health Surcharge**, **£1,035 per year of visa validity per adult**, upfront as part of your visa application, same as anywhere else in the UK. Scotland's devolved government funds and runs its own NHS board structure, but it does not set or waive the IHS, which is a UK Home Office charge tied to your immigration status, not a Scotland-specific health fee.
