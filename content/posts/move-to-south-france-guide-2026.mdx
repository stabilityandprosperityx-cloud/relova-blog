Southern France is not one market. **Côte d’Azur** (Nice, Antibes, Menton) has the densest English-speaking clubs and the highest tourist friction. **Provence** (Aix-en-Provence, Marseille, Luberon villages such as Gordes and Ménerbes) is quieter, more dinner-party than networking event. **Occitanie** (Montpellier, Toulouse) spreads anglophones thinner across universities and aerospace. All of the big southern cities undercut **Paris** on rent and purchase. None of them is a digital-nomad visa product — you are on standard French residence rules.

## Three regions, three social maps

Pick Côte d’Azur if you need English-language doctors, association calendars, and a coast that already knows expats. You pay for that in housing and summer crowds. Nice, Antibes, and Menton are the practical triangle: hospitals, airports, and a social calendar that does not require you to invent the expat club. Pick Provence if you want stone villages and a smaller pond — Marseille for a real city and port grit, Aix for a polished centre, Luberon villages such as Gordes and Ménerbes if you can drive and will not need a metro. Pick Occitanie if you want students, TGV, and less “Riviera performance”: Montpellier and Toulouse spread English speakers across campuses and aerospace parks, which is lonelier in month one and more French in month twelve.

Paris remains the comparison, not the neighbour. You can still **TGV Paris–Marseille in about three hours**, **Paris–Lyon in two**, with early fares from **€19**. Inside the southern cities, trams and buses with an unlimited pass from about **€35 a month** replace a car for many people. A car is still the Luberon tax; do not pretend a village life is a tram life.

## What housing actually costs

A central **one-bedroom in Marseille or Bordeaux** is often cited around **€600–700 a month**, versus **€1,200–2,000** in central Paris (and Paris rent is still described as about **40% below London**). That gap is the reason people leave the capital after the first winter.

Purchase medians for ready housing: around **€468,000** near Toulon, **€400,000** in Marseille/Aix, **€418,000** in Montpellier. In an expat-heavy Provençal village such as **Cotignac**, two-bedroom houses are advertised from about **€190,000** — a different asset class from a Nice sea-view. Do not average those numbers into one “south of France price.”

Lease in French, with a proper inventory. Summer furnished lets are not winter unfurnished lives. Agency fees and a deposit still land in the first month even when the advertised rent looks like a bargain versus the 8th arrondissement.

The purchase numbers are for **ready housing**, not a ruin-with-a-view. A Cotignac two-bed from **€190,000** is not a Nice renovation budget. If you are comparing “south of France” listings in one tab, sort by *département* first.

## VLS-TS and what changed for 2026

Non-EU long stays usually start with **VLS-TS** (Visa Long Séjour valant Titre de Séjour): it is both the visa and the **first one-year residence title**. You must **validate it online via ANEF within three months of entry**. Miss that and you are an overstayer with a pretty sticker.

From **2026**, multi-year cards (**carte de séjour pluriannuelle**) expect a **French A2** test plus civic knowledge. The residence-card fee moved from about **€200 to about €300** — treat **€300** as the planning number and confirm the prefecture tariff. This is not optional culture homework; it is the renewal filter. Book the language test before the prefecture appointment, not after a refusal letter.

EU citizens skip the VLS-TS theatre and still need CAF, a French bank, and a lease that a prefecture will recognise. Non-EU remote workers do not get a Côte d’Azur nomad visa; they get the same long-stay machine as everyone else.

## Healthcare and a monthly envelope

After **three months of legal residence**, **PUMa** can reimburse **70–80%** of standard care. Most people still buy a **mutuelle at €40–130 a month** for the rest. Comfortable living is often cited from **€1,800 a month** in a provincial city versus **€2,500+** in Paris. The south usually sits under the Paris floor if you avoid peak Nice — and still sits above a Balkan capital if you eat like a tourist every night.

Keep private travel cover until PUMa is actually attached to your number. Three months is a calendar, not a feeling. Pharmacies are excellent; specialists in English are a Côte d’Azur and university-town story more than a village story.

Southern France in 2026 is **region first, visa second**: Côte d’Azur vs Provence vs Occitanie, then VLS-TS plus A2, then a mutuelle on top of PUMa. The €600–700 Marseille one-bed is the number that makes the TGV from Paris worth it.
