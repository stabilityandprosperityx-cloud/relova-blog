**move to Stockholm 2026** now hinges on a sharper work-permit salary floor. From **1 June 2026**, a standard **arbetstillstånd** needs **90% of Sweden’s median wage**—**SEK 33,390/month**—up from the old **80%** band (~**SEK 29,680–30,640**). Sweden still has **no digital nomad visa**; you enter through employment, **EU Blue Card**, or self-employment tracks.

## The 1 June 2026 salary-floor shock

New work-permit filings from **1 June 2026** must clear **SEK 33,390/month** (90% of median). A transition helps some renewals: if your permit was issued **before** 1 June 2026 and you file the renewal between **1 June and 1 December 2026**, the old **80%** rule can still apply. From **1 December 2026**, renewals also need the new floor. About **152 shortage occupations** (medicine, many IT roles, etc.) can use a **75% of median** threshold instead of 90%—check the current list before you negotiate an offer. ## Blue Card, insurance, and permanent residence

**EU Blue Card** salary stays **SEK 52,000/month**, but validity is extended from two to **four years**. From **1 June 2026**, people staying **under one year** must hold **full private medical insurance** as a separate requirement. Permanent residence typically follows **four years** of continuous permit-based stay within the last seven. ## Stockholm cost of living and tax

A comfortable solo Stockholm budget is often **$2,000–2,950/month** (~**SEK 22,000–32,000**). Taxes are steep: municipal rates near **~32%**, plus up to **25%** state tax on income from about **SEK 642,100**, so marginal rates can approach **~57%**. Model net pay, not just the gross that clears Migration Agency thresholds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the new Swedish work-permit salary from June 2026?**

**SEK 33,390/month** (90% of median), up from the previous ~**SEK 29,680–30,640** (80%) band.

**Is there a transition for renewals?**

Yes. Permits issued before **1 June 2026** renewed between **1 June and 1 December 2026** can still use the old 80% rule. From **1 December 2026**, renewals need the new floor.

**Does Sweden have a digital nomad visa?**

No. Plan on employment, Blue Card (**SEK 52,000/month**, now up to **4 years**), or self-employment routes.

**What changed for short stays and insurance?**

From **1 June 2026**, full medical insurance is required if you will stay **less than one year**.

**How expensive is Stockholm, and how long to PR?**

Budget roughly **$2,000–2,950/month** comfortable solo living. Permanent residence is commonly after **four years** of continuous qualifying permits.
