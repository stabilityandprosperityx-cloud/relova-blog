Sydney's rental market hit its tightest point in years in early 2026, with vacancy sitting around 1.3% in February — which is the actual reason one-bedroom asking rents have pushed toward **AUD 750 a week** (roughly **AUD 3,255 a month** once you calculate it properly, not the naive weekly-times-four shortcut that undercounts by a full week's rent a year). Melbourne runs cooler by comparison, generally landing a few hundred dollars a month below Sydney for an equivalent one-bedroom, with its more extensive tram network doing real work to make outer, cheaper suburbs genuinely liveable without a car — a trade-off Sydney's geography doesn't offer nearly as well.

## The rent gap is about geography, not just supply

Sydney's harbour-and-inlet layout means desirable, transit-connected neighborhoods are genuinely scarce — the Eastern Suburbs and Inner West trade at a real premium (Bondi-area listings commonly clear AUD 920 a week) while outer-ring suburbs offer relief at the cost of longer commutes across a city that wasn't built on a grid. Melbourne's flatter, tram-gridded layout means more of the city is reasonably commutable, which is the practical reason its rent curve is gentler even before you account for the underlying supply numbers. If your budget is the deciding factor between the two cities, Melbourne's advantage isn't just the sticker price — it's how much more of the city that price actually opens up.

## Working Holiday just got a two-tier fee structure

From 1 July 2026, Australia introduced a genuinely new wrinkle to the Working Holiday visa (subclass 417/462): a **first** application costs **AUD 840**, but a **second or third** Working Holiday visa now costs **AUD 1,000 each** — plus a AUD 9-15 payment processing fee on top either way. This is a meaningful change from the flat-fee structure many older guides still describe, and it specifically penalizes the people extending their working holiday across multiple years rather than first-timers. Age eligibility still gates the whole program (broadly under-31, with some country-specific variations), so check your passport's specific bilateral arrangement before assuming the standard cutoff applies to you.

## Skilled visas run on a shifting points bar, not a fixed number

Australia's points-tested skilled visas (subclass 189 independent, 190 state-nominated, 491 regional) don't have one static cut-off — invitation rounds happen periodically through the program year and the minimum score that actually gets an invitation moves round to round based on demand in your occupation and how many people are competing in that round. A "65 points is enough" claim you read somewhere is only ever true for a specific round in a specific occupation category — check current SkillSelect invitation round results for your actual occupation before treating any points number as a target rather than a moving line.

## Medicare doesn't start the moment you land

Medicare, Australia's public health system, generally becomes available once you hold eligible permanent residence — not on arrival, and not automatically with a work visa, however senior the job. Anyone on a Working Holiday, Skilled Worker (temporary), or similar visa needs private health cover from day one, and several countries have reciprocal healthcare agreements with Australia that cover some emergency care but not the comprehensive coverage PR grants — check whether your home country has one of those agreements, but don't assume it replaces proper insurance while your PR application is still in process.
