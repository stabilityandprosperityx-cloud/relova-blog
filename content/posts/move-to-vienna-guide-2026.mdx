Vienna consistently ranks among the world's most livable cities, and for non-EU professionals the path in runs through Austria's points-based immigration system rather than a simple work-visa stamp. Understanding how that system scores you — and what changes once you've been in the country a while — matters more for Vienna than for almost any other European capital.

## The Red-White-Red Card and the Blue Card alternative

Most non-EU talent enters through the **Red-White-Red Card (RWR)**, a points-based system that scores applicants on qualifications, work experience, language ability, and age. Highly qualified applicants need **70 out of 100** points, while the shortage-occupation and key-worker categories have a lower bar of **55 out of 90**. Outside the points categories, "other key workers" need to show a minimum gross salary of at least **€3,465 a month** in 2026 to qualify. For applicants who'd rather use the EU-wide route, the **EU Blue Card** is available with an income floor of **€55,678 a year gross**, calculated including Austria's customary 13th and 14th salary payments — a detail that trips up applicants used to a 12-payment salary structure elsewhere in Europe. First cards issued under either route last **24 months** and are tied to the sponsoring employer, meaning a job change during that period generally requires a fresh application rather than a simple transfer. For the full points breakdown and document checklist, see the dedicated [Austria Red-White-Red Card guide](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/austria-red-white-red-card-guide-2026).

## From employer-tied to free labor market access

The employer lock isn't permanent. After **21 months** of qualifying employment, cardholders become eligible to apply for **RWR Card Plus**, which removes the employer tie entirely, opens up the full Austrian labor market, and is valid for **three years**. That fee, worth noting for budgeting purposes, rose to **€218** from **1 January 2026**, up from €212 previously — a small but real cost to plan for alongside the emotional relief of no longer being tied to one employer. From there, permanent residence — **Daueraufenthalt-EU** — typically follows after **five years** of continuous legal stay across the RWR and Plus periods. Citizenship sits further out: the standard track is **10 years**, though Austria allows a fast-tracked **6-year** route for applicants who can demonstrate exceptional integration, such as advanced language proficiency and civic engagement. ## Language requirements and processing timelines

German fluency isn't a legal requirement to file an RWR application, but it functions as a practical one: roughly **73%** of job listings that matter to RWR applicants expect at least **B2** proficiency, which is a genuinely high conversational bar. The formal requirements step up as you progress through the system — **A2** is required for RWR Card Plus, and citizenship applications typically expect **B1**. On timing, official processing guidance quotes up to **eight weeks**, but in practice, applicants commonly report **6–12 weeks** depending on the regional immigration office and how complete the initial submission is — building slack into your relocation timeline for this variability is worth doing rather than assuming the official figure.

## Living costs and getting around Vienna

Housing costs split fairly predictably by location. Central-district one-bedroom apartments run **€800–1,400 a month**, averaging close to **€1,100**, while outer districts — still well-connected by Vienna's excellent public transit — run somewhat lower, typically **€700–1,200**. A comfortable solo lifestyle budget, covering rent, transit, food, and some discretionary spending, spans a wide range from **€2,200–3,600 a month** depending on neighborhood and habits. On transit, Vienna's long-running and famous **€365-a-year** public transport pass ended after 2025; the current **Jahreskarte** annual pass now costs **€467 a year**, still an exceptional value compared to most European capitals for unlimited access to trains, trams, and buses across the city.
