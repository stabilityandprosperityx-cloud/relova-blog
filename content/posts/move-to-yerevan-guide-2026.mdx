Yerevan’s 2026 cost-of-living numbers do not agree with each other. That is not a rounding error. It is two different lives — a frugal month of cooking and marshrutkas versus a private flat plus dinners out — sold as one city. Treat every total as a **scenario**, not a promise. Then look at the visa clock, the honest limits of the internet-and-community stack, and whether **Gyumri, Vanadzor, or Dilijan** is the better first lease.

## Cost of living — and why the spreadsheets disagree

A cost-of-living index around **53.1 (New York = 100)** is a useful headline: Yerevan is cited as about **47% cheaper** than New York on that scale. It does not tell you which café you used.

Budget-oriented totals often land **$679–779 a month**. Other sources put a “comfortable” private-housing, eat-out-regularly life at **$531–744** — which overlaps the budget band and should make you suspicious of labels. Wider baskets (coworking, private insurance, more taxis, imported groceries) push some estimates to **$1,441**. All of these can be “true” for someone. None of them is *the* Yerevan number.

Rent is the lever. A **Kentron** (centre) one-bedroom is commonly **$700–1,100**. Outside the centre, **$335–750**. If your “comfortable $744” includes a $1,000 Kentron lease, the rest of the month does not exist. If it assumes a $400 outer-district studio, dinners out suddenly fit.

Local wages sit far below many remote incomes: average **net** pay in Yerevan is about **$646–682**. That gap is why the 2022 IT relocation wave could reprice restaurants and coworking without lifting every Armenian salary. Your budget is not the city’s budget.

Private international health cover is a separate line: **$40–100 a month** for a basic plan. Put it in the $1,441 basket, not the $679 one, unless you are gambling on cash clinics.

## Visa, stay length, and what “nomad” actually means

Citizens of the **EU, US, Canada, Australia** and many other countries can enter **visa-free for up to 180 days per year**. That is a visit clock, not residence.

Armenia also issues a temporary residence permit around **105,000 AMD (~$270)** and a permanent one around **140,000 AMD (~$360)**, with processing often near **two months**. A separate **nomad-style stay of up to 180 days a year** is described in public materials — confirm the current immigration circular before you treat it as a second 180 on top of visa-free entry. Do not stack slogans.

Taxes for remote workers usually run through a local IE / turnover setup if you register; the old “5%” talking point in blogs must be **re-checked** against 2026 law, not copied from a 2023 Telegram post. This article is not tax advice.

## Infrastructure: short stay versus long stay

The post-2022 wave of Russian and Belarusian IT specialists **did** thicken coworking, international restaurants, and a small startup scene. That is real. It is also not Lisbon.

Nomad reviews in 2026 still split: enough bandwidth for **basic remote work**, often **below** the fibre-and-5G floor of top European or Southeast Asian hubs; a thinner entertainment and nomad-community stack if you need a new dinner party every week. Several long-stay people conclude the city is excellent for a **cultural season** and awkward as a **two-year base** unless you already have local friends or a partner. Believe the people who lasted six months, not the people who lasted six days.

That is not a reason to skip Yerevan. It is a reason to book **three months**, not a two-year unfurnished lease, until you know whether the café-and-cascade circuit is enough. The 2022 wave built more desks than it built a Lisbon-scale scene. Test the upload on a real video call from the flat you might rent, not from a hotel lobby.

## Cheaper Armenian cities if Yerevan is the wrong scale

**Gyumri**, the second city, is often **25–35% cheaper** than Yerevan. **Vanadzor** and **Dilijan** undercut the capital while keeping more infrastructure than a village. Dilijan trades for nature and a smaller English bubble; Gyumri for space and a slower café scene.

Use them if rent in Kentron at $700–1,100 wrecks the $679 story. Do not use them if your job needs same-day in-person clients in Yerevan.

Yerevan in 2026 is a **cheap-or-not-cheap** city depending on the lease you sign, a **180-day** visa-free default for many passports, and a hub that grew fast after 2022 without becoming a top-tier nomad OS. Price the scenario, not the index.
