Zanzibar is a **semi-autonomous** island of Tanzania with a tourism economy measured in arrivals: from about **19,000** visitors in 1985 to roughly **917,000** in **2025**. Almost every foreigner-facing job, lease, and café sits on that stack. There is still **no launched digital-nomad visa** in 2026 — it is discussed, not issued. Most foreigners enter on a **business or tourist visa** and only later convert to a work or residence permit if an employer or investment file exists.

If you want island weather without pretending it is Lisbon-on-the-Indian-Ocean, read the residence routes, then Stone Town versus the beach belt, then a budget that survives tourist pricing, then the limits people skip in brochures.

## Residence: C11, condos, and the missing nomad visa

The **Class C11 Investor Residence Permit** (from **2024**) is a renewable **two-year** card for investors putting at least **$100,000** into a project approved by **ZIPA** (Zanzibar Investment Promotion Authority). Buying in an **approved development** can count. That is an investment immigration path, not a laptop stamp.

Owners of units under the **Condominium Act** can use a residence route **without incorporating a company**. Cited fees: about **$500–550** for the main applicant (a reduced **~$250** band for some **EAC / diaspora** citizens), **$50 per dependant**, first grant **two years**, renewable while you still own. Confirm the current ZIPA/immigration circular before you wire a developer.

Everyone else: tourist or business entry, then a **work or residence permit** only if you have a qualifying job or investment. Remote work for a foreign company on a tourist stamp is the usual grey area — plan the clock, do not assume a nomad product will appear before your visa expires.

## Where to live: Stone Town versus the beach

**Stone Town** is the urban bet: shops, somewhat more stable internet, hospitals that are still limited but closer than a north-coast villa. One-bedrooms often start around **$300–600**; boutique historic stock can hit **$1,000–1,500**. You trade humidity and maze streets for being able to buy a SIM and see a pharmacist without a 40-minute bajaji.

Beach belts — **Paje, Nungwi, Kengwa** and neighbours — are the postcard. Long-lets commonly **$700–1,000** for something you would actually work from; villas run **$1,500–10,000+**. Power cuts, generator culture, and “the fibre is coming” are part of the rent. Beautiful, and operationally a resort town even when you hold a local lease.

Choose Stone Town if you need errands and a desk. Choose the beach if your work is async and you accept generator budgets. Mixing both as a first lease usually means you pay beach prices for Stone Town logistics.

## A realistic monthly budget

Published bands for 2026:

- Solo / nomad: **$1,000–3,000** depending on district and how often you eat like a guest
- Couple / family: **$2,000–4,500+**
- Bare-bones local-price living **outside** tourist strips: sometimes cited around **$580–650** — treat that as a cook-at-home, no-villa, no-coworking figure, not a digital-nomad all-in

Stack rent from the section above, then add generator fuel or UPS, bottled water habits, island-priced imports, and a **medical-evac** line. The $580 story and the $3,000 story are both “Zanzibar”; they are not the same life.

## Honest limits before you book

The labour market for foreigners **outside tourism and hospitality** is thin. If your plan is “I’ll find local clients,” you are competing for a small pie. Healthcare is a planning constraint, not a footnote: serious cases still mean **evacuation to Nairobi or Dar es Salaam**. Buy cover that pays for that transport, not only a clinic visit in Stone Town.

Internet and power are the other quiet filters. Stone Town is usually the less painful desk. Beach villas look like a productivity fantasy until the generator and the “technician tomorrow” cycle starts. If your income depends on daily calls, test the exact unit at the hour you actually work — not at noon on a site visit — before you pay a deposit on a $1,500 villa.

Zanzibar in 2026 is a tourism island with two residence products (C11 at **$100k**, condo-linked permits at a few hundred dollars) and **no nomad visa**. Live in Stone Town or on the beach with your eyes open — the 917,000 arrivals built the economy you are joining, and they also set the prices.
