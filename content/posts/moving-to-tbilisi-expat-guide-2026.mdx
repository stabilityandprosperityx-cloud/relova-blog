This guide covers the city-level logistics of actually living in Tbilisi once you're there — neighborhoods, transport, and first-week setup — rather than visas or tax mechanics, which are covered in our [Georgia visa/residency overview](/blog/how-to-move-to-georgia-country) and [Georgia tax deep dive](/blog/georgia-digital-nomad-tax-visa-deep-dive-2026).

## Choosing a neighborhood: Vake, Saburtalo, and Old Town are the three real contenders

Vake is Tbilisi's most polished residential district — wide tree-lined streets, Vake Park, a concentration of good cafes and international schools, and rents to match, typically $600–900 for a well-finished one-bedroom, sometimes more in newer buildings. Saburtalo is the practical middle ground: a large, sprawling district with strong metro access, a big student and young-professional population, more modern apartment stock, and rents that run noticeably lower than Vake, often $400–650 for a comparable one-bedroom. Old Town (Kala/Abanotubani area) offers the atmosphere everyone pictures — cobbled streets, sulfur bathhouses, balconied historic buildings — but the housing stock is older, tourist rental competition pushes prices up in central pockets, and day-to-day practicality (parking, modern plumbing, noise) is more mixed. Vera, adjacent to Vake, splits the difference: central, walkable, well-served by cafes, with rents generally in the $500–800 range.

## Getting around: the metro is fast, marshrutkas fill the gaps

Tbilisi's metro has two lines and is by far the fastest way to cross the city during rush hour, with fares paid via a rechargeable Metromoney card that also works on city buses. Marshrutkas — shared minibus routes — cover areas the metro doesn't reach and are cheap but require knowing the route number system, which has a learning curve for newcomers; asking locals or using a transit app is faster than trying to memorize routes from scratch. For point-to-point trips, Bolt and Yandex Go both operate extensively in the city and are considerably cheaper than equivalent rides in Western Europe, making car-free living genuinely workable even outside the metro-served core.

## Opening a bank account: plan for one to two visits, not one

TBC Bank and Bank of Georgia are the two accounts most expats end up with, and both have reputations for being foreigner-friendly relative to many countries — some branches can open a basic account same-day with just a passport. In practice, expect to bring proof of a local address (a signed lease is usually sufficient) and be prepared for occasional additional questions about the source of incoming funds, especially for larger transfers. English-language service varies by branch; central branches in Vake and Saburtalo tend to have more English-speaking staff than outer-district branches.

## Getting a SIM card and internet set up

A local SIM (Magti, Geocell, or Beeline are the three main carriers) can typically be purchased same-day at a carrier store with just a passport, and prepaid data plans are inexpensive by regional standards. Home internet installation through the same carriers or dedicated ISPs is generally reliable in Vake, Saburtalo, and central districts, with installation taking anywhere from a couple of days to about a week depending on whether the building already has existing lines.

## Apartment hunting: expect to negotiate, and check the building directly

Most rental listings run through local platforms (myhome.ge is the dominant one) and Facebook expat groups rather than international listing sites, so it's worth searching in both places. Deposits are commonly one month's rent, sometimes negotiable, and many landlords prefer or require payment in USD rather than GEL for longer-term leases, which is worth clarifying upfront since it affects your effective cost if the lari moves against your home currency. Viewing the building itself matters more in Tbilisi than in newer housing markets — older Soviet-era buildings can have excellent renovated interiors behind unremarkable exteriors, or the reverse, so photos alone are a poor guide.
