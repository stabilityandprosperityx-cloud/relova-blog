Choosing between [Naples](/blog/move-to-naples-guide-2026), [Rome](/blog/move-to-rome-guide-2026), and Milan is really a choice about what kind of Italian life you want, not just which city has the best postcards. All three sit under the same national visa rules, so the deciding factors are cost, job market depth, climate, and how much bureaucracy and chaos you're willing to tolerate in exchange for character and affordability.

## The same legal baseline, three different realities

Whichever of the three cities you choose, the core legal pathway for non-EU movers doesn't change: Italy's **Digital Nomad Visa** applies a national income threshold, commonly cited around **€28,000 a year**, along with health insurance, accommodation proof, and the standard apostilled-document packet that any Italian visa route requires. Because the legal baseline is identical, the meaningful differences between these three cities show up entirely in cost of living, job market character, and day-to-day quality of life — which is where this comparison is actually useful, rather than in visa mechanics that are the same no matter which city you land in.

## Cost of living: a genuine north-south gap

The cost gap between these three cities is large enough to materially change your monthly budget. Naples sits at the affordable end, with one-bedroom rents commonly in the **€600–1,000** range — the lowest of the three by a clear margin — but that affordability comes paired with a thinner formal job market, meaning fewer white-collar and international-company roles relative to Rome or Milan. Rome occupies the middle ground, with rents typically **€900–1,500**, offering a genuine balance between culture, career options, and the bureaucratic friction Rome is famous for; it's the city most likely to satisfy people who want "real Italy" without sacrificing too much job market depth. Milan sits at the top of the cost range, with rents commonly **€1,200–1,800**, reflecting its position as Italy's actual economic capital — it has by far the strongest job market of the three, particularly in finance, fashion, design, and corporate multinational roles, but that strength comes at Northern European prices layered on top of Southern European bureaucracy. For remote workers and digital nomads rather than locally-employed movers, monthly budgets across all three cities tend to range **€1,500–2,500**, with the exact figure depending heavily on which city and neighborhood you choose within that range.

## Climate, pace, and who each city actually suits

Beyond the numbers, these three cities have genuinely different personalities that matter as much as cost for a long-term move. Naples offers the warmest climate of the three, the most affordable cost of living, and an intensity and authenticity that some expats love and others find overwhelming — it's a city that rewards people who want unfiltered Italian life and are comfortable with more visible urban chaos and a less polished infrastructure than Rome or Milan. Rome sits in the middle climatically and culturally: warm enough for genuine year-round outdoor living, with the depth of history and culture that draws people to Italy in the first place, but also home to some of the country's most notorious administrative bureaucracy, which can make even routine paperwork — a SPID digital identity, a codice fiscale, a residency registration — take longer than it reasonably should. Milan is the coolest and greyest of the three climatically, more Central European in feel than classically Mediterranean, but it's also the most efficient of the three cities to actually live in — public transport runs reliably, professional services are faster, and the job market is deep enough that career-focused movers often find it the easiest of the three to build a long-term life around, even if it's the least "postcard Italy" of the choices.
