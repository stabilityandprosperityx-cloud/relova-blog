For expats and digital nomads earning through their own business rather than an employer, the jurisdiction you incorporate in can matter as much as the country you actually live in. Company formation costs, tax treatment, and ongoing compliance burden vary enormously across the handful of jurisdictions that dominate the expat and remote-founder conversation, and picking the wrong one can mean years of unnecessary tax drag or compliance headaches.

## Estonia's e-Residency and the 0% retained earnings model

Estonia has built one of the most genuinely foreigner-friendly company formation systems in the world through its **e-Residency** program, which lets non-residents register and manage an Estonian **OÜ** (private limited company) entirely online. Setup costs are commonly cited around **€190**, and the structure's standout feature is Estonia's distinctive corporate tax model: profits are taxed at **0%** as long as they're retained in the business rather than distributed as dividends, meaning founders who reinvest earnings rather than pulling money out immediately can defer corporate tax indefinitely. This makes Estonia particularly attractive for founders still growing their business and not yet drawing significant personal income from it, though it's worth remembering that tax owed in your actual country of tax residence is a separate question entirely — an Estonian OÜ doesn't exempt you from your personal tax obligations wherever you're considered tax resident.

## UK, Wyoming, Singapore, and UAE alternatives

The UK's **LTD** structure remains one of the cheapest and fastest company formations available anywhere, with online registration fees often as low as **£12** through Companies House, and same-day or next-day incorporation in many cases — its appeal lies in speed, low cost, and the credibility of a UK-registered entity for clients and banking purposes, though UK corporate tax and reporting obligations apply once the company is trading. In the US, a **Wyoming LLC** is a popular choice among non-resident founders specifically because Wyoming charges no state income tax and keeps annual costs low, typically around **$100** for the base state renewal fee, making it a favorite for founders who want a simple US entity for invoicing US clients or building US business credibility without the higher fees charged by states like Delaware or California. For founders targeting Asia-Pacific clients or wanting a jurisdiction with strong banking infrastructure, **Singapore's Pte Ltd** structure is commonly cited with registration fees around **SGD 300** and a **17%** corporate tax rate — notably lower than most Western jurisdictions, paired with Singapore's reputation for stable, business-friendly regulation. In the Gulf, **UAE free zone** company formations, such as those through **IFZA** (International Free Zone Authority), often start around **AED 12,500 per year**, offering 100% foreign ownership and, in many free zones, no corporate tax below a certain profit threshold — an increasingly popular option for founders relocating to Dubai or Abu Dhabi specifically to combine personal tax residency with a local company structure.

## Matching the jurisdiction to your actual situation

The right jurisdiction depends heavily on where you'll actually be tax resident, who your clients are, and how you plan to draw income from the business. A founder living in a country with favorable personal tax treatment for foreign-sourced or company dividend income might get real benefit from Estonia's retained-earnings structure; a founder who needs fast, cheap incorporation purely for invoicing purposes might do better with a UK LTD or Wyoming LLC. Before committing to any structure, build a document matrix tracking incorporation dates, registered agent renewals, and any annual filing deadlines specific to your chosen jurisdiction — missed annual filings are one of the most common and avoidable sources of company formation regret, often resulting in late fees or, in worse cases, involuntary dissolution of the entity.
