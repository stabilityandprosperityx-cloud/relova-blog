Whether your pension keeps growing once you leave the country depends almost entirely on your passport, and the two biggest expat populations — Americans and Britons — face genuinely different rules. ## How U.S. Social Security travels with you

Most American retirees can keep receiving Social Security while living overseas, but "most" is doing real work in that sentence — a handful of countries (Cuba and North Korea, plus a few former Soviet states without a totalization or payment agreement) are excluded or require special arrangements. Before you move, run your destination through the Social Security Administration's Payments Abroad Screening Tool, which gives a country-specific answer rather than a rule of thumb. If you're cleared, International Direct Deposit will route payments to a local bank account in dozens of countries with U.S. banking arrangements, which is far more reliable than relying on a U.S. account and a wire transfer every month.

The part that trips people up isn't eligibility — it's the paperwork that keeps it running. Roughly every one to two years, SSA mails (or emails, if you've registered for electronic delivery) a questionnaire confirming you're still alive, still at that address, and still eligible. Ignore it, even by accident because it went to an old address, and payments simply stop until you respond. Build a standing reminder to check your SSA correspondence a few times a year, especially if you move within your destination country.

A genuinely good development for anyone who spent years in public-sector work: the Social Security Fairness Act, signed into law on 5 January 2025, repealed the Windfall Elimination Provision and Government Pension Offset — two rules that had reduced benefits for people who also collected a pension from work not covered by Social Security (many teachers, firefighters, and federal employees, for instance). The repeal applies to benefits payable from January 2024 onward, and most affected retirees saw a lump-sum back-payment land around April 2025, followed by a higher ongoing monthly amount. If you were affected by WEP or GPO and haven't seen an adjustment, it's worth contacting SSA directly.

One distinction that causes real financial pain if missed: Supplemental Security Income (SSI) is not the same program as Social Security retirement benefits, and it does not travel abroad the same way. SSI is a needs-based program, and payments generally stop after a full calendar month — commonly cited as 30 consecutive days — outside the United States. If you're planning a move based on SSI income, that plan doesn't work; SSI is built around U.S. residency. Tax-wise, moving your Social Security abroad also raises U.S. filing questions — see [U.S. expat taxes](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/us-expat-taxes-guide-2026) for how foreign income interacts with your return.

## The UK State Pension's frozen-pension problem

British retirees face a structurally different — and for many, worse — situation. The UK State Pension only continues to rise with annual inflation and wage adjustments (the "triple lock") if you live in the UK, the EEA, Switzerland, or one of a specific list of countries with a reciprocal social security agreement that includes uprating, the United States being the most prominent example. Move to Australia, Canada, New Zealand, South Africa, or most of the Commonwealth outside that list, and your pension freezes at whatever amount it was when you first claimed it — even as UK inflation erodes its value year after year. This affects an estimated 500,000 British pensioners worldwide, and it's a policy that campaigners have fought for decades without success.

For 2026/27, the full new State Pension for those still receiving uprating sits at roughly £230.25 to £241.30 a week depending on when the annual increase takes effect. To qualify for the full amount you generally need 35 qualifying years of National Insurance contributions, with a minimum of 10 years required for any payment at all. If you're short on years and living abroad, voluntary Class 3 contributions can still fill gaps, but the cheaper Class 2 voluntary rate — historically available to people who were employed or self-employed abroad — is being phased out, with the concessionary rate ending in April 2026. After that, gap-filling gets noticeably more expensive. Annual "life certificates," a periodic proof-of-life check similar to the SSA questionnaire, also apply to overseas State Pension recipients, and a missed one stalls payments just as surely as an unanswered SSA form. Keeping your home-country paperwork current matters as much as the move itself — see [maintaining home-country ties](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/maintain-home-country-ties-living-abroad-2026) for a broader checklist.

## Where both systems actually fail

In practice, neither system usually fails on legal technicalities — it fails on administration. An unanswered SSA questionnaire, a missed UK life certificate, or a retiree who assumed SSI rules applied to their Social Security retirement benefit are the three most common causes of a pension interruption. None of these are hard problems to solve; they just require a calendar reminder and a habit of opening official mail before you book six months of travel.
