Everything in Portuguese freelancing starts with a NIF (Número de Identificação Fiscal) — the tax number that unlocks a Finanças online password, a bank account, and eventually a rental contract. Once you have it, activating recibos verdes ("green receipts," the freelance invoicing system) means logging into the Portal das Finanças and declaring your activity under Category B (independent business/professional services, the vast majority of freelancers) or Category H (a narrower list of liberal professions with specific rules). This registration is what makes your invoices legal and triggers your Social Security enrollment automatically.

## How the simplified tax regime actually works

Most freelancers under the revenue caps use the regime simplificado, which doesn't tax full revenue — instead it applies an automatic deemed-expense deduction (commonly 75% for most services, varying by activity code) so you're taxed on roughly the remaining 25% of declared revenue under [Portugal](/blog/lisbon-freelancer-setup-guide-2026)'s progressive IRS income tax bands. This is generous enough that many freelancers never bother tracking real expenses in detail, though it's worth comparing against the organized-accounting regime once revenue grows, since actual deductible costs can sometimes beat the flat 75% assumption. IFICI (the newer regime replacing the old NHR for eligible newcomers) offers a flat 20% rate on Portuguese-source employment and self-employment income in qualifying high-value-added activities, for a set number of years — foreign-source income treatment differs and depends on the specific income type and any applicable tax treaty, so this needs case-by-case verification with a Portuguese accountant rather than assumed.

## Social Security and the invoicing mechanics

Segurança Social contributions for freelancers are calculated at 21.4% applied to 70% of relevant quarterly income (not the full amount), which works out to an effective rate closer to 15% of gross income for most people — still a meaningful monthly obligation that catches newcomers who budget only around income tax. Since certified electronic invoicing became mandatory in 2018, freelancers use software like InvoiceXpress, Moloni, or PHC to issue recibos verdes, typically costing €7–20 a month depending on invoice volume and features. One detail that surprises people billing Portuguese companies: many corporate clients are legally required to withhold 25% of the invoice value as advance tax, paid directly to Finanças on your behalf and credited against your annual IRS bill — it reduces your cash flow up front even though it isn't an extra tax. The annual IRS return deadline generally falls by the end of June for the prior tax year, and it's the point where over- or under-withholding through the year gets reconciled.

## When the simplified regime stops making sense

The regime simplificado has revenue caps (currently in the low six figures in euros annually) beyond which freelancers must move to organized accounting (contabilidade organizada), which requires a certified accountant (contabilista certificado) and tracks real expenses rather than the flat deemed deduction. This transition usually happens naturally as a freelance business scales into something closer to a small company, and it's worth having a Portuguese accountant model both regimes side by side before the switch becomes mandatory, since organized accounting can sometimes produce a lower tax bill for freelancers with genuinely high deductible business costs (equipment, travel, subcontracted work) that exceed what the 75% deemed-expense assumption would credit them for.

## Practical setup sequence for newcomers

For anyone arriving without an existing Portuguese tax history, the practical order matters: get the NIF first (either in person at Finanças, through a Portuguese consulate before arrival, or via a fiscal representative service used by many non-EU applicants), then open a Portuguese bank account, then activate the Finanças online password needed to file recibos verdes and access IFICI or other regime applications online. IFICI applications specifically have their own deadline tied to the calendar year of becoming a Portuguese tax resident, so timing residency registration and the IFICI application together — rather than treating them as sequential, unrelated tasks — avoids missing a window that, once passed, generally can't be recovered for that tax year.
