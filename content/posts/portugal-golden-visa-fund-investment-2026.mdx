Portugal's [Golden Visa](/blog/buying-property-portugal-guide-2026) isn't the real-estate program it was known for through the 2010s. Since the "Mais Habitação" reforms took effect in October 2023, direct and indirect residential property investment no longer qualifies at all — the fund route is now effectively the only mainstream path remaining for most applicants, alongside smaller-volume options like job-creation and cultural-donation investments.

## Why funds are now the default route

The qualifying investment-fund route requires a minimum subscribed capital of €500,000 into a fund registered and regulated by the CMVM (Comissão do Mercado de Valores Mobiliários, Portugal's securities market regulator). Crucially, since the 2023 reform, these funds are barred from investing more than 20% of their capital directly or indirectly in Portuguese real estate — the fund route was deliberately redesigned to channel [Golden Visa](/blog/portugal-golden-visa-requirements-2026) capital toward productive sectors like venture capital, private equity, and business expansion rather than the housing market that the 2023 reform was meant to relieve pressure on. Dozens of CMVM-registered funds currently court [Golden Visa](/blog/athens-vs-lisbon-expat-guide-2026) investors, but not all are created equal: due diligence on fund manager track record, fee structure, and underlying portfolio composition matters far more here than it did when the calculation was simply "buy an apartment in Lisbon."

## The process and what it actually costs beyond the €500k

After selecting and subscribing to a qualifying fund, applicants apply through AIMA (Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo, which absorbed the old SEF's immigration functions), typically holding a Portugal-linked visa stamp while the residence permit application processes. AIMA processing timelines have been cited in the 12-18 month range for [Golden Visa](/blog/lisbon-vs-barcelona-expat-guide-2026) applications specifically, reflecting a persistent backlog that predates the agency's 2023 restructuring — build that delay into any planning, since it affects when the residency clock and eventual citizenship eligibility actually start. Beyond the €500,000 principal, budget for legal and application fees (commonly several thousand euros per applicant plus dependents), the fund's own management fees (often 1.5-2.5% annually plus a performance fee), and the mandatory minimum-stay requirement — historically an average of seven days per year over the residence permit's validity, one of the lowest physical-presence requirements of any residency-by-investment program in Europe.

## The citizenship timeline is genuinely in flux — verify before you commit

This is the fact most older Golden Visa content gets wrong for 2026: Portugal's nationality law has been under active revision, with legislative changes moving to lengthen the standard naturalization residency period for non-EU, non-CPLP applicants from the historic five years toward a longer window — reporting through 2025-2026 has cited both a seven-year track for EU/CPLP nationals and a ten-year track for most others, with the residency clock now proposed to start from AIMA's permit issuance date rather than the application filing date. This directly affects Golden Visa investors, many of whom priced the €500,000 commitment partly against a five-year citizenship timeline. As of this writing, implementation details, retroactivity for pending applications, and a reported constitutional challenge from Golden Visa investor groups remain unresolved — treat any specific "five years to citizenship" claim as outdated, and confirm the current legal status directly with Portuguese immigration counsel before treating citizenship timing as a fixed input to your investment decision.

## Returns are not guaranteed — read the fund documents

CMVM regulation of a fund covers structural and disclosure compliance, not investment performance. Advertised target IRRs in the 3-8% range are projections from fund managers, not contractual guarantees, and Golden Visa funds carry the same illiquidity as any private capital vehicle — your €500,000 is typically locked up for a 5-6 year minimum hold period regardless of fund performance, with exit only through fund maturity, secondary sale (often difficult), or occasionally an early-redemption clause that comes with penalties.
