Choosing among **Portugal**, **Spain**, and **Georgia** is less about which country is “best” and more about which constraint you refuse to compromise: monthly burn, visa friction, tax on foreign-source income, or a long-term citizenship clock. Here is how the three stack up with **2026** numbers.

## Monthly burn: what a realistic solo or couple budget looks like

**Portugal** starts around **€1,500/month** for one person outside peak tourist traps; couples away from Lisbon’s hottest postcodes often land **€2,500–3,000/month**.

**Spain** starts near **€1,600/month** solo. Households or couples commonly need **€2,700+/month**. Comfortable life in **Valencia** or **Seville** often sits in the **€1,600–2,200/month** band if you are disciplined on rent.

**Georgia (Tbilisi)** is the clear cost outlier: **$800–1,200/month** per person including rent is still realistic for many remote workers.

On overall living costs, **Portugal runs roughly 20% cheaper than Spain**, with the gap clearest in housing, utilities, and everyday services. Georgia undercuts both Iberian options by a wide margin if your priority is runway, not Schengen convenience.

## Tax reality (where your invoice actually lands)

**Spain** uses progressive personal income tax. Corporate tax is generally **25%**, with a **15%** relief rate for new companies in early periods.

**Portugal** is also progressive for individuals. Small and medium companies can use a preferential **17%** rate on the first **€25,000** of company profit — useful if you incorporate locally rather than staying a pure foreign contractor.

**Georgia** is the radical option for registered freelancers on the **Small Business** status: **1% tax** on qualifying foreign-source income. Among the three, it is the lowest structural tax barrier if you can run clean books and stay inside the rules.

## Visa friction and how long you can stay

**Portugal**

- **D7** (passive income): about **€920/month** after the January 2026 minimum-wage uplift.
- **D8** (digital nomads): about **€3,680/month**.
- Citizenship timing changed: under the Nationality Law promulgated in **May 2026**, most applicants now face **10 years**; EU and CPLP nationals typically **7 years**.

**Spain**

- **Non-Lucrative Visa**: roughly **€28,800/year** (400% of IPREM).
- **Digital Nomad Visa**: about **€2,849/month** after the February 2026 SMI rise.

**Georgia**

- Visa-free entry for **365 days** for many nationalities — the lowest entry barrier of the three.
- Living there for a year does **not** by itself mint residence or a citizenship path; you still need a separate residence strategy if permanence matters.

## Housing price signals in the capitals

Iberian capitals sit in a similar purchase-price conversation: Spain roughly **€3,300–5,700/m²**, Portugal roughly **€3,200–6,100/m²**, depending on property type and neighbourhood. Georgia’s rental affordability is the bigger daily-life win for most newcomers; buying in Tbilisi is a different decision than renting a furnished flat for the first year.

## Who should pick which country

| Priority | Better fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Larger local labour market & higher local salaries | **Spain** | Bigger domestic economy; DNV / NLV if you qualify |
| Lower Iberian burn + clear residence routes on income proof | **Portugal** | D7/D8 still readable; costs ~20% under Spain |
| Minimal tax + bureaucracy, accept weak automatic PR path | **Georgia** | 365-day entry + 1% small-business lane |

If you need Schengen mobility and a structured residence title in year one, Iberia wins. If you need twelve months of low burn while you test remote life, Georgia wins — just do not confuse a long tourist-style stay with a citizenship plan. Map your passport, income source, and five-year goal before you fall in love with a city photo.


Lifestyle fit still decides more moves than spreadsheets. Spain’s larger domestic market helps partners who want local employment, not only remote invoices. Portugal’s smaller scale can feel calmer and cheaper, but Lisbon and Porto rents have compressed the “cheap Iberia” myth for newcomers who insist on city-centre flats. Georgia’s social scene for remote workers is real in Tbilisi, yet public services, banking KYC, and long-term residence planning remain DIY compared with EU residence titles.

Also separate **tax residence** from **immigration status**. A Spanish DNV or Portuguese D8 does not automatically optimise your home-country filing; Georgia’s 1% small-business lane only helps if you actually register and invoice correctly. Run the three-country comparison once for visas, once for monthly burn, and once for five-year citizenship — the winner often changes between those three lenses.
