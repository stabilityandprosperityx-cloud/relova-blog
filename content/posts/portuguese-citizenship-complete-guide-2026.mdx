Portugal's [citizenship](/blog/eu-citizenship-guide-2026)-by-[naturalization](/blog/french-citizenship-naturalization-guide-2026) process changed materially in 2026, and anyone researching this route needs the current rules, not the widely-repeated "five years" figure that circulated for most of the last decade.

## The residency-year requirement just changed

Under the previous regime, foreign nationals could apply for Portuguese citizenship after five years of legal residence — the shortest timeline of any major EU country, and the reason Portugal became such a popular [naturalization](/blog/italian-citizenship-naturalization-guide-2026) target alongside its Golden Visa and D7 visa programs. That changed with **Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026**, which entered into force on **19 May 2026** and extends the residency requirement to **10 years for most nationalities**, with a reduced **7-year requirement for citizens of CPLP countries** (the Community of Portuguese Language Countries — Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde, and others) and EU/EEA nationals. Critically, the clock is now defined as starting from the date your **first residence permit** was issued, not the date you first apply for citizenship — a distinction that has already tripped up applicants doing their own math. If you filed your citizenship application with the IRN (Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado) before 19 May 2026, you remain grandfathered under the old five-year rule; anyone filing after that date, even if they'd already banked five years of residence, falls under the new extended timeline. This does not affect Portugal's separate permanent residency track, which still becomes available after five years — only the [naturalization](/blog/netherlands-citizenship-naturalization-guide-2026)/citizenship timeline moved.

## The language and civics requirement

Regardless of which residency track applies, applicants must pass **CIPLE** (Certificado Inicial de Português Língua Estrangeira), a basic A2-level Portuguese proficiency exam administered through the Centro de Avaliação de Português Língua Estrangeira network, or hold an equivalent recognized qualification. The exam fee runs around EUR 75, and A2 is a genuinely reachable bar for most applicants with six months to a year of consistent study — it covers everyday communication, not fluency. There's no separate civics or history test in Portugal's process, which puts it well below the bar countries like Germany or the Netherlands now set.

## Fees, documents, and processing time

Total government fees for a citizenship application, including the nationality registration fee paid to the Conservatória, typically land in the EUR 250–500 range depending on family composition and whether minors are included. The heavier cost, in practice, is document assembly: an apostilled birth certificate, a criminal record certificate from every country you've lived in for the relevant period, and proof of the residency history itself — gaps or unregistered address changes are the most common cause of delay. Processing timelines have run **12 to 24 months** in recent years due to backlog at the Conservatória dos Registos Centrais, and the 2026 law change is widely expected to add further pressure to that queue as advisors and applicants recalibrate strategy.

## What this means if you're mid-process

If you already hold a residence permit and were planning around the old five-year timeline, the practical question is whether your existing residence history counts toward the new 7- or 10-year requirement — and the honest answer, per the law as written, is yes: time already accrued under a valid residence permit counts, it's simply that the total bar to clear moved higher for anyone filing after 19 May 2026. That makes the filing date, not the accrual date, the pivotal detail worth confirming with an immigration lawyer if you're close to a threshold. Portugal also permits dual citizenship without requiring you to renounce your existing nationality, which remains unchanged by this law and is part of why the country stays attractive despite the longer runway — you're not trading one passport for another, just adding one, whenever the residency clock finally runs out.
