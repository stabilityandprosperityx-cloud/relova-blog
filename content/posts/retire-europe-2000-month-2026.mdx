$2,000 a month is a genuinely comfortable retirement budget in a handful of European and near-European destinations, and a tight one in others carrying the same "Europe" label — the difference comes down almost entirely to which specific city you pick, not which country's average cost-of-living statistic you glanced at.

## The Balkans: the strongest value cluster

Southeastern Europe consistently delivers the best rent-to-quality ratio on the continent for retirees working with a fixed monthly budget. Sofia, Bulgaria's capital, offers one-bedroom apartments in the €400–600 range, paired with EU membership, decent healthcare infrastructure, and a genuinely low overall cost of living that lets $2,000 stretch across rent, food, transport, and health insurance with room left over. Belgrade, Serbia, runs slightly higher at €450–650 for a one-bedroom but adds a livelier capital-city atmosphere and strong connectivity across the Balkans and into Western Europe. Tirana, Albania, has emerged as one of the cheapest capital-city options in Europe at €350–550, though it comes with less mature healthcare and financial infrastructure than Sofia or Belgrade — worth weighing if ongoing medical needs are a factor. Bucharest, Romania, sits at €500–700, offering EU membership and a larger, more diversified economy than Tirana or Sofia, at a modest premium.

## Portugal's interior: EU comfort without coastal prices

Portugal's Algarve and Lisbon get the marketing attention, but the country's interior — particularly the Alentejo region — offers a genuinely different value proposition: one-bedroom rentals often run €450–700, a fraction of coastal or Lisbon pricing, while still delivering EU residency rights, the Schengen zone, and access to Portugal's public healthcare system once you're a legal resident. The trade-off is real: Alentejo is rural and slower-paced, with less English spoken and a longer drive to major hospitals or international airports than Lisbon or the Algarve offer. For retirees prioritizing budget and quiet over urban amenities and beach proximity, it's one of the strongest euro-for-euro deals inside the EU itself — and it qualifies for the same D7 passive-income visa route that applies across Portugal.

## Georgia: the non-EU wildcard

Outside the EU entirely, Tbilisi, Georgia, offers one-bedroom rentals around $350–600 alongside one of the most retiree-friendly entry policies anywhere — many Western passport holders get a full year of visa-free stay, no formal retirement visa application required to simply arrive and live there for that window. Georgia's territorial tax system also generally doesn't tax foreign-sourced pension income, an appealing detail for retirees living off overseas pensions or investments. The catch is that Georgia sits outside the EU and Schengen area, which matters for anyone who wants easy onward travel across Europe or who eventually wants an EU-based long-term residency status; it also means healthcare and consumer-protection frameworks differ meaningfully from EU norms, so due diligence on private insurance and hospital quality matters more here than in an EU destination.

## Why "Europe" is the wrong unit of budgeting

A $2,000 monthly budget genuinely doesn't translate into one story across "Europe" — it's the difference between a comfortable two-bedroom with savings left over in Sofia or Tirana, and a tight, compromise-heavy budget in Lisbon proper or most of Western Europe's capital cities. The practical approach is to build your budget city-first, not country-first: get real rent comps for the specific neighborhood you're considering, price local health insurance (since even EU residents typically want supplemental private cover for faster access and English-speaking providers), and build in a genuine contingency — 15 to 25% above your baseline estimate — for currency swings, one-off setup costs in the first few months, and the inevitable gap between what a listing advertises and what you actually negotiate to.

## Sequencing the move itself

Whichever city you land on, treat the visa and the budget as parallel tracks rather than sequential ones: match your income evidence to the specific residency category you're pursuing (EU countries like Portugal, Bulgaria, and Romania have their own passive-income or retiree visa mechanics; Georgia's visa-free entry sidesteps this for the first year but requires a longer-term plan if you intend to stay past it), and keep a document matrix tracking apostille status and expiry dates so paperwork doesn't stall your move once you've committed to a lease.
