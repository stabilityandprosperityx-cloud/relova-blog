## Investment citizenship: what the marketing figures actually mean

Citizenship-by-investment (CBI) marketing sells speed above everything else, and **Vanuatu** is the clearest example: agencies routinely advertise the programme from **~$130,000** with processing in as little as **30–60 days**, making it the fastest CBI route most planners encounter. That speed comes with real due diligence to do on your own account before treating it as a silver bullet — Vanuatu's visa-free access to the UK, EU, and other destinations has been suspended and reinstated more than once in recent years amid EU pressure over CBI due-diligence standards, and some banks have grown cautious about opening accounts for Vanuatu-passport holders whose primary residence and income ties remain elsewhere. Verify current visa-free access and banking acceptance directly rather than relying on a sales deck.

**Malta**'s citizenship-by-investment/naturalization-for-exceptional-services route sits at the opposite end of the spectrum: all-in planning figures typically start around **€690,000+** once you include the direct contribution, real estate or rental commitment, and required donation, and the process involves a genuine residency period and rigorous due diligence rather than a same-quarter turnaround. It is the most expensive mainstream option but also the one offering EU citizenship, which is a meaningfully different product than a Caribbean or Pacific passport.

**Turkey** occupies a middle ground: the government's real-estate-based citizenship route is commonly cited at a **$400,000** minimum property purchase held for a **three-year** minimum period before resale, after which processing has historically been faster than the EU programmes. Confirm the current title-transfer rules, appraisal requirements, and resale/liquidation mechanics before wiring funds, since Turkish authorities have tightened valuation scrutiny on qualifying properties in past cycles.

## Citizenship by descent: Italy, Ireland, Portugal, and Poland

Descent-based citizenship is the cheapest route for people who actually qualify, but "cheapest" does not mean "fast." **Italy**'s **jure sanguinis** principle is well known for having no generational cap in most legal interpretations — if you can document an unbroken citizenship chain back through your ancestors (and that ancestor never naturalized elsewhere before a child was born, among other technicalities), you may qualify regardless of how many generations separate you from Italy. In practice, consulate appointment queues in high-demand jurisdictions like the US and Argentina have stretched to multiple years, which has pushed many applicants toward filing directly through Italian courts instead — slower to start but often faster to resolve than waiting for a consulate slot.

**Ireland** allows claims through a great-grandparent in some circumstances, though the exact chain depends on whether your intervening ancestor registered their own Irish birth before you were born — a detail that trips up a lot of otherwise-eligible applicants. **Portugal** and **Poland** both maintain descent-based paths for qualifying lineages, generally requiring you to prove the family connection through original vital records, apostilles, and certified translations — budget real time and money for document-gathering regardless of which country's programme you pursue, since archival requests from small municipal registries can take months to fulfill.

## Naturalization: the slow, ordinary path

Standard naturalization — becoming a citizen through years of lawful residence — remains the route most people actually end up using, even if it gets less attention than CBI headlines. It typically requires several years of continuous legal residence, language proficiency tests, and sometimes a civics exam, and the exact timeline varies enormously by country. Treat forum timelines and anecdotal "I got mine in three years" posts as data points, not guarantees, since processing speed depends heavily on your specific consulate, case backlog, and personal documentation quality. If you are already pursuing residency-by-investment routes like a [Portugal Golden Visa](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/portugal-golden-visa-requirements-2026), naturalization eligibility is usually a downstream benefit worth mapping out early rather than an afterthought.

## Scams, compliance, and why banks ask sharper questions now

The second-passport industry attracts opportunists, and the tell is usually a promise of speed and price that no legitimate programme can match. If an agent guarantees an impossibly fast timeline for a suspiciously low fee, or pressures you to wire funds before due diligence checks are complete, treat that as a red flag rather than a bargain. Separately, banks worldwide have become far more aggressive about correlating a new passport with a coherent source-of-funds narrative — a second citizenship obtained through investment, without a matching paper trail explaining where that investment money came from, is now one of the fastest ways to trigger account freezes or enhanced due diligence requests. Keep your financial documentation as clean and complete as your immigration file.

## How a second passport interacts with tax residency

Getting a second passport does not, by itself, change where you owe taxes. Tax residency is generally determined by physical presence, center-of-vital-interests tests, or formal registration — not by which passport is in your pocket.
