**Singapore EntrePass entrepreneur 2026** is MOM’s founder visa for people building an innovative Singapore Pte Ltd—not a generic work pass. You must hold at least **30%** of the company, clear one of the innovativeness tracks, and plan for renewal metrics (local hires and business spend) from day one. Unlike Employment Pass, EntrePass has **no minimum salary** floor (EP commonly starts near **SGD 5,600/month**).

## What EntrePass is (and is not)

EntrePass is for founders who will operate a Singapore company with a credible innovation story. It is not a shortcut for coffee shops, food courts, bars, massage parlours, employment agencies, and similar restricted categories unless you can prove a genuinely innovative model. If you mainly need salaried employment, compare [Move to Singapore](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/move-to-singapore-guide-2026) and Employment Pass rules instead of stretching EntrePass.

## Ownership, ACRA timing, and capital

You need at least **30%** shareholding in a Singapore Pte Ltd. The company should be registered with **ACRA** and, at filing, usually **under six months old**—or not yet incorporated. Formal paid-up capital can be as low as **SGD 1**, but many advisers recommend about **SGD 50,000** so renewal officers see real skin in the game. There is still **no statutory minimum salary** on EntrePass itself.

## Innovativeness criteria you must clear

Satisfy **at least one** track:

1. Funding of at least **SGD 100,000** from an accredited VC or business angel  
2. Ownership of registered intellectual property  
3. Active participation in a Singapore government–recognised incubator or accelerator  

Weak “me-too” retail concepts without one of these anchors are the usual rejection pattern.

## Duration, renewals, and the path to PR

The first pass is typically **1 year**. The first renewal is often **12 months**; later renewals commonly run **24 months** each. Renewal evidence usually includes at least **2 local employees** (rising to **4** from the second year in many briefings) and **total business spending** of about **SGD 100,000/year** (about **SGD 150,000** from year two). After **two successful renewals**, founders often evaluate a **permanent residence (PR)** filing—eligibility is never automatic.

## Processing time and restricted businesses

Plan on roughly **8 weeks** of processing from a complete application to a decision. Keep [company setup abroad](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/open-company-abroad-expat-2026) and [Singapore vs Hong Kong](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/singapore-vs-hong-kong-expat-guide-2026) in parallel if you are still choosing a hub: EntrePass only pays off when the Singapore entity is the real operating company, not a paper shell set up purely to hold a visa.

Putting the full picture together: you need at least 30% shareholding in an ACRA-registered Pte Ltd, one of the three innovativeness tracks (VC/angel funding of at least SGD 100,000, owned IP, or an accredited incubator/accelerator seat), no minimum personal salary requirement, and roughly SGD 50,000 in recommended (if not formally mandatory) paid-up capital. The first pass runs one year, the first renewal typically another 12 months, and later renewals commonly stretch to 24 months each — provided you can show at least 2 local hires (rising to 4 from year two in typical guidance) and about SGD 100,000 in annual business spending (SGD 150,000 from year two). After two successful renewals, many founders start evaluating a PR filing, though MOM and ICA treat that as a separate, non-automatic decision.

A detail that trips up many first-time applicants is that EntrePass evaluators are explicitly looking for a business model that could plausibly generate export revenue or serve a market beyond Singapore's own small domestic base — a food delivery app targeting only Singaporean consumers reads very differently to an assessor than a SaaS platform with a credible path to regional or global customers, even if the two businesses have similar early revenue. If your business model is genuinely local-facing, expect closer scrutiny of your innovativeness evidence, and lean harder on IP ownership or incubator backing to offset that concern.

It's also worth separating EntrePass from Singapore's other founder-adjacent visa options before committing. Some entrepreneurs are better served by starting on an Employment Pass as a director-employee of their own company if they don't yet clear the innovativeness bar, then transitioning to EntrePass once the business has matured — a sequencing decision worth discussing with an immigration consultant rather than assuming EntrePass is the only route into running a Singapore company as a foreign founder.
