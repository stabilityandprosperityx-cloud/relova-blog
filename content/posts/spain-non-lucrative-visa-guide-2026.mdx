**Spain [non-lucrative visa](/blog/retire-to-spain-guide-2026) 2026** is Spain’s passive-income residence route: you prove you can live without working in Spain, then renew on a 1+2+2 cadence if you actually spend enough days in the country. The 2026 financial floor for a solo applicant is **€28,800 per year** (400% of IPREM at €600/month). Remote work for a foreign employer is **not** allowed on this visa—use Spain’s digital nomad route if you need to keep earning.

## Who the [non-lucrative visa](/blog/lisbon-vs-madrid-expat-guide-2026) is for

This visa fits retirees, investors, and anyone living on savings or other non-Spanish employment income who will **not** work in Spain—including remote roles. Consulates in 2025–2026 tightened scrutiny on that work ban. If your plan depends on a laptop job paid abroad, switch to the [Spain digital nomad visa](https://blog.relova.ai/blog/spain-digital-nomad-visa) instead of forcing NLV paperwork.

## 2026 financial thresholds

IPREM remains **€600/month** (unchanged since 2023). Solo applicants need **€28,800/year**. A spouse or partner adds **€7,200/year**; a child typically adds **€3,600/year** (often framed as 50% of IPREM—confirm your consulate’s checklist). That means a couple needs about **€36,000/year**, and a couple with one child about **€43,200/year**. Private health insurance with **€0 deductible** is mandatory for every person on the file.

## Work ban vs digital nomad visa

NLV: no Spanish employment and no remote work for a foreign employer. Digital nomad visa: remote work is the point, with a 2026 income floor near **200% of the minimum wage (~€34,188/year)**. Pick the route that matches how you actually earn money, not the one with the nicer brochure.

## Fees, processing, and first-month TIE

Expect **2–4 months** from appointment to decision, with consulate variance. Indicative consular fees: United States **$140** (U.S. citizens) / **$106** (other nationalities applying in the U.S.); United Kingdom **£516 + £9.45**; Canada **CAD $1,085 + $35.40**. After entry, obtain your **TIE** residence card within about **one month**—do not treat the visa sticker as the end of the process.

## Renewals, 183-day rule, and long-term status

Initial authorization is **1 year**, then renewals typically run **+2 years, then +2 years**. From **20 May 2025**, Real Decreto 1155/2024 restored the **183-day** physical-presence expectation for renewal; the soft period of living mostly outside Spain is closed. Temporary residence can lead to permanent residency after **5 years**. Ordinary naturalization is often **10 years**; citizens of many Latin American countries, the Philippines, and Portugal may qualify after **2 years**—verify nationality-specific rules with counsel.

Taken together, the NLV's full shape is: 1-year initial authorization renewing on a +2/+2 pattern, income evidence starting at €28,800 for a solo applicant and scaling up per dependent, mandatory zero-deductible private health insurance for everyone on the file, and a hard ban on any form of work — remote or local. It rewards people who can genuinely live on passive income or savings without touching employment, and it punishes anyone who tries to quietly keep freelancing on the side once Spanish tax residency and the 183-day presence expectation are both back in force.

Applicants should also think carefully about how the income and savings evidence is actually documented, since this is where a surprising number of otherwise-qualified applicants stumble. Consulates generally want to see either sustained income (pension statements, investment income, rental income) or a lump sum in savings sufficient to cover several years at the required threshold, and the exact documentary standard — how many months of bank statements, whether a notarized translation is required, how recently the funds must have been in the account — varies meaningfully by consulate. Applicants who assume their home consulate follows the same checklist as a friend's experience at a different consulate are a common source of avoidable rejections, so always pull the specific document list from the consulate handling your application rather than relying on secondhand accounts.

Tax residency is the other detail that catches people off guard after approval. Spending more than 183 days a year in Spain on the NLV generally triggers Spanish tax residency, which means worldwide income — not just Spanish-source income — becomes reportable and potentially taxable in Spain, subject to any applicable tax treaty with your home country. Retirees living on a pension from a treaty country often find this manageable with proper planning, but it is not something to discover after the fact; a cross-border tax consultation before your first extended stay is worth the cost relative to an unwelcome surprise at tax-filing time.
