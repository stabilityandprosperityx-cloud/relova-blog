Tbilisi is small enough that a wrong neighbourhood is a lifestyle error, not a commute tragedy — but rent, hills, and who you meet at 11 a.m. still split the city. Central one-bedrooms in **Vera**, **Vake**, and **Sololaki** now run about **$700–1,200 a month**, roughly **double in dollar terms since 2022**. **Saburtalo** and further-out belts sit nearer **$400–600**. **Didube, Gldani, Isani** can still show **$250–400**. Those bands are the map. The rest is who lives where, and how you get across town.

## Districts at a glance

| District | Typical 1BR (2026) | Upside | Downside | Fits |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vera** | $700–1,200 | Walkable cafés and coworking; informal expat HQ; near the Old Town | Noise, tourist overflow, prices at the top of the central band | People who want to walk everywhere and meet other foreigners without trying |
| **Vake** | $700–1,200 | Prestige, greenery, embassies, international schools | **No metro**; you pay for calm | Families, embassy/NGO staff, remote workers who will Bolt to everything |
| **Saburtalo** | $400–600 | Newer stock, **metro**, clearly cheaper | Less “pretty Tbilisi,” more apartment-block | First landing; people who want a line on a map and a lower lease |
| **Sololaki** | $700–1,200 | Historic streets, atmosphere, local bakeries | Old fabric: stairs, heating quirks, thin walls | Anyone trying to live *in* the city rather than in an expat cul-de-sac |
| **Didube / Gldani / Isani** | $250–400 | Budget that still exists after 2022 | Longer trips, thinner English, fewer “accidental” cafés | Strict budgets, longer leases, people who already know the city |

Read the table as **ranges**, not listings. A renovated Vera walk-up can exceed $1,200; a tired Saburtalo block can undercut $400. Dollar quotes hide lari swings — confirm the currency in the contract.

## Who actually lives where

The expat map is four layers, not one Facebook group. **Pre-war long-stayers** (companies, missions, NGOs) cluster toward Vake and embassy-adjacent streets. The **2022 wave** of Russian, Belarusian, and Ukrainian arrivals filled Saburtalo and parts of Vera at speed and pushed the dollar rents you see now. **Digital nomads** on short stays concentrate in Vake and Saburtalo for Wi-Fi and new buildings. **Diaspora returnees** scatter, often preferring Sololaki or family districts over the café strip.

That mix is why “the expat area” is a weak search term. Vera on a Wednesday morning is not Vake school-run, and neither is Gldani on a Sunday.

## Getting around and how safe it feels

**Metro plus Bolt** covers most of the useful city. Vake’s missing metro is the tax you pay for parks and schools: budget ride-hail as a line item, not a treat. Metro-connected Saburtalo is the reason long-term residents tell newcomers to start there even if the Instagram grid is Vera courtyards.

Tbilisi is widely treated as one of the **safer European capitals** for street crime of the violent kind. Petty theft exists — phones on café tables, bags on metro hooks. Walk like a city person, not like a tourist with a laptop backpack unzipped. Night Bolt from a restaurant is normal, not dramatic.

## If you are landing this month

Long-stayers repeat two opposing scripts, both usable:

1. **Start in Saburtalo** for metro and the $400–600 band, then **move to Vake** when the budget grows and you know which school or office actually matters.
2. **Start in Vera or Sololaki** if your goal is to leave the English-menu bubble and accept older buildings in exchange for walking into the city.

Do not sign a 12-month central lease on day three. Book two to four weeks of flexible housing, walk Vera at 8 a.m. and 8 p.m., ride the metro to Saburtalo in rush hour, and only then wire a deposit. The 2022 doubling of central rents punished people who used $600-a-month blog posts as contracts.

If you have school-age children, reverse the usual nomad script: shortlist **Vake** for international schools first, then decide whether you can live with no metro. If you are a solo remote worker, invert it: metro access in Saburtalo is worth more than a pretty courtyard you will photograph once. Couples often split the difference with a winter lease in Saburtalo and weekend walking in Sololaki until they know which friction they hate.

Pick a district for **how you move and who you meet**, not for a skyline photo. The table is the shortlist; the first month of walking is the decision.
