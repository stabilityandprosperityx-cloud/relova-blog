**[Tbilisi](/blog/move-to-georgia-russian-ukrainian-2026) versus Batumi** is really a choice between capital-city infrastructure and coastal resort living, and the two cities suit noticeably different kinds of expat. Both benefit from the same national framework — Georgia's 365-day visa-free entry for most Western passports and the 1% turnover tax available to registered Individual Entrepreneurs apply identically whether you settle in the capital or on the Black Sea coast, so the immigration and tax mechanics aren't a differentiator here. The real decision comes down to pace of life, seasonality, and what kind of infrastructure you need day to day.

[Tbilisi](/blog/tbilisi-complete-guide-2026) is the obvious choice for anyone who wants a functioning, non-seasonal capital city: government offices, the bulk of Georgia's private hospitals, international schools, and the country's most developed remote-work infrastructure are concentrated there. The city's coworking scene alone tells the story — [Tbilisi](/blog/tbilisi-digital-nomad-setup-guide-2026) has more than 60 coworking venues in active operation, a number Batumi's smaller, distinctly seasonal scene doesn't come close to matching. That density means better odds of finding a space that fits your specific workflow, more networking opportunities with other remote workers and founders, and infrastructure that doesn't wind down once summer tourist season ends.

Batumi, by contrast, is fundamentally a resort city, and its rental market reflects that directly: one-bedroom apartments swing from roughly USD 250–500 in the winter off-season up to USD 400–800 during the summer high season, when tourists and seasonal residents flood in and push demand (and prices) up sharply. That seasonality cuts both ways — someone who can time a lease around the off-season gets genuinely excellent value and a much quieter city, while anyone who needs to be there in peak summer pays a real premium and deals with the crowds and traffic that come with Georgia's biggest beach destination. Tbilisi's rental market is comparatively stable year-round, with one-bedrooms typically running USD 350–700 regardless of season, which makes budgeting simpler even if the ceiling is similar to Batumi's summer peak.

The practical read: digital nomads and remote workers who need consistent infrastructure, a large peer community, and access to healthcare and international schools generally gravitate to Tbilisi, while people drawn to a slower, beach-adjacent lifestyle — and who can either tolerate the seasonal rent swing or time their stay around it — tend to find Batumi's coastal setting worth the trade-off. Neither city requires a different visa strategy, so this is genuinely a lifestyle decision rather than a legal one.

Climate is worth factoring in too, since it shapes the two cities very differently. Batumi sits on the humid, subtropical Black Sea coast and gets noticeably more rainfall than the capital, with a genuine summer-holiday atmosphere that swells the city's population and energy from roughly June through September before it quiets down dramatically in the colder months. Tbilisi's inland climate is drier and more continental, with a defined but manageable winter and hot, dry summers that don't carry the same beach-town seasonal swing. For someone who thrives on a lively, resort-town buzz for part of the year and doesn't mind a quieter off-season, Batumi's rhythm can be appealing; for someone who wants steady year-round energy without the boom-and-bust cycle, Tbilisi is the more predictable choice.

Healthcare access also tilts toward Tbilisi in practical terms — the capital holds the bulk of Georgia's private hospital network and the widest selection of English-speaking specialists, while Batumi's private healthcare options, though improving, remain comparatively limited, which matters more for longer-term residents or families than for someone passing through for a short seasonal stay. Transport links favor Tbilisi as well: its international airport carries far more direct connections than Batumi's smaller airport, which leans heavily on seasonal charter and regional routes rather than a dense year-round network. Weigh all of this against your own tolerance for seasonality and your need for backup infrastructure before picking a base, since both cities deliver a genuinely good expat experience — they just deliver it on very different terms.
