Teaching English abroad in 2026 is a labour market with regional rules, not a backpack and a smile. The global TEFL-education industry is estimated around **$95 billion** this year — a career track with international-school, online, and admin exits, not a gap-year side quest. What you need on paper still depends on the map: a bachelor’s degree is a visa requirement in much of Asia and a nice-to-have in much of Latin America.

## Whether you need a degree depends on the region

Most of **South Korea, Japan, China, and Vietnam** want a **bachelor’s in any field** plus a **TEFL certificate of at least 120 hours** before they will stamp a work visa. That is immigration, not a school’s taste. Without the degree, the legal job usually does not exist.

**Latin America** and parts of Europe and Southeast Asia are looser. **Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Cambodia, and Costa Rica** still hire on a TEFL certificate without a degree for many language-school roles. “Can hire” is not “will sponsor a visa.” Confirm the work-permit path before you buy a one-way ticket on a 120-hour PDF.

Industry hiring has moved past the bare 120-hour webinar. A recent survey of employers who **sponsor visas** found **73% want a 150-hour-plus accredited course with observed teaching practice**. If your plan is EPIK or a Japanese dispatch company, spend the extra hours. If your plan is a cash-in-hand conversation club in Medellín, you can be cheaper — and you will also be more replaceable.

## Government programmes versus private schools versus online

**EPIK** (South Korea, public schools), **JET** (Japan), and **CIEE** (Spain) are the structured doors: orientation, a cohort, and someone who has done the visa before. They are slow and competitive. Age caps are real: EPIK is often around **55**; Japan and China commonly talk **60**, with exceptions for strong specialists. If you are 58, do not apply to EPIK as your only plan.

Private academies and international schools hire on a shorter fuse and a wider quality spread. Some will fly you in and house you; some will leave you in a studio above a fried-chicken shop. Read the contract for housing, flights, and who pays the work-permit medical.

Online platforms (**VIPKid, iTutorGroup, Cambly** and peers) pay about **$10–25 an hour**. They are a supplement, a first-year bridge, or a way to teach from a cheaper country. They are not a Korean public-school salary and they rarely include a visa. Treat them as income, not as immigration.

## Pay bands — and what you can actually save

**South Korea and Japan**: about **$1,700–2,650 a month**, often with housing and a partial or full flight. Savings exist if you do not recreate Seoul nightlife every weekend and if the apartment is actually free, not “subsidised” at a fake market rent.

**UAE and other Gulf states**: the top of the global table — **$3,500–5,500 net a month**, no income tax in the usual packages, plus housing and flights. The hours, the curriculum, and the exit rules are the fine print. Net is the headline; read the probation and the ticket home.

**Europe**: often **$700–1,800 a month** equivalent (in sterling, **£700–1,800**). You teach for the visa and the city, not for a down payment. **Latin America** usually pays less in dollars and costs less in rent; the savings rate can still work if you live like a local teacher, not like a remote worker on a US wage.

A $95 billion industry has a senior track: coordinator, academic manager, international-school classroom with a teaching licence. The 120-hour certificate is the on-ramp, not the career.

## How long preparation actually takes

Government programmes: **three to six months** from application to wheels up. File in the winter for a late-summer start. Private schools: **four to eight weeks** when they are desperate in August or January. Volunteer posts can close in **two to four weeks** — which is also how fast a bad placement can strand you.

Start the degree scan, apostille, criminal-record check, and TEFL with observed practice **before** you browse apartments in Busan. The 73% of visa-sponsoring employers who want 150 hours will not wait while you upgrade a weekend course.

Teaching abroad in 2026 is **region-specific paperwork, then a pay band, then a calendar**. Asia wants the degree; the Gulf pays the net; Europe pays in lifestyle; online pays in hours. Pick the map first. The certificate is the second purchase.
