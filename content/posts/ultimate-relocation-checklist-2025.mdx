A clean relocation is a calendar problem. Start **6–12 months** out — especially if your destination has competitive international school waitlists (places often book **6–12 months** ahead) or a visa that takes months to adjudicate. Use this timeline as the spine; fill each phase with the country-specific forms later.

## 6–12 months before departure

Lock the **priority order** that 2026 relocation guides keep repeating: **visa/residence → housing → finance/banking → healthcare/insurance → schools (if kids) → shipping logistics**. Do not reverse it because a beautiful apartment listing arrived first.

Order apostilles and sworn translations early for birth certificates, police certificates, marriage/divorce papers, and medical/vaccination records. Legalisation routinely eats weeks. Open a fintech account (**Wise/Revolut**) while you still have home-country KYC — you will want a working SEPA or multi-currency account on day one.

Sketch a cash budget of **$8,000–15,000** per person for the move, **2–3 months** of living costs, and a rent deposit. Full household containers push the number higher. Add a **+20%** buffer for FX and surprises.

## 3 months before departure

Submit or chase the residence file. Book temporary housing for arrival if permanent rent must wait for an in-person search. Compare health policies that match visa wording. If you have children, chase school offers in writing — some countries want enrolment proof **before** issuing dependent visas; others reverse that order, so verify rather than assume.

Renew or cancel home-country insurance on a schedule. Start mail forwarding. List subscriptions and memberships to freeze. Check whether your destination accepts an international driving permit or forces a local licence exchange within a fixed window after arrival.

## 1 month before departure

Align flight dates with the visa validity window. Re-check Schengen **90/180** day counts if you will transit or visit other Schengen states — a national D-visa elsewhere does not always give you unlimited free roaming, and double-counting days is a classic trap.

Confirm apostilled packets are in hand. Photograph every document. Pack a “landing folder”: passport copies, insurance certificate, lease or booking, bank IBAN letter, school letters, and medication scripts. Tell your bank and mobile provider the travel dates so fraud systems do not freeze cards on landing day.

## First week on the ground

Register address where required. Open or upgrade the local bank once you have the ID numbers your destination demands. Activate local SIMs. Visit the clinic listed on your policy so you know where to go before an emergency. Do not ship the container until temporary housing and address registration are stable enough to receive it.

## First month on the ground

Convert temporary housing into a longer lease if that was the plan. Finalise school start dates and paediatric vaccination alignment with the local calendar. Close leftover home-country obligations you deferred. Revisit the budget against real rent and groceries; the **+20%** buffer exists for this moment.

Treat the checklist as living software, not a PDF you print once. The movers who finish calmly are the ones who respected the 6–12 month runway and the visa-before-sofa sequence — not the ones who packed first and paperwork last.


Between phases, keep a single shared tracker (spreadsheet or project board) with owners and due dates. Couples and families fail checklists when tasks live only in one person’s head. Assign visa, housing, school, and shipping as separate workstreams with weekly syncs.

Also build a “kill list” of home-country obligations with cancellation dates: gym, insurance auto-renewals, local tax portals, and government digital IDs that expire if unused. Closing cleanly prevents surprise charges six months after you leave. If you will keep a home property rented, schedule property management and tax advice in the 3-month window — that is relocation-adjacent work that still burns calendar time.

Finally, rehearse the landing week once on paper: airport to temporary address, SIM, grocery, clinic location, and the first registration appointment. A written landing script reduces decision fatigue when jet lag peaks.


If your destination uses digital residence portals, create accounts early and store login credentials in a password manager shared with your co-mover. Portal outages and two-factor codes tied to old phone numbers are a frequent last-month failure mode. Test that your authenticator app works offline before you board.
